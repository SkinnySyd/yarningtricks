Meet **Cinnamon**, a delightfully round red panda wearing a lilac-pink scarf and blue-and-cream striped trousers. There's something irresistible about the contrast of bright orange fur, pale ears and those little cherry-red toes.

This cheerful woodland project combines **one-piece head and body shaping**, soft cream facial colorwork, separate little arms and feet, plus separate striped shorts and a wonderfully textured scarf. It's best for a crocheter who already knows how to increase and decrease in spirals and is ready to practice tidy color changes.

**About the source and pictures:** *Animal Friends of Pica Pau 3* has a red panda project on pages 70–75, but Cinnamon is a **new Thread & Petal construction designed for the supplied scarf-wearing red panda image**. The round sequences, proportions and accessory instructions below were drafted separately, not copied from the book. The pictures are visual references rather than photographs of a sample made from these exact rounds. **This version has not yet been physically test-crocheted**, so check fit, placement and proportions as you work.

## Before the first stitch

- **Difficulty:** Intermediate; the cream markings and stripe placement ask for steady counting.
- **Crochet terminology:** US. **MR** = magic ring; **sc** = single crochet; **inc** = 2 sc in one stitch; **dec** = decrease over two stitches; **sl st** = slip stitch; **BLO** = back loop only; **ch** = chain; **FO** = fasten off.
- **Construction:** Head and orange torso in one continuous piece; two ears, an oval muzzle, two cheeks, two arms, two feet, separate striped shorts and a long ribbed scarf. An optional short tail goes on the back.
- **Counting:** Keep a removable marker in the first stitch of each new round. The total in **[brackets]** shows the stitch count at the end.
- **Size:** Finished measurements will depend on yarn and tension; this drafted version has not been made and measured.

Have the [magic-ring lesson](/learn/magic-ring-crochet-in-the-round/) handy, along with our [color-change tutorial](/learn/changing-yarn-colors-crochet/). The scarf is easier when the hook has a little more room than the body stitches.

2. **Rnd 2:** Inc in every stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** *(sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** *(3 sc, inc)* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** *(4 sc, inc)* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** *(5 sc, inc)* six times. **[42]**
8. **Rnd 8:** *(6 sc, inc)* six times. **[48]**
9. **Rnd 9:** *(7 sc, inc)* six times. **[54]**

The next color sections create two bright cream shapes on the cheeks and a narrow orange area between them. Keep the beginning-of-round marker at the **back** so the face sits opposite it.

## Part 2 — Make the two cream face markings

Complete every section of a round in the color written before it. Begin a new color with the **last yarn-over of the preceding stitch**, not one stitch too late.

12. **Rnd 13:** Orange **18 sc**, cream **8 sc**, orange **8 sc**, cream **8 sc**, orange **18 sc**. **[60]**
13. **Rnd 14:** Orange **16 sc**, cream **10 sc**, orange **8 sc**, cream **10 sc**, orange **16 sc**. **[60]**
14. **Rnd 15:** Orange **15 sc**, cream **12 sc**, orange **6 sc**, cream **12 sc**, orange **15 sc**. **[60]**
15. **Rnd 16–19:** Orange **14 sc**, cream **13 sc**, orange **6 sc**, cream **13 sc**, orange **14 sc** each round. **[60 per round]**
16. **Rnd 20:** Orange **16 sc**, cream **10 sc**, orange **8 sc**, cream **10 sc**, orange **16 sc**. **[60]**
17. **Rnd 21:** Work all 60 stitches in orange. **[60]**

**Look before you decrease:** The face should read as two cream panels above the round cream snout, with a narrow orange strip between them. Mark the centerline with a spare thread. Pin the muzzle and check eye positions now. You may install toy eyes between rounds 15 and 16 on the orange or cream boundary, or embroider dark eyes later.

18. **Rnd 22:** *(8 sc, dec)* six times. **[54]**
19. **Rnd 23:** *(7 sc, dec)* six times. **[48]**
20. **Rnd 24:** *(6 sc, dec)* six times. **[42]**
21. **Rnd 25:** *(5 sc, dec)* six times. **[36]**
22. **Rnd 26:** *(4 sc, dec)* six times. **[30]**
23. **Rnd 27:** *(3 sc, dec)* six times. **[24]**

Stuff the head carefully while the opening is accessible, spreading the fiberfill toward both cheeks. The [stuffing and shaping lesson](/blog/the-little-art-of-stuffing-amigurumi/) is useful if the face starts to bulge unevenly.

34. **Rnd 50:** *(7 sc, dec)* six times. **[48]**

Stuff the torso and shape a soft, seated belly. The brown-orange body should be slightly narrower around the chest than at the hip; the separate striped shorts will provide the blue-and-white color outside.

**Shape check:** You should have **48 stitches around Rnd 38** and **60 at the widest hip section**. We'll allow extra ease in the shorts. If your body becomes more than a little wider than those planned circumferences, measure the actual piece before starting the garment.

## Part 4 — Soft cream ears with tiny red details

The supplied close-up shows **rounded pale ears**, with red embroidered shapes inside. Crochet two matching cream ear pieces and add the red details afterward.

1. **Rnd 1:** In cream, 6 sc in MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Inc around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** *(sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4–5:** Sc 18 around. **[18 per round]**
5. **Rnd 6:** *(sc, dec)* six times. **[12]**
6. **Rnd 7:** Sc 12 around. **[12]**

Fasten off leaving a long sewing tail. Flatten the pieces **without stuffing** and pinch slightly at the base.

With a tapestry needle and bright red yarn, embroider **three short rays or a small V** on the front of each ear. Keep the stitches loose enough not to bunch the cream fabric.

Pin the ears high on the head, one on each side, and sew them down with small even stitches. Look at the face from straight ahead before tying the tails.

## Part 5 — A cream smiling muzzle and rosy cheeks

### Make an oval cream muzzle

The earlier version used a round circle and asked you to flatten it. This updated muzzle is **actually worked as an oval around a foundation chain**, giving a wider, friendlier smile area.

1. **Foundation:** With cream, **ch 6**.
2. **Rnd 1:** Starting in the second chain from the hook, sc in each of the next **4 chains**, place **3 sc in the final chain**, turn to work along the underside of the foundation, sc in the next **3 underside loops**, then put **2 sc into the last remaining underside loop**. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 2:** Inc in each of the next 2 stitches, sc in the next 3, inc in each of the next 3, sc in the next 3, and inc in the last stitch. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 3–4:** Work 18 sc around in each round, without increases. **[18 per round]**

Leave the base open, fasten off with a long tail, and shape it into a **horizontal oval**. Embroider a little charcoal nose and a relaxed W-shaped smile *before* sewing it to the face. You can tuck a small amount of filling behind the center as the seam closes.

**Oval count check:** For Rnd 1, **4 + 3 + 3 + 2 = 12** stitches. Rnd 2 contains **six increases**, so the count becomes **18**. If one end looks angular, massage the piece gently rather than adding an undocumented stitch.

### Two pink cheeks

In blush pink, make **two** circles: 6 sc into an MR **[6]**, then inc all around **[12]**. Slip stitch to finish, fasten off with a tail, and sew one circle to each side of the lower face.

**Expression tip:** Pin the muzzle and cheeks first. A few millimeters of movement can change a serious panda into a particularly cheerful one. Our [embroidered-face lesson](/learn/embroider-amigurumi-faces/) can help with the little mouth.

## Part 6 — Orange arms with cherry-red paws

Make **two**, beginning in deep red to create softly rounded mittens.

1. **Rnd 1:** In red, 6 sc in MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Inc around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** *(3 sc, inc)* three times. **[15]**
4. **Rnd 4–6:** Sc 15 around. **[15 per round]**
5. **Rnd 7:** Change to orange. Sc around. **[15]**
6. **Rnd 8–12:** Sc 15 around. **[15 per round]**
7. **Rnd 13:** *(3 sc, dec)* three times. **[12]**
8. **Rnd 14–15:** Sc 12 around. **[12 per round]**

Stuff the red paw tips lightly but keep the tops of the arms soft. Flatten each opening, fasten off, and leave long sewing tails. Pin the arms around the shoulder line just below the head.

1. **Rnd 1:** In red, 6 sc in MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Inc around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** *(sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Sc 24 around. **[24]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Change to cream. Sc 24 around. **[24]**
7. **Rnd 7:** In sky blue, sc 24 around. **[24]**
8. **Rnd 8:** In cream, sc 24 around. **[24]**
9. **Rnd 9:** In blue, *(6 sc, dec)* three times. **[21]**
10. **Rnd 10:** In cream, sc 21 around. **[21]**
11. **Rnd 11:** In blue, *(5 sc, dec)* three times. **[18]**
12. **Rnd 12:** In cream, sc 18 around. **[18]**

Stuff the rounded red toes, leaving the upper opening softer. Flatten and leave a long tail. Pin each leg to the bottom front of the round body, angling the toes outward for the seated pose in the image.

## Part 8 — Separate blue-and-cream striped shorts

These shorts are a **new Thread & Petal construction**, designed to fit over Cinnamon's round orange lower body and give the image's trouser stripes **two actual leg openings**. Unlike the earlier built-in stripes, this is a separate, dressable garment.

**Important fit change:** The panda's widest hips are 60 stitches. A ready-made 52-stitch waistband would have to stretch over them before reaching the narrower waist. To avoid that problem, begin with a roomy **64-stitch tube**, dress the panda, and **shape the pink waistband after the shorts are on**.

### Work the roomy trouser tube

1. **Foundation:** In cream, **ch 64**. Lay the chain flat, check for twists, then join with a slip stitch.
2. **Rnd 1:** Work one sc in each chain. **[64]**
3. **Rnd 2–10:** Work 64 sc per round, changing color in this order: **sky blue, cream, sky blue, cream, sky blue, cream, sky blue, cream, sky blue**. **[64 per round]**

**Check the fit before joining the crotch:** Slide the open tube over the panda's lower orange body with the cream foundation edge at the top. The 64-stitch opening should pass over the 60-stitch hips without strain. Adjust your starting chain if your yarn or stuffing makes the fit noticeably tighter than expected.

### Make two actual leg openings

1. Flatten the **lower open edge** front-to-back and align the middle front with the middle back.
2. Mark **four adjoining front stitches** at the center and **four corresponding back stitches**. Those eight stitches form the bridge between the legs.
3. Recount the unmarked stitches: there should be **28 on the left** and **28 on the right**. The total is **4 + 4 + 28 + 28 = 64**.
4. With a needle and matching yarn, **sew the four front stitches to the corresponding four back stitches**, creating a short crotch seam. Do not crochet into the eight joined stitches again.

### Finish both cuffs

- **Left cuff, Rnds 11–13:** Join cream at the inner edge of the left opening. Crochet **28 sc around** for Rnd 11, change to sky blue for Rnd 12 and cream for Rnd 13. **[28 per round]**
- **Right cuff, Rnds 11–13:** Repeat on the other opening. **[28 per round]**
- Optional: work one loose final round of **28 sky-blue slip stitches** around each cuff, then weave in the yarn.

**Count checkpoint:** If a cuff has 27 or 29 stitches, double-check that the four front and four back crotch stitches were sewn together in matching pairs. There should be no stitches worked into the closed bridge.

### Gather and edge the waist *after dressing*

Slip the shorts over the body **before attaching the separate legs**. With the garment sitting around the lower orange torso, join dusty-mauve yarn to an unused loop of the **64-stitch starting-chain edge** at the back.

1. **Waist edging, Rnd 1:** Repeat *(3 sc, dec)* **12 times** and then work **4 sc**. The 64 loops give you **52 stitches** after the twelve decreases. **[52]**
2. **Waist edging, Rnd 2:** Sc around **52** for a neat, slightly snug waistband. **[52]**

This little taper stays in place once the body is dressed; it is **not meant to be forced back over the widest hip**. If you want the shorts removable, omit the decreases and secure the waist gently with a detachable tie instead.

**Styling note:** The orange body underneath stays solid orange. The blue-and-cream stripes live on these separate shorts and on the leg pieces, creating the layered appearance shown in your picture.

## Part 9 — A soft pink scarf with lengthwise ribs

The photo shows a broad scarf whose raised lines run **along its length**. To get that effect, this revised version begins with a **long foundation chain** and works short rows across it, rather than starting with a narrow eight-stitch row and repeating it dozens of times.

Use dusty-pink or lilac yarn and a hook **about 0.5 mm larger** than your amigurumi hook if needed.

**Half-double slip stitch (hdslst):** Yarn over, insert your hook, pull up a loop, then pull that new loop **through the other two loops** on your hook in one smooth motion. In the rows below, work into the **back loop only (BLO)**, so the fabric develops flexible, lengthwise ridges.

1. **Foundation:** Chain **84**. This is a suggested length; before working the second row, check that it reaches around your finished red panda's neck and leaves a comfortable front tail.
2. **Row 1:** Starting in the third chain from the hook, work **82 hdslst**. Chain 1 and turn. **[82]**
3. **Rows 2–10:** Work **82 hdslst in the back loops only**, chain 1 and turn after each row except the last. **[82 per row]**

Fasten off and weave in the ends. Wrap the scarf loosely around Cinnamon's neck and let one end fall forward. If it is too short, add more stitches to the starting chain next time; if too narrow, add more **rows**, not foundation chains.

**For a different texture:** If you find half-double slip stitch too fiddly, use *single crochet in back loops only* for a similar ribbed effect, though the drape will be slightly different.

**Safety note:** A hanging scarf is not suitable for a young child's toy. Omit it or replace it with a short, securely attached decorative collar, and embroider eyes rather than relying on small plastic components.

## Optional — A small striped tail

The supplied front-view image doesn't show a tail, so this is a **completely optional extra**. For a little woodland flourish, start with orange:

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Inc around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** *(sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4–7:** Sc 18 around, using orange for Rnds 4–5 and cream for Rnds 6–7. **[18]**
5. **Rnd 8–11:** Sc 18 around, alternating orange for Rnds 8–9 and cream for Rnds 10–11. **[18]**
6. **Rnd 12:** *(sc, dec)* six times. **[12]**

Stuff very lightly and flatten the opening. To avoid interfering with the trouser waistband, sew the tail to the **back of the orange torso above the shorts**, not onto the blue garment. Keep it small enough that Cinnamon can sit.

## Part 10 — Join the little woodland friend

1. Secure the **oval muzzle** at the center of the face, then pin and sew the ears and two pink cheeks. Finish the embroidered or installed eyes before closing any remaining head access.
2. Attach the two **orange arms** at matching shoulder heights.
3. **Slip on the finished striped shorts before sewing on the legs.** Align the sewn crotch with the body's center and work the 52-stitch mauve waist edging after the shorts are pulled over the lower orange torso.
4. Pass each **red-toed leg through its own 28-stitch cuff**, then pin its upper end to the lower body. Sew each leg to the **orange body fabric**, not merely to the shorts, so its weight is supported.
5. Pull the shorts into their final position, stitch the waistband discreetly to the torso, and tack the cuffs in place if you don't want them to slide.
6. If you made the optional tail, attach it high enough at the **back of the orange body above the waistband** that it doesn't obstruct the separate shorts. The supplied front-view image doesn't show a tail, so leaving it out is fine.
7. Tie the **pink scarf** loosely, arranging the lengthwise ridges to face outward. Hide all sewing tails and gently redistribute the stuffing until the panda sits steadily.

A neat fit is more important than a perfectly round number on the garment. If the hip tube is tight, add circumference before sewing the crotch; don't try to pull a reduced waistband over the hips. For help making strong seams, see [sewing amigurumi pieces together](/learn/sew-amigurumi-pieces-together/).

## Tiny fixes that really help

| If you notice… | Try… |
| --- | --- |
| The face markings seem lopsided | Count the cream segments in each of the 60-stitch rounds; keep the marker at the back |
| The ear embroidery pulls in | Rework the red rays with a little slack between stitches |
| The scarf is stiff | Use a slightly larger hook and keep the half-double slip stitches loose; the ribs should drape, not stand up |
| The feet look mismatched | Compare the colored toe rounds before stuffing and sewing |
| The panda falls backward | Pin both legs farther forward and check stuffing before you sew through the orange body |
| The trouser stripes seem to spiral | Keep the color join at the back of the separate garment; don't add stitches to straighten it |

## A gentle Thread & Petal goodbye

Cinnamon is a reminder that handmade things can be both bright and peaceful. Choose the pink shade you love best, make each ear with care, and enjoy watching a few simple stitches turn into a friend with a very cozy scarf.
