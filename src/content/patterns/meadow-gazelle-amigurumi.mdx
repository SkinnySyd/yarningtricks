There's something especially sweet about a crochet animal with a little wardrobe of its own. Meet **Meadow Gazelle**: a gentle, long-legged friend with expressive ears, two tiny horns, a softly colored face, and a removable pink skirt that gives her a touch of personality.

This project is a lovely one for a crocheter who has already tried a few amigurumi animals. You'll begin with a separate, two-color muzzle, then grow the **head, narrow neck, sweater-like torso, and legs in one continuous piece**. After that come the small details: bobble-stitch hands, layered ears, upright horns, a tiny tail and the skirt.

The instructions below explain each stage in everyday language while keeping the original construction and stitch totals. Don't hurry the color changes or the leg division; a few extra moments of counting are worth it when the pieces begin to take shape.

## Before you begin

- **Finished height:** approximately **37 cm (14.5 inches)**, measured with the horns, using the suggested yarn and hook. Your tension may change the result.
- **Level:** intermediate. Experience with working in a spiral, increases, decreases and basic sewing will help.
- **Hook:** **2.75 mm (US C/2)**.
- **Construction:** a separate snout; joined head and body with worked-in legs; two arms; two layered ears; two horns; a small tail; and a separate skirt.
- **Terminology:** all stitch names are **US crochet terms**. The number in **[brackets]** is the total stitch count after that round.

Use a removable stitch marker to show the **first stitch of each round**. Most sections are worked in continuous spirals, without joining the final stitch to the first. When changing colors, complete the final pull-through of the preceding stitch using the new yarn so the transition is as tidy as possible.

Need a refresher? Our [magic ring lesson](/learn/magic-ring-crochet-in-the-round/) and [crochet shaping guide](/learn/crochet-increases-and-decreases/) are good places to practice before starting.

## Stitch abbreviations (US terms)

| Abbreviation | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **MR** | Magic ring |
| **sc** | Single crochet |
| **inc** | Make 2 sc in the same stitch |
| **dec** | Single crochet two stitches together |
| **ch** | Chain |
| **sl st** | Slip stitch |
| **BLO** | Work into back loop only |
| **5-dc bobble** | Five partly worked double crochets closed together in one stitch |
| **st / sts** | Stitch / stitches |
| **Rnd / Rnds** | Round / rounds |

For a toy intended for a child under three, securely embroidered eyes are safer than plastic eyes with removable parts. Secure all seams and yarn ends carefully.

## Part 1 — Crochet the two-tone muzzle

We'll make the muzzle first. It's a small rounded cup whose **off-white sides frame an ochre center**. Work the starting rounds in off-white yarn, then change colors within individual rounds. Keep the strands resting loosely inside so the fabric doesn't tighten between colors.

### Shape the starting circle

1. **Rnd 1:** Work 6 single crochets into an adjustable magic ring, then pull the ring closed. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in each of the six stitches. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 sc, then an increase* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Crochet one sc in each stitch around. **[18]**

### Build the color blocks

5. **Rnd 5:** Crochet **6 sc in off-white**, change to **ochre for 6 sc**, then change back to **off-white for 6 sc**. **[18]**
6. **Rnd 6:** In off-white, repeat *2 sc, inc* twice. Work the same sequence twice in ochre, then twice more in off-white. Each color block now holds eight stitches. **[24]**
7. **Rnds 7–8:** For **each** of these two rounds, work **8 off-white sc, 8 ochre sc, 8 off-white sc**. **[24]**
8. **Rnd 9:** In off-white, repeat *3 sc, inc* twice; make the same two repeats in ochre, then twice again in off-white. **[30]**
9. **Rnds 10–11:** Crochet **10 sc in off-white, 10 sc in ochre and 10 sc in off-white** for each round. **[30]**

Cut the yarn and leave a long sewing tail. With **black yarn**, embroider a little nose and mouth onto the center of the muzzle while the back is still easy to reach. Add fiberfill until it looks gently rounded, but not so much that the color sections are stretched apart.

**Little checkpoint:** When you set the muzzle down, you should see three balanced areas—cream, ochre, cream. If one side looks wider, count the stitches in the preceding color-change round before sewing the muzzle to the head.

## Part 2 — Grow the head, neck and body as one piece

Start in **ochre yarn** at the top of the head. We'll widen the crown into a rounded face, work the pale facial markings, then narrow the opening into a neck. The upper torso changes to **slate gray**, giving the gazelle the look of a fitted little sweater without crocheting a separate garment.

### Rounds 1–14: make the crown

1. **Rnd 1:** Make a magic ring and crochet 6 sc into its center. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Work an increase in every stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 sc, inc* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *2 sc, inc* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *3 sc, inc* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *4 sc, inc* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *5 sc, inc* six times. **[42]**
8. **Rnd 8:** Repeat *6 sc, inc* six times. **[48]**
9. **Rnd 9:** Repeat *7 sc, inc* six times. **[54]**
10. **Rnd 10:** Repeat *8 sc, inc* six times. **[60]**
11. **Rnds 11–14:** Crochet one sc into each of the 60 stitches for **four complete rounds**. **[60]**

Your fabric will have become a wide, softly curved cup. The next rounds place off-white shapes around the eyes. Keep a marker at the beginning of the round and consider adding a second marker whenever you change color.

### Rounds 15–22: give the face its two pale markings

12. **Rnd 15:** Work **16 ochre sc, 4 off-white sc, 20 ochre sc, 4 off-white sc and 16 ochre sc**. **[60]**
13. **Rnd 16:** Crochet **14 ochre sc, 8 off-white sc, 16 ochre sc, 8 off-white sc and 14 ochre sc**. **[60]**
14. **Rnds 17–19:** Repeat this exact color arrangement for **each** of three rounds: **12 ochre sc, 12 off-white sc, 12 ochre sc, 12 off-white sc, 12 ochre sc**. **[60]**
15. **Rnd 20:** Work **13 ochre sc, 10 off-white sc, 14 ochre sc, 10 off-white sc and 13 ochre sc**. **[60]**
16. **Rnd 21:** Crochet **14 ochre sc, 8 off-white sc, 16 ochre sc, 8 off-white sc and 14 ochre sc**. **[60]**
17. **Rnd 22:** Return to **ochre only** and work 60 sc around. **[60]**

The two white markings should now look similar in size and sit symmetrically when the face is viewed straight on. Small differences can come from tension, so count carefully but avoid pulling the carried strands too tight.

### Rounds 23–28: narrow the face into the neck

18. **Rnd 23:** Repeat *3 sc, dec* twelve times. **[48]**
19. **Rnd 24:** Repeat *2 sc, dec* twelve times. **[36]**
20. **Rnd 25:** Repeat *4 sc, dec* six times. **[30]**

**Pause before closing the head.** Center the stuffed muzzle on the side opposite the beginning-of-round line and sew it between **head rounds 15 and 24**. Place the **10 mm safety eyes between rounds 18 and 19**, about **three stitches away from the muzzle** on either side. Make sure the expression looks balanced from the front, then secure the eyes. Add stuffing to the head as it narrows.

21. **Rnd 26:** Repeat *3 sc, dec* six times. **[24]**
22. **Rnd 27:** Repeat *2 sc, dec* six times. **[18]**
23. **Rnd 28:** Crochet one sc in each stitch to finish the narrow neck. **[18]**

**Stuff the head firmly** before continuing. The neck will later support a fairly tall head, so pay particular attention to the filling at the base.

### Rounds 29–40: make the slate-gray bodice

Change to **slate-gray yarn**. Continue with the same working yarn as you widen the upper torso.

24. **Rnd 29:** Repeat *2 sc, inc* six times. **[24]**
25. **Rnd 30:** Repeat *3 sc, inc* six times. **[30]**
26. **Rnds 31–33:** Crochet one sc into each stitch for three rounds. **[30]**
27. **Rnd 34:** Repeat *4 sc, inc* six times. **[36]**
28. **Rnds 35–40:** Work 36 sc in every round for six rounds. **[36]**

You can already see the fitted body taking shape beneath the head. As you work, continue adding fiberfill so the neck and torso don't collapse inward.

### Rounds 41–46: finish the ochre lower body

Switch to **ochre yarn** again. A back-loop-only round creates a small, neat line at the lower edge of the gray bodice.

29. **Rnd 41:** Crochet 36 sc, working into the **back loops only** of the previous round. **[36]**
30. **Rnds 42–46:** Work 36 sc in each of these five rounds. **[36]**

**Important count:** The open lower body has **36 stitches**. Don't fasten off or close it; those stitches are divided into the two long legs below.

## Part 3 — Make the two long gazelle legs

Unlike a toy with separate legs sewn on, this gazelle's legs grow straight from the bottom of its body. Before working the first leg, place removable markers to divide those **36 stitches** into **15 for each leg**, plus **three stitches at the front and three at the back** to form the small opening between them.

Hold the body facing you and look at the face. If the leg openings appear crooked, the source recommends working or undoing a few sc before starting the first leg so the split lines up with the head.

### First leg

Bring the back edge of the marked leg opening toward its front edge. Crochet one joining sc to make the opening into a round; count this as the first stitch of the next round.

1. **Rnds 47–72:** Work **15 sc in every round** for twenty-six rounds. **[15]**
2. **Rnd 73:** Repeat *1 sc, dec* five times. **[10]**
3. **Rnd 74:** Work five decreases around. **[5]**

Stuff the body and leg firmly while you can still reach the lower tube. Fasten off, leaving a long tail. With a yarn needle, pass the tail through the **front loops of the remaining five stitches**, pull the opening closed and hide the end.

### Second leg

Locate the **fourth unworked stitch at the back of body Round 46** and rejoin the ochre yarn there. That is the beginning of the second leg.

1. **Rnd 47:** Crochet around the second marked **15-stitch** leg opening. When you get back to its beginning, work into the first stitch to connect the circle and continue in a spiral. **[15]**
2. **Rnds 48–72:** Work 15 sc in every round, matching the length of the first leg. **[15]**
3. **Rnd 73:** Repeat *1 sc, dec* five times. **[10]**
4. **Rnd 74:** Make five decreases. **[5]**

Stuff the second leg to match the first, close the final five-stitch opening, then sew the **three-stitch space between the legs** securely closed.

**Fit tip:** Before hiding the last yarn end, stand the gazelle on a flat surface. The slim legs don't need to be rigid, but they should be the same length and hang evenly from the body.

## Part 4 — Crochet two arms with tiny bobble hands

The arms are crocheted separately in **ochre yarn**. Each is a slim tube with one playful **five-double-crochet bobble** near the starting end. A bobble is counted as one stitch, not five.

Make **two identical arms**:

1. **Rnd 1:** Place 6 sc in a magic ring. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in each stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnds 3–4:** Crochet one sc in each stitch for two rounds. **[12]**
4. **Rnd 5:** Make **1 sc**, then one **5-dc bobble** in the next stitch, followed by **10 sc**. **[12]**
5. **Rnds 6–20:** Work 12 sc in each of the next fifteen rounds. **[12]**
6. **Rnd 21:** Repeat *1 sc, dec* four times. **[8]**

Fasten off and leave a long tail for sewing. Stuff each arm softly, allowing the upper part to bend against the body. Pin them on opposite sides of the gray bodice **between body rounds 30 and 31** and sew securely when their placement looks balanced.

**Bobble help:** Yarn over, insert the hook into the stitch, pull up a loop and partially work a double crochet, leaving its final loop on the hook. Repeat until five unfinished double crochets are stacked in that stitch, then yarn over and draw through all the remaining loops together.

## Part 5 — Make the tall, two-layer ears

Those lovely ears are built from two layers: a long **ochre outer shape** and a flat **off-white inner oval**. You'll crochet two of each piece, then stitch the lighter layer onto the darker one. The ears are deliberately **unstuffed** so they can be gently flattened.

### Outer ears — make two in ochre

1. **Rnd 1:** Make 5 sc inside a magic ring. **[5]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Crochet 5 sc around. **[5]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Increase into every stitch. **[10]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Crochet 10 sc around. **[10]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *1 sc, inc* five times. **[15]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Work 15 sc evenly. **[15]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *2 sc, inc* five times. **[20]**
8. **Rnd 8:** Work 20 sc evenly. **[20]**
9. **Rnd 9:** Repeat *3 sc, inc* five times. **[25]**
10. **Rnds 10–17:** Crochet 25 sc in each round for eight rounds. **[25]**
11. **Rnd 18:** Repeat *3 sc, dec* five times. **[20]**
12. **Rnds 19–20:** Work 20 sc for each of the final two rounds. **[20]**

Cut the yarn with a long sewing tail. Leave the ear empty and flatten it gently between your fingers; the broad upper section and narrower base will help it look like a leaf.

### Inner ears — make two in off-white

These are worked **around the two sides of a starting chain** rather than in turned rows. Chain **13** to begin each one.

1. **Rnd 1:** Starting in the **second chain from the hook**, work **2 sc into that chain**. Crochet 1 sc in each of the next **10 chains**, then **3 sc in the last chain**. Continue around the underside of the starting chain and make **11 sc**. **[26]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in each of the next **2 stitches**; make **10 sc**; increase in each of the next **3 stitches**; make **10 sc**; then increase in the last stitch. **[32]**

Slip stitch into the next stitch and fasten off, keeping a long tail. Sew an off-white inner oval onto the center of each ochre ear. Flatten and gently pinch the base of each completed ear, then pin them to the top of the head so the face looks symmetrical before sewing down the bases.

## Part 6 — Crochet the two slender horns

Use **dark-gray yarn** for the horns. The narrow columns are quite simple to make; what matters is keeping their stitches even and using only a little stuffing so they don't become bulky.

Make **two**:

1. **Rnd 1:** Begin with 5 sc in a magic ring. **[5]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Crochet 5 sc around. **[5]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Increase in each of the five stitches. **[10]**
4. **Rnds 4–20:** Work 10 sc in each round for seventeen rounds. **[10]**

Fasten off, leaving a sewing tail. **Stuff lightly.** Place the two horns between the ears and secure their bases with several close stitches. Check from the front and side as you pin them; a tiny difference in angle can change the character's expression.

## Part 7 — Crochet the tiny tail

Even a little three-round detail makes the finished doll feel more complete. Use **ochre yarn**, and make one tail.

1. **Rnd 1:** Make 5 sc into a magic ring. **[5]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in every stitch. **[10]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 sc, inc* five times. **[15]**

Fasten off with a long tail for sewing. **Do not stuff** this little piece. Sew it onto the center of the back of the body, aligned with **Round 43**. Because the skirt will sit over the hips, check where the tail lands before tightening your stitches.

## Part 8 — Make the separate pastel-pink skirt

The pink skirt is made on its own and gently slips over the gazelle's legs. It starts as a narrow ring at the waist, then expands into a softly flared shape. Use **pastel-pink yarn**.

### Form the skirt

Chain **42**. Before joining, lay the chain on a flat surface to be sure it isn't twisted. Slip stitch into the first chain to close it into a ring, then continue working in a spiral.

1. **Rnd 1:** Crochet 1 sc in each of the 42 foundation chains. **[42]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Repeat *6 sc, inc* six times. **[48]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *7 sc, inc* six times. **[54]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *8 sc, inc* six times. **[60]**
5. **Rnds 5–8:** Work 60 sc in each of these four rounds. **[60]**
6. **Rnd 9:** Work 1 slip stitch in each of the 60 stitches to give the lower hem a clean finish. **[60]**

Fasten off and weave the ends in neatly. Try the skirt on over the doll's feet to check the fit before adding its contrasting waistband.

### Add the light-pink waistband

Using **light-pink yarn**, join to the first stitch of the skirt's original Round 1. Work along the **top edge** of the skirt.

1. **Waistband Rnd 1:** Crochet 42 sc all the way around. **[42]**
2. **Waistband Rnd 2:** Work 42 slip stitches to finish the top edge. **[42]**

Fasten off and hide the ends. The waistband creates a subtle line between the pink skirt and the slate-gray bodice. If the fabric looks a little rippled, smooth it with your fingers rather than tugging tightly on the yarn.

## Part 9 — Put the gazelle together

This is the stage where all those small pieces turn into a complete character. **Pin everything in place before sewing permanently**, and check the overall expression from the front and side.

1. **Finish the face.** Sew the stuffed muzzle across **head rounds 15–24**, opposite the beginning-of-round line. Position the two eyes between **rounds 18–19**, approximately three stitches from the muzzle on each side. Embroider the nose and mouth in black if you haven't already.
2. **Check the stuffing.** Make sure the head, neck, torso and both long legs hold their shape without visible filling showing between stitches.
3. **Layer the ears.** Sew each off-white oval inside its ochre outer ear. Flatten, pinch and arrange them near the top of the head before securing.
4. **Set the horns.** Place the lightly stuffed dark-gray horns between the ears. Pin both in position so their height and angle look balanced, then sew around the bases.
5. **Attach the arms.** Sew one arm to either side of the gray bodice **between rounds 30 and 31**. Face the little bobble details forward.
6. **Add the tail.** Sew the unstuffed ochre tail at the back, around **body round 43**.
7. **Dress your gazelle.** Slide the skirt carefully up over the legs and adjust the waistband at the base of the gray bodice.
8. **Add the final touches.** You may embroider a few tiny light-pink cheek marks. Check every seam, hide all remaining tails, and gently straighten the ears and horns.

## Helpful little fixes

**My white facial patches look uneven.** Follow the color blocks of **head rounds 15–21** exactly. Marking each switch between ochre and off-white makes the pattern easier to count without losing track of the round.

**The legs don't line up with the face.** Before working the first leg, recheck the two 15-stitch openings and the three-stitch gap at both front and back. Move the beginning point a few stitches if necessary so the split is centered.

**The ears are too stiff.** Unlike the head and horns, the ears aren't stuffed. Flatten each one and sew the cream oval in place before pinching the base.

**One horn leans too much.** Lighten the stuffing and secure the base with several small stitches. A pair of pins helps you compare angles before sewing.

**The skirt is difficult to pull on.** Check your 42-chain foundation and avoid making it too tight. Try the garment over the lower body before weaving in the final ends.
