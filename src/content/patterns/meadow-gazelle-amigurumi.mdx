Meet Meadow Gazelle: a gentle, long-legged crochet companion with two bright ears, tiny horns and a soft pink skirt that can be slipped on after the main toy is complete. The details make this one such a lovely project: the softly colored muzzle, the off-white patches near the eyes, and the fitted slate-grey bodice that grows directly from the head.

This is a satisfying make for a crocheter who's comfortable counting rounds and ready to practice **color placement, sewing small pieces neatly, and splitting one crochet tube into two legs**. We'll build the gazelle one section at a time, with clear stitch totals and little reminders where they matter.

## Your gazelle at a glance

- **Finished height:** around **37 cm / 14.5 in**, including the horns, with the suggested yarn and hook.
- **Skill level:** intermediate. The body is straightforward once you're used to spirals; the face colors and leg split need more attention.
- **Hook:** **2.75 mm (US C/2)**.
- **Construction:** a separately crocheted muzzle; continuous head, neck, torso and legs; two separate arms; layered ears; horns; a tiny tail; and a removable skirt.
- **Terminology:** **US crochet terms** throughout. The numbers in square brackets tell you how many stitches should be present at the end of the round.

If you haven't worked with a magic ring before, begin with [our magic-ring lesson](/learn/magic-ring-crochet-in-the-round/). For the shaping below, you can keep [the increase-and-decrease guide](/learn/crochet-increases-and-decreases/) open beside you.

## Crochet abbreviations

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **MR** | Magic ring |
| **sc** | Single crochet |
| **inc** | Work 2 single crochets into 1 stitch |
| **dec** | Single crochet 2 stitches together |
| **ch** | Chain |
| **sl st** | Slip stitch |
| **BLO** | Back loops only |
| **5-dc bobble** | Five partially worked double crochets closed together in one stitch |
| **rnd** | Round |
| **st / sts** | Stitch / stitches |
| **[ ]** | Stitch count at the end of the round |

**Before you start:** Unless stated otherwise, work in **continuous spiral rounds**, placing a marker at the beginning of every round. When changing colors within one round, complete the last yarn-over of the previous stitch in the new color. Keep the carried strand loose on the inside so the facial fabric doesn't pucker.

## Part 1 — Make the small, two-tone muzzle

We'll start with the muzzle because it's easier to embroider its expression **before** sewing it to the face. Begin in **off-white**. The off-white sides and ochre center emerge as the tube widens.

1. **Rnd 1:** Make 6 single crochet stitches inside a magic ring, then draw the ring closed. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Work an increase into each stitch around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 sc, then an increase* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Crochet one sc in every stitch. **[18]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Work **6 off-white sc**, change to **ochre for 6 sc**, then return to **off-white for 6 sc**. **[18]**
6. **Rnd 6:** In off-white, repeat *2 sc, inc* twice. Do the same two repeats in ochre, then twice again in off-white. You'll have three blocks of 8 stitches. **[24]**
7. **Rnds 7–8:** For **each** round, make **8 off-white sc**, **8 ochre sc**, then **8 off-white sc**. **[24]**
8. **Rnd 9:** In off-white, repeat *3 sc, inc* twice; work the same pattern twice in ochre; then twice in off-white. You should finish with 10 stitches in each color section. **[30]**
9. **Rnds 10–11:** Work **10 off-white sc**, **10 ochre sc** and **10 off-white sc** in each round. **[30]**

Fasten off, leaving a long tail for sewing. Embroider a little nose and curved mouth with **black yarn** now, while you can still reach the inside easily. Add fiberfill to the muzzle; aim for a softly rounded shape rather than a tightly packed ball.

**Color tip:** The three color blocks need to stay aligned. Put a removable marker at each changeover if the different strands make it hard to track the round.

## Part 2 — Crochet the head, neckline and torso in one piece

Now we'll build the main shape from the **top of the head downward**, working without a seam between head and body. Start with **ochre yarn**.

### Widen the top of the head

1. **Rnd 1:** Crochet 6 sc into a magic ring. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in each of the 6 stitches. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 sc, inc* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *2 sc, inc* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *3 sc, inc* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *4 sc, inc* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *5 sc, inc* six times. **[42]**
8. **Rnd 8:** Repeat *6 sc, inc* six times. **[48]**
9. **Rnd 9:** Repeat *7 sc, inc* six times. **[54]**
10. **Rnd 10:** Repeat *8 sc, inc* six times. **[60]**
11. **Rnds 11–14:** Work 60 sc in each of the next four rounds. **[60]**

At this point the head is a wide, shallow cup. The next rounds form the gazelle's cream-colored facial patches. Watch the **order** of the color sections; don't simply distribute the off-white at random.

### Place the off-white areas around the eyes

12. **Rnd 15:** Work **16 ochre sc, 4 off-white sc, 20 ochre sc, 4 off-white sc, 16 ochre sc**. **[60]**
13. **Rnd 16:** Make **14 ochre sc, 8 off-white sc, 16 ochre sc, 8 off-white sc, 14 ochre sc**. **[60]**
14. **Rnds 17–19:** In **each** of these three rounds, make **12 ochre sc, 12 off-white sc, 12 ochre sc, 12 off-white sc, 12 ochre sc**. **[60]**
15. **Rnd 20:** Work **13 ochre sc, 10 off-white sc, 14 ochre sc, 10 off-white sc, 13 ochre sc**. **[60]**
16. **Rnd 21:** Make **14 ochre sc, 8 off-white sc, 16 ochre sc, 8 off-white sc, 14 ochre sc**. **[60]**
17. **Rnd 22:** Change fully back to **ochre** and work 60 sc around. **[60]**

You should now see two balanced, off-white facial patches on either side of the front. Count each section before you turn to the next shade: a single missed stitch can shift a patch across the face.

### Shape the lower face and place the features

18. **Rnd 23:** Repeat *3 sc, dec* twelve times. The head narrows to **48 stitches**. **[48]**
19. **Rnd 24:** Repeat *2 sc, dec* twelve times. **[36]**
20. **Rnd 25:** Repeat *4 sc, dec* six times. **[30]**

**Pause for the face.** Pin and sew the prepared muzzle over **Rounds 15–24**, positioning it on the side opposite the beginning-of-round seam. Place the **10 mm safety eyes between Rounds 18–19**, about **3 stitches away from the muzzle** on each side. Check the placement from the front before pressing on the washers. For toys intended for very young children, embroider the eyes instead. Stuff the head as you work the next decrease rounds.

21. **Rnd 26:** Repeat *3 sc, dec* six times. **[24]**
22. **Rnd 27:** Repeat *2 sc, dec* six times. **[18]**
23. **Rnd 28:** Work 18 sc evenly around the narrow neck. **[18]**

### Grow the slate-grey bodice

Change to **slate-grey yarn**. The next increases turn the neck opening into a small sweater-like upper torso without sewing on a separate garment.

24. **Rnd 29:** Repeat *2 sc, inc* six times. **[24]**
25. **Rnd 30:** Repeat *3 sc, inc* six times. **[30]**
26. **Rnds 31–33:** Crochet 30 sc in each round. **[30]**
27. **Rnd 34:** Repeat *4 sc, inc* six times. **[36]**
28. **Rnds 35–40:** Keep the same 36-stitch count for six complete rounds. **[36]**

Change back to **ochre** for the lower torso. The first round is worked **through back loops only**, leaving a subtle ridge at the hem of the bodice.

29. **Rnd 41:** Work 36 sc through the **back loops only**. **[36]**
30. **Rnds 42–46:** Work 36 sc in each round. **[36]**

**Counting checkpoint:** You should have **36 stitches** around the lower edge of the body. Leave the opening unfinished; those stitches are divided into two legs in the next section.

## Part 3 — Divide the body into two long legs

The legs aren't sewn-on tubes: they are formed **directly from the ochre torso**. Before crocheting either one, use four removable markers to plan the division of the 36-stitch opening:

- **15 stitches** for leg one.
- **15 stitches** for leg two.
- **3 stitches** left between the legs at the front.
- **3 stitches** left between the legs at the back.

Check that the leg positions line up with the front of the face. If the join sits off-center, move the beginning point by making or undoing a few sc before you start the leg.

### First leg

Bring the designated front and back edges together and make a single crochet stitch to join the first leg into a ring. Count that joining stitch as the **first stitch of Round 47**.

1. **Rnds 47–72:** Crochet **15 sc in every round** for twenty-six rounds. Keep the stitch marker moving up the long, narrow tube. **[15]**
2. **Rnd 73:** Repeat *1 sc, dec* five times. **[10]**
3. **Rnd 74:** Make five decreases, leaving **5 stitches**. **[5]**

Stuff the leg and body **firmly as you work**; it's difficult to push filling down the entire tube once the end has narrowed. Fasten off with a long tail. Thread the tail through the **front loop** of each final stitch and draw the opening shut, then hide the yarn end.

### Second leg

Reattach ochre yarn in the **fourth unworked stitch at the back of Round 46**, as indicated by the original construction. Work around the other 15-stitch opening.

1. **Rnd 47:** Crochet 15 sc around the second leg opening, including the joining stitch that closes the circle. **[15]**
2. **Rnds 48–72:** Crochet 15 sc per round, matching the first leg's length. **[15]**
3. **Rnd 73:** Repeat *1 sc, dec* five times. **[10]**
4. **Rnd 74:** Work five decreases. **[5]**

Add more filling if needed. Close the final five loops as on the first leg, and sew the **3-stitch gap between the legs** closed with a tapestry needle. The legs should hang straight and be about the same length.

## Part 4 — Make the arms with tiny bobble details

Make **two arms in ochre yarn**. Each one is a slim tube with a small **five-double-crochet bobble** near the hand. The bobble is worked into a single stitch and counts as **one stitch**, so it doesn't change your round total.

1. **Rnd 1:** Make 6 sc in a magic ring. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in every stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnds 3–4:** Crochet 12 sc in each round. **[12]**
4. **Rnd 5:** Crochet **1 sc**, make a **5-dc bobble** in the next stitch, then work **10 sc** around. **[12]**
5. **Rnds 6–20:** Work 12 sc in each of these fifteen rounds. **[12]**
6. **Rnd 21:** Repeat *1 sc, dec* four times. **[8]**

Fasten off with a long sewing tail. Add fiberfill, keeping the upper arm soft enough to lie naturally against the body. Make a second arm, then sew both to the sides of the slate-grey bodice **between Rounds 30 and 31**.

## Part 5 — Make two-layer gazelle ears

Each tall ear consists of an **ochre outer piece** with a smaller **off-white inner oval** sewn inside. We'll make two of each, then attach them to the head. **Do not stuff the ears.**

### Ochre outer ears (make 2)

1. **Rnd 1:** Start with 5 sc in a magic ring. **[5]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Crochet 5 sc evenly. **[5]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Increase in all five stitches. **[10]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Crochet 10 sc evenly. **[10]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *1 sc, inc* five times. **[15]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Crochet 15 sc. **[15]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *2 sc, inc* five times. **[20]**
8. **Rnd 8:** Crochet 20 sc. **[20]**
9. **Rnd 9:** Repeat *3 sc, inc* five times. **[25]**
10. **Rnds 10–17:** Crochet 25 sc in each of these eight rounds. **[25]**
11. **Rnd 18:** Repeat *3 sc, dec* five times. **[20]**
12. **Rnds 19–20:** Work 20 sc in each round. **[20]**

Fasten off, leaving a long tail. Flatten each ear into a gentle leaf shape without adding any stuffing.

### Off-white ear centers (make 2)

These smaller ovals are worked **around both sides of a foundation chain**. Start in off-white and **chain 13**.

1. **Rnd 1:** Beginning in the **second chain from the hook**, make an increase, work **10 sc**, then **3 sc in the final chain**. Turn the work around and crochet **11 sc along the underside** of the foundation chain. **[26]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in each of the next **2 stitches**; crochet **10 sc**; increase in the next **3 stitches**; crochet **10 sc**; then increase in the last stitch. **[32]**

Slip stitch into the following stitch and fasten off with a sewing tail. Arrange one cream oval over the center of each ochre ear. Stitch it in place, then **pinch the base of the ear** to give it a gentle inward curve. Sew both finished ears to the top of the head, checking their angle before tightening the seams.

## Part 6 — Crochet the long, dark-grey horns

Make **two slender horns in dark-grey yarn**. They are intentionally simple tubes: their tall shape comes from a narrow stitch count and many even rounds.

1. **Rnd 1:** Work 5 sc inside a magic ring. **[5]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Crochet 5 sc around. **[5]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Increase in every stitch. **[10]**
4. **Rnds 4–20:** Work 10 sc in each of the next seventeen rounds. **[10]**

Fasten off with a long tail and **stuff lightly** so the horns stand without becoming stiff or bulky. Position them between the two ears and sew them carefully to the top of the head. A little extra stitching around their bases can help them sit upright.

## Part 7 — Add the tiny tail

This is one of the quickest pieces. In **ochre yarn**:

1. **Rnd 1:** Make 5 sc in a magic ring. **[5]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Work one sc in each stitch. **[5]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Increase in every stitch. **[10]**

Fasten off with a sewing tail. **Do not stuff.** Sew the tail onto the center back of the lower torso, around **Round 43**.

## Part 8 — Crochet the removable pink skirt

The gazelle's skirt is made separately, so you can dress her after the body is finished. Use **pastel-pink yarn** and make a **chain of 42**. Lay the chain flat, check that it isn't twisted, and close it into a circle with a slip stitch.

1. **Rnd 1:** Work one sc in each of the 42 chains. **[42]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Repeat *6 sc, inc* six times to start flaring the fabric. **[48]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *7 sc, inc* six times. **[54]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *8 sc, inc* six times. **[60]**
5. **Rnds 5–8:** Crochet 60 sc in each of these four rounds. **[60]**
6. **Rnd 9:** Make a slip stitch in all 60 stitches for a neat lower hem. **[60]**

Fasten off and weave in the yarn ends. The skirt should gently flare from the waist without pulling the legs together.

### Finish the waistband

Pick up a little **light-pink yarn** and attach it to the first stitch of the skirt's original **Round 1**.

1. **Waistband Rnd 1:** Crochet 42 sc all the way around the top edge. **[42]**
2. **Waistband Rnd 2:** Slip stitch into each of those 42 stitches. **[42]**

Fasten off and hide the ends. Slip the completed skirt over the gazelle's legs and gently adjust it at the base of the slate-grey bodice.

## Part 9 — Put everything together

All the little pieces become much easier to place when you **pin before sewing**. Take a moment to look at the whole character from the front and side.

1. **Face:** Sew the stuffed two-tone muzzle between head Rounds **15 and 24**, on the side opposite the beginning of the round. Add the 10 mm eyes between Rounds **18 and 19**, approximately three stitches from the muzzle on either side. Embroider the nose and mouth in black if you haven't already.
2. **Stuffing:** Check that the head, body and both legs are filled evenly, then close the ends as described above.
3. **Ears:** Stitch each off-white ear center inside its ochre outer layer, pinch the bases, and sew them to the top of the head.
4. **Horns:** Secure the two lightly stuffed dark-grey horns between the ears.
5. **Arms:** Attach the arms to the bodice between **Rounds 30 and 31** with the bobble details facing forward.
6. **Tail:** Stitch the little ochre tail onto the back near **Round 43**.
7. **Skirt:** Pull the pink skirt on over the legs, arrange the waistband around the torso, and smooth the hem.
8. **Finishing:** Embroider small light-pink cheek accents if you'd like, adjust any crooked parts, and weave in every loose yarn tail.

## Little tips for a lovelier finish

**My facial patches are uneven.** Check the exact sequence of ochre and off-white stitches in Rounds 15–21 of the head. Marking the color changes makes them much easier to keep symmetrical.

**The ears look flat.** They shouldn't be stuffed. The gentle folded shape comes from pressing the ear flat, placing the off-white oval at its center and pinching the base before sewing.

**The horns lean.** Add a small amount of stuffing and sew around the base using several secure passes through the head stitches. Try pinning both horns while checking the profile before fastening them down.

**One leg looks longer.** Each leg has the same **26 straight rounds** before its two closing rounds. Count those rounds carefully and make sure neither leg is stretched while stuffing.

**The skirt won't slide over the hips.** Check the 42-chain foundation ring for tightness and confirm the stitch totals before the skirt widens. A slightly looser starting chain can help when using a less stretchy cotton yarn.
