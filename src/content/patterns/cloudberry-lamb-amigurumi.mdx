Some crochet friends have a way of looking like they belong on a favorite armchair. **Cloudberry Lamb** is one of them. There's a soft white fleece, two floppy grey ears, enormous round feet and a small pink bow that makes the whole toy feel extra sweet.

The nicest detail is one you might not notice straightaway: **the head is made in two layers**. You crochet the soft-grey face first, then make a separate fluffy white covering that cups it like a little woolly hood. That double construction creates Cloudberry's unmistakably lamb-like face without hiding the eyes under fuzzy yarn.

We'll work through the pieces carefully, with checkpoints for the stitch counts and assembly along the way. This is an **intermediate** project, especially if fuzzy yarn is new to you, but you don't need unusual stitches—just patient counting and a few securely sewn seams.

## Before you begin

- **Size:** approximately **30 cm / 12 in tall when seated**, using yarn of a similar weight to the original design. Your finished size may vary with yarn and tension.
- **Hooks:** **4 mm** for the grey smooth-yarn parts; **6.5 mm** for the white fluffy head covering and body.
- **Yarns:** pale grey for the inner head, ears, arms and upper feet; dark grey for the bottom of the feet; white textured bulky yarn for the fleece; a little pink for the nose and a pink ribbon.
- **Construction:** two-layer head, separately stuffed white body, two arms, two broad seated feet, two ears and a little tail.
- **Terms:** **US crochet terminology**. Unless a section says otherwise, crochet in continuous spirals, using a marker to track the beginning of the round.

**A note about fluffy yarn:** It can hide the top loops of each stitch. The source suggests holding a smooth yarn in the same shade alongside the fluffy strand if that helps you see and feel the stitches. Use a hook that keeps the fabric closed enough to hold the stuffing.

If you need a refresher first, our [magic-ring tutorial](/learn/magic-ring-crochet-in-the-round/) and [increase-and-decrease guide](/learn/crochet-increases-and-decreases/) are handy companions.

## Stitch guide

| Abbreviation | What to do |
|---|---|
| **MR** | Magic ring |
| **sc** | Single crochet |
| **inc** | Two single crochets in the same stitch |
| **dec** | Decrease two stitches into one; an invisible decrease works neatly |
| **ch** | Chain |
| **st / sts** | Stitch / stitches |
| **rnd** | Round |
| **[number]** | Total stitches after that round |

The repeated directions are written out in everyday language, and the **[stitch count]** at the end of each round is your little checkpoint. When a range is shown, complete the same instruction in **every round** of that range.

## Part 1 — Crochet the smooth grey face and inner head

Use **pale-grey worsted yarn** and your **4 mm hook**. Start at the crown of the head and work downward. This first ball becomes the firm, softly rounded face that will be visible through the white fleece.

1. **Rnd 1:** Place 6 sc into a magic ring and pull the center closed. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase once in each stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Work *1 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *2 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *3 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *4 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Crochet one sc into each of the 36 stitches. **[36]**
8. **Rnd 8:** Repeat *5 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[42]**
9. **Rnd 9:** Repeat *6 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[48]**
10. **Rnd 10:** Repeat *7 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[54]**
11. **Rnd 11:** Repeat *8 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[60]**
12. **Rnd 12:** Repeat *9 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[66]**
13. **Rnd 13:** Work 66 sc evenly around. **[66]**
14. **Rnd 14:** Repeat *10 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[72]**
15. **Rnds 15–16:** Keep the circle at 72 sc for **both** rounds. **[72 each round]**
16. **Rnd 17:** Repeat *11 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[78]**
17. **Rnds 18–19:** Work 78 sc in each round. **[78 each round]**
18. **Rnd 20:** Repeat *12 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[84]**
19. **Rnd 21:** Crochet 84 sc without shaping. **[84]**
20. **Rnd 22:** Repeat *13 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[90]**
21. **Rnds 23–30:** Work eight even rounds, with **90 sc in each one**. **[90 each round]**
22. **Rnd 31:** Repeat *13 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[84]**
23. **Rnd 32:** Repeat *12 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[78]**
24. **Rnd 33:** Crochet 78 sc without shaping. **[78]**
25. **Rnd 34:** Repeat *11 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[72]**
26. **Rnd 35:** Repeat *10 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[66]**
27. **Rnd 36:** Make 66 sc evenly. **[66]**
28. **Rnd 37:** Repeat *9 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[60]**
29. **Rnd 38:** Repeat *8 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[54]**
30. **Rnd 39:** Repeat *7 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[48]**
31. **Rnd 40:** Repeat *6 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[42]**
32. **Rnd 41:** Repeat *5 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[36]**
33. **Rnd 42:** Repeat *4 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[30]**

**Time to make the face:** Place the **15 mm safety eyes between Rounds 23 and 24**, with **13 stitches between the eyes**. Check the front view before fitting the washers. Embroider a small dark-pink nose and a gentle mouth with pink yarn, securing the ends on the inside. If the toy is intended for a child under three, use embroidered eyes rather than safety eyes.

Firmly but evenly stuff the grey head, then close it with the final four shaping rounds:

34. **Rnd 43:** Repeat *3 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[24]**
35. **Rnd 44:** Repeat *2 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[18]**
36. **Rnd 45:** Repeat *1 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[12]**
37. **Rnd 46:** Work six decreases. **[6]**

Fasten off. Pull the yarn tail through the front loops of those last six stitches and cinch the small opening closed. Hide the end within the head.

**Shape check:** The inner head is a complete, stuffed grey piece before the fleece goes on. Don't try to change colors halfway through it—the white covering is its own separate crochet piece.

## Part 2 — Make the fluffy white head covering

Switch to **white textured bulky yarn** and the **6.5 mm hook**. This section looks like a loose, rounded bowl when finished. It is not stuffed separately; instead, the grey head fits inside it.

The original starts with a chain-two center. You can use that same method: **ch 2 and place the first 6 sc into the second chain from the hook**.

1. **Rnd 1:** Start with 6 sc in the second chain from the hook. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase six times. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *2 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *3 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *4 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Work 36 sc without shaping. **[36]**
8. **Rnd 8:** Repeat *5 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[42]**
9. **Rnd 9:** Crochet 42 sc evenly. **[42]**
10. **Rnd 10:** Repeat *6 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[48]**
11. **Rnd 11:** Work 48 sc evenly. **[48]**
12. **Rnd 12:** Repeat *6 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[42]**
13. **Rnd 13:** Repeat *5 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[36]**
14. **Rnd 14:** Repeat *4 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[30]**

Cut the yarn, leaving **about 60 cm / 24 in** for attaching the fluffy covering. With the opening facing forward, gently pull the covering over the finished grey head. Smooth the fleece so the grey face is centered, then sew around the opening **roughly 1.5 cm / ½ in in from the edge**, catching stitches of the grey head beneath the fluffy yarn. Work all the way around so the covering cannot pull loose.

**Fluffy-yarn checkpoint:** The source emphasizes a snug fit with no loose gaps. If the white covering is much too large or small, check that you've used the correct yarn and hook for this section before changing any stitch counts.

## Part 3 — Grow the rounded white body

Keep the **6.5 mm hook and fluffy white yarn**. This body is a separate stuffed shape, attached beneath the head at the end.

To start, **ch 2** and crochet 6 sc into the second chain from the hook, just as you did for the outer head.

1. **Rnd 1:** Work 6 sc at the center. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in all six stitches. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *2 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *3 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *4 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *5 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[42]**
8. **Rnd 8:** Repeat *6 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[48]**
9. **Rnd 9:** Repeat *7 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[54]**
10. **Rnds 10–11:** Keep the count at 54 sc for both rounds. **[54 each round]**
11. **Rnd 12:** Repeat *7 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[48]**
12. **Rnds 13–14:** Make 48 sc in each round. **[48 each round]**
13. **Rnd 15:** Repeat *6 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[42]**
14. **Rnd 16:** Work 42 sc around. **[42]**
15. **Rnd 17:** Repeat *5 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[36]**
16. **Rnd 18:** Repeat *4 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[30]**
17. **Rnd 19:** Repeat *3 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[24]**
18. **Rnd 20:** Repeat *2 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[18]**

Leave a long tail when fastening off. Stuff the white body so it looks plump without opening holes in the fuzzy fabric. Place the finished head over its narrow opening and sew it on with **several secure passes**. Because the lamb has a large head, this seam should be particularly strong.

## Part 4 — Crochet the two slim grey arms

Return to **pale-grey worsted yarn and your 4 mm hook**. Make two identical arms, keeping the stuffing soft enough that they hang naturally.

1. **Rnd 1:** Begin with 6 sc in a magic ring. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in each stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *2 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *3 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnds 6–8:** Crochet 30 sc in **each** of these three rounds. **[30 each round]**
7. **Rnd 9:** Repeat *3 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[24]**
8. **Rnd 10:** Crochet 24 sc evenly. **[24]**
9. **Rnd 11:** Repeat *2 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[18]**
10. **Rnds 12–22:** Work **11 rounds** of 18 sc each to make the long narrow arm. **[18 each round]**

Fasten off both arms, leaving generous sewing tails. Stuff each one, with a little more filling near the rounded hand and less at the top. Pin the arms to either side of the body so their lengths match, then sew them on securely.

## Part 5 — Make the oversized seated feet

For Cloudberry's two broad feet, start with **dark-grey yarn** and your **4 mm hook**, then switch to **pale grey** when indicated. Make the whole sequence twice.

1. **Rnd 1:** Make 6 sc in a magic ring. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in every stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *2 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *3 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *4 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *5 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[42]**
8. **Rnd 8:** Repeat *6 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[48]**
9. **Rnd 9:** Repeat *7 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[54]**

**Change to pale-grey yarn** for the next round. The lower toe area should remain noticeably darker than the upper foot.

10. **Rnd 10:** Repeat *8 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[60]**
11. **Rnd 11:** Work 60 sc around. **[60]**
12. **Rnd 12:** Repeat *8 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[54]**
13. **Rnd 13:** Repeat *7 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[48]**
14. **Rnd 14:** Crochet 48 sc evenly. **[48]**
15. **Rnd 15:** Repeat *6 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[42]**
16. **Rnd 16:** Work 42 sc without shaping. **[42]**
17. **Rnd 17:** Repeat *5 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[36]**
18. **Rnd 18:** Work 36 sc evenly. **[36]**
19. **Rnd 19:** Repeat *4 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[30]**
20. **Rnd 20:** Repeat *3 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[24]**
21. **Rnds 21–24:** Work 24 sc in each of these four rounds. **[24 each round]**
22. **Rnd 25:** Repeat *2 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[18]**
23. **Rnds 26–30:** Crochet 18 sc in each of the final five rounds. **[18 each round]**

Fasten off, keeping a long sewing tail. Stuff the feet—generously at the toes and more softly along the narrow part. **Sew each opening closed before attaching the foot** to the lower front of the body.

**Sitting tip:** Pin both feet in place while the lamb is seated on a table. The source emphasizes secure, repeated stitches here because these feet are comparatively heavy. Adjust their angle until the lamb can sit without tipping backward, then sew them on firmly.

## Part 6 — Fold and attach the little grey ears

Make **two ears** in **pale-grey yarn** with the **4 mm hook**. They are softly cupped shapes; do not stuff them.

1. **Rnd 1:** Make 6 sc in a magic ring. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in every stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Work 18 sc around. **[18]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *2 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[24]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *3 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[30]**
7. **Rnds 7–8:** Work two plain rounds of 30 sc each. **[30 each round]**
8. **Rnd 9:** Repeat *3 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[24]**
9. **Rnd 10:** Crochet 24 sc evenly. **[24]**
10. **Rnd 11:** Repeat *2 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[18]**
11. **Rnds 12–14:** Make 18 sc in each of these three rounds. **[18 each round]**

Fasten off with a long tail. Flatten each ear and sew the open end closed. Fold that closed base in half and stitch through the layers to make a gently pinched shape. Attach one ear on each side of the head, taking the needle **through the fluffy outer layer and into the underlying grey head** so the ear is properly anchored.

## Part 7 — Crochet a small tail for the back

Use **pale-grey yarn** and the **4 mm hook**. The tail is only lightly filled so it stays a little flattened instead of forming a perfect sphere.

1. **Rnd 1:** Start with 6 sc in a magic ring. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase six times. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *2 sc, 1 inc* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnds 5–8:** Make 24 sc in each of four rounds. **[24 each round]**
6. **Rnd 9:** Repeat *2 sc, 1 dec* six times. **[18]**
7. **Rnd 10:** Crochet 18 sc without shaping. **[18]**

Fasten off with a long sewing tail. Add a little fiberfill and flatten the piece with your fingers. Stitch it to the **center back** of the lamb, high enough that it won't stop the toy sitting upright.

## Part 8 — Bring Cloudberry together

Arrange all the pieces before you tighten any seams. If you've already joined the head and body as you worked, focus now on balance and the smaller details.

1. **Set the head:** Position the grey face inside its fluffy covering; check that the opening sits evenly all the way around and the eyes remain unobstructed.
2. **Connect head to body:** Sew through the bottom of the finished head and the body's 18-stitch neck opening with multiple strong passes.
3. **Attach the ears:** Pinch the ears gently, angle them outward and stitch all the way into the grey inner head.
4. **Add the arms:** Position one stuffed grey arm on each side of the fluffy body and secure the joins.
5. **Balance the feet:** Sit Cloudberry on a flat surface while pinning the two large feet. Sew through several rows for strength.
6. **Fix the tail:** Add the lightly stuffed, flattened tail to the back without interfering with the sitting pose.
7. **Tie the ribbon:** Finish with a soft pink ribbon tied around the neck. For a toy intended for a small child, omit the loose ribbon to avoid a strangulation hazard.
8. **Hide ends and check seams:** Weave in every tail, tug gently on all attachments and check for exposed stuffing.

## Troubleshooting the fluffy yarn

**I can't see where my next stitch goes.** Run a finger along the top of the last round and count the little bumps one at a time. Try pairing the fluffy yarn with a smooth yarn in a matching shade, as the book suggests, to make the stitch structure easier to find.

**The white head covering looks baggy.** Confirm the hook size and make sure the smooth grey inner head is stuffed before inserting it. Pin the opening evenly before sewing; the cover should wrap around the head with no big gaps.

**The lamb keeps falling backward.** The feet are large and add weight. Move them slightly forward and test the seated pose with sewing pins before attaching the seams permanently.

**My white body shows bits of stuffing.** Use tighter stitches or a slightly smaller hook while keeping the same round counts. The original design relies on the fuzzy yarn to mask small gaps, not wide holes.

**The ears don't stay put.** Sewing only through the fluffy surface won't give them much support. Pass the needle deeply enough to catch the grey inner head underneath.
