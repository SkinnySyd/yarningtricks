Meet **Heather Highland Cow**: a little bundle of ginger yarn with a soft snout, a pair of cream-colored horns and a fringe that looks as though she's just wandered out of a windswept Scottish meadow. Those long, tousled strands are the magic of this make. Instead of attaching one piece of hair at a time, you'll **crochet loops into the coat and cut them open** when the piece is finished.

There is plenty to enjoy here if you like projects with character. Heather has a **smooth, rounded head** growing from a beige muzzle, a separate shaggy **topknot**, a thick fluffy body, little folded ears, four snug hooves and a **braided tail**. We'll take it slowly, piece by piece, so you can watch her personality appear as you work.

This is an **advanced amigurumi project** because of the loop stitches, two strands of yarn held together and the way the legs are attached. Basic increases and decreases will feel familiar; the shaggy texture is the skill you'll be practicing most.

## Before you begin: your Highland cow at a glance

- **Finished height:** approximately **18 cm / 7 in**, depending on your yarn, hook and tension.
- **Difficulty:** advanced, especially if you haven't made loop-stitch fur before.
- **Hook:** **3 mm (US D/3)**, with light-worsted-weight yarn.
- **Main colors:** beige for the muzzle and hooves; dark orange for the head, ears, upper legs and coat; light orange mixed with dark orange for the fluffy areas; cream for the horns; black for the face and split-hoof markings.
- **Construction:** crochet the oval head and muzzle together, then separately make the topknot, two horns, two ears, fluffy body, four legs and braided tail. Sew the pieces into position at the end.
- **Terms:** written in **US crochet terminology**. Bracketed numbers such as **[24]** show the stitch count after completing that round. Unless we say otherwise, work in continuous spirals and move the beginning-of-round stitch marker upward.

For a quick refresher, keep our [magic-ring and crochet-in-the-round lesson](/learn/magic-ring-crochet-in-the-round/) and [shaping guide for increases and decreases](/learn/crochet-increases-and-decreases/) close by.

## Crochet stitches and abbreviations

| Short form | What it means |
|---|---|
| **MR** | Magic ring |
| **ch** | Chain |
| **sc** | Single crochet |
| **inc** | Two single crochets worked into the same stitch |
| **dec** | Single-crochet two stitches together, preferably invisibly |
| **BLO** | Back loop only |
| **sl st** | Slip stitch |
| **loop 1** | One loop stitch, counted as one stitch |
| **loop 1 + sc** | A loop stitch **and** one regular single crochet in the **same** stitch (an increase) |
| **st / sts** | Stitch / stitches |
| **rnd** | Round |

### The special stitch: make a long yarn loop

This pattern gets its shaggy coat from a **loop stitch**, which works a stretched loop of yarn into an otherwise single-crochet-like stitch. For this particular cow, make each loop about **5 cm / 2 in long** before cutting it open later.

1. Hold the working yarn so it makes a long loop around your index finger. For the fluffy sections, work with **one strand of light orange and one of dark orange held together**.
2. Put the hook into the next stitch and catch **both strands of that loop**. Draw them back through the stitch, keeping the long loop stretched out. You should have **three loops on your hook**.
3. Yarn over once more, and pull through all three loops. You have completed **one loop stitch**. Keep the long loop outside the fabric, where you'll be able to cut it later.
4. When you see **loop 1 + sc**, put **one loop stitch and one normal sc into the same base stitch**. They count as **two stitches**, which is why those rounds increase.

**Try a mini practice swatch first.** A few rows of loop stitches help you find an even loop length. Keep your stitch marker on the plain crochet side so it doesn't disappear into the fringe. Don't snip any loops until the whole piece is finished, stuffed and shaped; the intact loops are easier to manage while crocheting.

## Part 1 — Crochet the rounded head and beige muzzle

Begin with **beige yarn**. The head starts with a small oval rather than a magic ring. That foundation-chain oval becomes Heather's softly rounded snout; after a few rounds, the yarn changes to ginger to form the rest of her face.

**Start with a chain of 6.** Work around both sides of the chain so that the first round is oval-shaped.

1. **Rnd 1:** Starting in the second chain from the hook, make **2 sc** in that stitch. Work **1 sc in each of the next 4 chains**. Continue along the opposite side of the foundation chain: make **2 sc** in the first stitch, then **1 sc in each of the next 4 stitches**. **[12]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in the next **2 stitches**, crochet **3 sc**, increase in the next **3 stitches**, crochet **3 sc**, and increase in the final stitch. **[18]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *8 sc, inc* twice. **[20]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Crochet one sc in each of the 20 stitches. **[20]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *4 sc, inc* four times. The little beige muzzle now has **24 stitches**. **[24]**

Switch to **dark-orange yarn**. There is no separate muzzle piece to sew on: the beige section leads directly into the main head.

6. **Rnds 6–9:** Make **24 sc in each round**, for four even rounds. **[24]**
7. **Rnd 10:** Repeat *3 sc, inc* six times. **[30]**
8. **Rnd 11:** Work 30 sc without increasing. **[30]**
9. **Rnd 12:** Repeat *4 sc, inc* six times. **[36]**
10. **Rnd 13:** Crochet 36 sc around. **[36]**
11. **Rnd 14:** Repeat *5 sc, inc* six times. **[42]**
12. **Rnd 15:** Repeat *6 sc, inc* six times. **[48]**
13. **Rnds 16–20:** Work one sc into each stitch for five rounds. **[48]**

### Where the little eyes go

Hold the head with the **original six-chain foundation running left to right** across the muzzle. Find the middle of **Round 12** at the front and mark it. The original construction places the **9 mm eyes between Rounds 11 and 12**, approximately **five stitches to either side** of that central mark. Pin or temporarily insert both eyes and look straight at the face before fitting the washers.

If this cow will be handled by a baby or very young child, **embroider the eyes** rather than using safety-eye hardware. It is easiest to sew the expression while you can still reach the inside of the head.

Now we'll gradually close the back of the head. Add fiberfill along the way, keeping the beige muzzle rounded.

14. **Rnd 21:** Repeat *6 sc, dec* six times. **[42]**
15. **Rnd 22:** Repeat *5 sc, dec* six times. **[36]**
16. **Rnd 23:** Repeat *4 sc, dec* six times. **[30]**
17. **Rnd 24:** Repeat *3 sc, dec* six times. **[24]**
18. **Rnd 25:** Repeat *2 sc, dec* six times. **[18]**
19. **Rnd 26:** Repeat *1 sc, dec* six times. **[12]**
20. **Rnd 27:** Make six decreases, until only **six stitches** remain. **[6]**

Finish stuffing. Cut the yarn with a tail left over, run the tail through the **front loops** of those six stitches, and pull snugly to close the back of the head. Hide the end inside.

**Give the muzzle its expression:** With a little black yarn, make each nostril from **four short running stitches over Round 3**, keeping roughly **three stitches between the two nostrils**. Add one longer stitched line along the lower side of the muzzle for a smile. Check the face from the front so the nose and mouth sit naturally on the beige oval.

## Part 2 — Make the fluffy ginger topknot

This is Heather's trademark hairstyle. Rather than attaching loose lengths of hair individually, we'll work a small cap in **long loop stitches**. Hold **one light-orange strand and one dark-orange strand together** for the entire piece, keeping the loops around **5 cm / 2 in** long.

1. **Rnd 1:** Make a magic ring and work **6 sc** into it. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** In each of the six stitches, work **one loop stitch followed by one sc into the same stitch**. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 loop stitch, then (1 loop stitch + 1 sc in the same stitch)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *1 loop stitch in each of the next 2 stitches; (loop stitch + sc) in the following stitch* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *3 individual loop stitches, then a loop stitch + sc increase* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *4 individual loop stitches, then a loop stitch + sc increase* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *5 individual loop stitches, then a loop stitch + sc increase* six times. **[42]**

Fasten off, leaving a **long dark-orange tail** to sew with; weave in the light-orange tail. Carefully **cut through the outer tip of each loop** so it becomes two hanging strands. Trim only where necessary—this is meant to be a playful, untamed fringe.

Position the little shaggy cap across the **upper back of the head, spanning approximately Rounds 14–27**, and pin it before sewing. Use the dark-orange tail to anchor the cap around its edge. You can then tease a few strands toward the forehead without hiding the eyes.

## Part 3 — Crochet the two cream horns

Each horn is a slim, gradually widening tube. Work **two in cream yarn** with the 3 mm hook.

1. **Rnd 1:** Work 3 sc into a magic ring. **[3]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in all three stitches. **[6]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Make 6 sc around. **[6]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Crochet 5 sc, then increase in the last stitch. **[7]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Work 7 sc without shaping. **[7]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Crochet 3 sc, increase in the next stitch, then finish with 3 sc. **[8]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Make 8 sc around. **[8]**
8. **Rnd 8:** Work 7 sc and an increase. **[9]**
9. **Rnd 9:** Crochet 9 sc evenly. **[9]**
10. **Rnd 10:** Work 4 sc, inc, then 4 sc. **[10]**
11. **Rnd 11:** Work 10 sc around. **[10]**

Fasten off, leaving enough yarn to sew the horn in place. Make the matching horn before attaching either one.

**Simple, soft-toy finish:** Add a little stuffing, pin both horns beneath the edge of the topknot and sew their bases down. Set the top side of each horn roughly in line with the **back center of the corresponding eye**. For a child-friendly toy, **leave out all internal wire**.

**Optional poseable finish (display only):** The source offers a **30 cm / 12 in white pipe cleaner** for shaping. Fold a piece so its doubled section matches the horn's length, twist the wire to hold it, and keep one end about **1 cm / ½ in longer** to tuck into the head. Slip the twisted section inside the lightly stuffed horn along what will be its uppermost edge, anchor the horn just in front of the topknot, and gently bend the tip upward. **Do not use wire or exposed pipe-cleaner ends in toys intended for children**; a firmly sewn, stuffed horn is the safer option.

## Part 4 — Make the two drooping ears

Use **dark-orange yarn** for both ears. They're simple little shapes, but the flattened seam makes them hang in the right direction.

1. **Rnd 1:** Start with 6 sc in a magic ring. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Repeat *1 sc, inc* three times. **[9]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Work 9 sc evenly. **[9]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *2 sc, inc* three times. **[12]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Work 12 sc. **[12]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *3 sc, inc* three times. **[15]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Crochet 15 sc around. **[15]**
8. **Rnd 8:** Repeat *4 sc, inc* three times. **[18]**
9. **Rnd 9:** Make 18 sc evenly. **[18]**
10. **Rnd 10:** **Flatten the ear opening** so its front and back layers meet. Chain 1 for positioning (this does not count as a stitch), then crochet **9 sc through both layers** to seal the opening. **[9]**

Leave a long tail, **pinch the flat base** and stitch its lower edges into a compact little fold. Make another ear. Sew the sealed seams **horizontally just below the horns** on the sides of the head. A small extra stitch from the **back of each ear into the head** helps keep the ears angled downward rather than sticking out sharply.

## Part 5 — Crochet the shaggy two-tone body

Now it's time for the big, cozy coat! As with the topknot, hold **one strand of dark orange and one of light orange together**, and use **long loop stitches approximately 5 cm / 2 in**. Work the loop stitches so the strands hang to the outside of the growing body.

1. **Rnd 1:** Make 6 sc into a magic ring. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** In each stitch, work *(loop stitch + 1 sc)*, using the same base stitch for both. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 loop stitch, then (loop stitch + sc) in the next stitch* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *2 loop stitches, then (loop stitch + sc) in the next stitch* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *3 loop stitches, then (loop stitch + sc) in the next stitch* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *4 loop stitches, then (loop stitch + sc) in the next stitch* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *5 loop stitches, then (loop stitch + sc) in the next stitch* six times. **[42]**
8. **Rnds 8–9:** Work one loop stitch in **every stitch** for both rounds. **[42]**
9. **Rnd 10:** Repeat *5 loop stitches, dec* six times. **[36]**
10. **Rnds 11–12:** Work one loop stitch in each stitch for two full rounds. **[36]**
11. **Rnd 13:** Repeat *4 loop stitches, dec* six times. **[30]**
12. **Rnds 14–15:** Work a loop stitch into every stitch for two rounds. **[30]**
13. **Rnd 16:** Repeat *3 loop stitches, dec* six times. **[24]**
14. **Rnds 17–18:** Work 24 loop stitches in each round. **[24]**
15. **Rnd 19:** Repeat *2 loop stitches, dec* six times. **[18]**
16. **Rnd 20:** Finish with one loop stitch in each of the 18 stitches. **[18]**

Fasten off, leaving a **long dark-orange sewing strand**. Weave the light-orange strand into the inside of the crochet fabric. **Cut open the long outer loops**, checking as you go that you're only snipping the intentional fringe and not the structural stitch below it. Fill the body with fiberfill, keeping it plush but not distorted.

Sew the **18-stitch neck opening** to the underside of the head. The source sets the **back of that opening approximately two rounds below the rear edge of the topknot**. Pin before stitching, making sure the face points forward and the head is supported. Run the needle around the neck join more than once so Heather's big head sits securely over her fluffy little body.

## Part 6 — Crochet four little legs and shape the hooves

You'll make **four matching legs**, beginning at the beige hoof and finishing with dark-orange upper legs. Each leg is shaped the same way; the different **seam orientation** gives the front and back legs their proper positions.

### Legs (make 4)

Start with **beige yarn**.

1. **Rnd 1:** Make a magic ring with 6 sc. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in each stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 sc, inc* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *2 sc, inc* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Work 24 sc **through the back loops only**. This makes the hoof's edge stand out a little. **[24]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *6 sc, dec* three times. **[21]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *5 sc, dec* three times. **[18]**
8. **Rnd 8:** Repeat *4 sc, dec* three times. **[15]**

Now switch to **dark-orange yarn** for the leg above the hoof.

9. **Rnds 9–18:** Work 15 sc in **each of ten rounds**, keeping the little tube straight. **[15]**

Fasten off with a **long yarn tail**. Stuff each leg, with enough support inside the beige hoof to keep its rounded shape. **Flatten the 15-stitch top edge** and stitch it shut. Make four of these matching pieces, then separate them into a pair of front legs and a pair of back legs.

### Where to sew the front legs

Pin the front two legs to the **front of the body near Rounds 18–19**, with about **four stitches between their upper seams**. Sew each flattened upper seam **horizontally**. To help them stay close against the body, take **one additional stitch through the back of each leg and into the body halfway down the leg**.

### Where to sew the back legs

Attach the remaining two legs at the **sides and back of the body**, using their flattened upper seams **vertically**. Their position should bring the **front of each back hoof near the back of the corresponding front hoof**. A small securing stitch halfway down the **inside** of each rear leg helps keep the seated posture stable.

### Add the split-hoof markings

To give each beige hoof its little cloven-hoof groove, thread a tapestry needle with **black yarn**. Bring the needle up from the **center magic-ring opening at the bottom of the hoof** to the **upper edge of the beige hoof**. Pull gently to create an indentation, then secure the ends beneath the foot and hide them inside. Repeat on all four hooves. Don't tug so hard that you flatten the stuffing or pull the shape sideways.

## Part 7 — Braid the tiny ginger tail

The tail isn't crocheted as a tube; it's a short braid made from the same **dark-orange yarn** as Heather's head and legs. It's a simple, satisfying finishing detail.

1. Cut **six pieces of dark-orange yarn**, each approximately **18 cm / 7 in** long.
2. Find a stitch post at the **lower center back of the body**. Put the hook beneath it, fold all six lengths in half over the hook, and pull the fold underneath that stitch post to make one big loop.
3. Pass the twelve loose yarn ends through this loop and pull gently but firmly to secure the tassel. You now have **12 strands** attached to the body.
4. Divide those twelve strands into **three bundles of four**. Braid the bundles together until the plait is about **5 cm / 2 in long**.
5. Tie the end with another dark-orange strand, trim the loose ends evenly, and fluff the little tassel at the tip.

If the braiding feels fiddly, use clips or small elastic bands to hold the three bundles separated before you begin.

## Part 8 — Assembly checklist and final tidy-up

Heather's charm comes from her expression and her fluffy silhouette. Lay all of the pieces on a table and check your placements before securing the final seams.

1. **Head:** Check the beige muzzle, eye position, nostrils and small stitched smile. Make sure the stuffing supports the rounded face.
2. **Topknot:** Position the cut-loop fringe over the upper head around **Rounds 14–27**, then sew down securely without trapping too much fringe beneath the stitches.
3. **Horns:** Add the two stuffed cream horns immediately below the topknot, one to either side. Poseable pipe-cleaner support is optional and for display only.
4. **Ears:** Sew the flattened dark-orange ears beneath the horns, adding a stabilizing stitch behind each one for that sweet downward tilt.
5. **Body:** Stuff and attach the ginger loop-stitch body beneath the head, with the back of the neck opening just below the edge of the topknot.
6. **Four legs:** Place the front legs with **horizontal** top seams and the back legs with **vertical** top seams. Check their placement from the front and side before sewing.
7. **Hooves:** Shape the clefts in the beige ends using black yarn, then tuck away any exposed ends.
8. **Tail:** Attach the six folded strands at the lower back and braid the twelve ends into a neat, short tassel.
9. **Fringe:** Give the coat and topknot one final look. Use small snips to separate any remaining intact loops, and trim the longest strays very lightly if needed. Your Highland cow should still look wonderfully tousled.

## Little tips for making that shaggy coat look lovely

**My loop stitches keep coming out uneven.** Wrap the yarn around the **same part of your index finger** every time, and check the loops are close to **5 cm / 2 in** long. Practice a few stitches before working the topknot. The two strands should be held together with the same tension.

**The body looks sparse after cutting the loops.** Check whether you've cut open **all** of the outer loops and gently separate the strands with your fingers. Avoid pulling out the root of any loop—only cut at its outer fold. Lightly redistribute the strands to cover small gaps.

**I accidentally cut the crochet underneath.** Stop and examine the stitch structure before continuing. Cut only the **long loops standing away from the fabric**, not any short stitch that holds the rounds together. Work slowly with sharp scissors and protect the base fabric with your fingers.

**My horns won't curve.** Firm stuffing and deliberate sewing can make the tips sit naturally without adding anything rigid. The source also describes a pipe-cleaner option, but leave that out if the toy might be used by a child.

**The front legs keep splaying outward.** Check the **four-stitch separation** and remember the little midway tacking stitch on the back of each front leg. For the back pair, turn the top seams vertically so the hooves sit closer to their front partners.

**The head feels too heavy.** Use a few extra passes of yarn around the neck join, working through sturdy crochet stitches on both pieces, and check the head's position before tightening the stitches.
