Some birds bring sunshine-colored feathers. Others have the most wonderful shades hiding in ordinary grey. **Clover the City Pigeon** is a small, round visitor with pale wings, a perky golden beak and the sort of pink feet that make every little step look important.

Clover is an **original Thread & Petal amigurumi**, built from simple separate pieces: a rounded body, a little head, two soft wings, a gently striped tail and two tiny feet. There is no one-piece chain-extension shaping to navigate. You can move the pieces around until the bird looks just right, then stitch them in place.

**About the cover:** This is a generated crochet-style portrait for illustration, not a photograph of a physically test-crocheted sample. Follow the written stitch counts and assembly notes rather than measuring proportions from the image.

## Before you begin: a quick project guide

- **Method:** US crochet terms throughout. Work in continuous spirals except for the finishing details. Mark the start of each round.
- **Approximate size:** 11–14 cm / 4¼–5½ in tall with DK yarn. The estimate depends on your tension and is not a physically tested gauge.
- **Counting:** **[12]** means you should have 12 stitches at the end of the round. Count before moving your marker.
- **Pieces:** One light-grey body, one slate-grey head, one yellow beak, two pale wings, one striped tail and two coral feet.
- **Safety:** For a toy intended for young children, avoid small hard eyes and detachable details; use securely embroidered features and assess the finished item against applicable toy-safety rules.

## Part 1 — Build Clover's rounded body

Use **dove-grey yarn**. Start at the base and increase into a generous little oval-looking shape. The last rounds become a short neck opening. All rounds are continuous.

1. **Rnd 1:** Crochet 6 sc into an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in each stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *(1 sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *(3 sc, inc)* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *(4 sc, inc)* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *(5 sc, inc)* six times. **[42]**
8. **Rnd 8:** Repeat *(6 sc, inc)* six times. **[48]**
9. **Rnds 9–14:** Work 48 sc in each of six rounds. **[48 each round]**
10. **Rnd 15:** Repeat *(6 sc, dec)* six times. **[42]**
11. **Rnd 16:** Work 42 sc. **[42]**
12. **Rnd 17:** Repeat *(5 sc, dec)* six times. **[36]**
13. **Rnd 18:** Work 36 sc. **[36]**
14. **Rnd 19:** Repeat *(4 sc, dec)* six times. **[30]**
15. **Rnd 20:** Repeat *(3 sc, dec)* six times. **[24]**
16. **Rnd 21:** Repeat *(2 sc, dec)* six times. **[18]**
17. **Rnds 22–23:** Work 18 sc in each round. **[18 each round]**

Stuff the belly steadily, keeping the lower half broad and the upper section firm enough to support the head. Fasten off with a generous tail. **Leave the 18-stitch opening free** for assembly.

> **The pigeon-shape check:** After round 14, the body should be widest at **48 stitches**; by round 21 it has narrowed to **18** for the neck. If it looks like a long tube instead of a plump body, recount the six decreases in each shaping round. Fill the broad lower part in small pieces so it stays gently rounded without stretching the stitches apart.

## Part 2 — Crochet a slate-grey head

Begin at the top using **slate-grey yarn**. The head is a simple sphere, slightly smaller than the body. Make sure the eye positions face the same way as the beak when you sew it on.

1. **Rnd 1:** Work 6 sc in an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase all around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *(1 sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *(3 sc, inc)* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *(4 sc, inc)* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnds 7–11:** Work 36 sc in each of five rounds. **[36 each round]**

If using decorative plastic eyes, position them **between rounds 8 and 9** on the front, approximately eight stitches apart. Check the beak's future position before securing the washers. Embroidered eyes can be added after assembly.

> **Give Clover a curious glance:** Temporarily place the beak between the eyes and look at the face from the front *and* in profile. A head that looks even from above may still have one eye too far back. Mark both positions before fastening anything. For a toy for a very young child, choose securely embroidered eyes instead of plastic attachments.

8. **Rnd 12:** Repeat *(4 sc, dec)* six times. **[30]**
9. **Rnd 13:** Repeat *(3 sc, dec)* six times. **[24]**

Fill the head with small tufts of stuffing, keeping its front smooth.

10. **Rnd 14:** Repeat *(2 sc, dec)* six times. **[18]**
11. **Rnd 15:** Repeat *(1 sc, dec)* six times. **[12]**
12. **Rnd 16:** Decrease six times. **[6]**

Draw a sewing tail through the front loops of the last six stitches, close the opening and bury the tail inside. The completed underside sits just inside the body's neck opening.

## Part 3 — A tiny mustard-gold beak

For Clover, a modest little beak says plenty. Use **mustard-yellow yarn**, and keep the tube short so it doesn't become a long bill.

1. **Rnd 1:** Work 4 sc into an MR. **[4]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Work 4 sc. **[4]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *(1 sc, inc)* twice. **[6]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Work 6 sc. **[6]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *(2 sc, inc)* twice. **[8]**

Flatten the open end and work **4 sc through both layers**. **[4 closing stitches]** Fasten off with a long tail. Keep this piece unstuffed for a neat, softly flattened tip.

## Part 4 — Two rounded silver-grey wings

Make **two** with **pale silver yarn**. The round starting end becomes the wing's soft outer tip, while its flattened open edge is the attachment point.

1. **Rnd 1:** Make 6 sc in an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in each stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *(1 sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnds 5–6:** Work 24 sc in each round. **[24 each round]**
6. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *(2 sc, dec)* six times. **[18]**
7. **Rnd 8:** Work 18 sc. **[18]**

Do not stuff the wings. Flatten each piece and crochet **9 sc through both layers** of its opening. **[9 closing stitches]** Leave long tails for attaching. They should lie like two plump, flat leaves against the body.

> **Two wings, one silhouette:** Count the **nine closing stitches** on each wing and hold them side by side. If one piece looks fuller, check rounds 5–8 before sewing. On the body, pin them at equal heights, then let the lower sections drape rather than stretching the fabric to force a perfect mirror image.

If you like the texture in the portrait, you can embroider one or two shallow little feather lines on the wings after attachment using a matching shade of grey.

## Part 5 — Make one softly striped tail

Use **slate grey** for the first rounds, **pale silver** for the middle stripe, then return to slate grey. The tail is one small stuffed-free piece rather than a fan of many feathers.

1. **Rnd 1:** With slate grey, crochet 6 sc in an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase all around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *(1 sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnds 4–5:** Work 18 sc in each round. **[18 each round]**
5. **Rnd 6:** Change to pale silver and make 18 sc. **[18]**
6. **Rnd 7:** Work 18 sc in pale silver. **[18]**
7. **Rnd 8:** Change back to slate grey and work 18 sc. **[18]**
8. **Rnd 9:** Repeat *(1 sc, dec)* six times. **[12]**

Flatten the open end. Work **6 sc through both layers** to close. **[6 closing stitches]** Leave a long sewing tail and keep the tail unstuffed. The little stripes will show nicely when it points upward at the back.

> **A tiny color-change trick:** To make the silver stripe start neatly, complete the *last pull-through* of the stitch just before round 6 with silver. Do the same with slate grey before round 8. In a continuous spiral, the join can still step slightly upward—that's a normal feature of spiral rounds, not a reason to add an extra stitch.

## Part 6 — Crochet two rosy feet

These feet start as very short tubes, with three little chain toes worked into their last round. Make **two** in **soft coral yarn**.

1. **Rnd 1:** Work 6 sc into an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnds 2–4:** Crochet 6 sc in each round. **[6 each round]**
3. **Rnd 5 — make the toes:** Repeat the following group three times: **1 sc, ch 3, sl st in the second chain from the hook, sl st in the next chain, then 1 sc in the next stitch of the round.** **[6 sc with 3 small chain toes]**

Fasten off and weave the ending yarn tail securely into the foot. Keep the **starting tail** available for attaching the foot. Leave both feet unstuffed. The three little chain branches become Clover's toes; angle them toward the front during sewing.

## Part 7 — Give Clover a cheerful expression

Make sure the body has been filled and the head is complete. Set everything on the table before sewing. Our [assembly lesson](/learn/sew-amigurumi-pieces-together/) has more help with positioning.

**Before the first seam:** Check that you have a body with an **18-stitch open top**, a **fully closed head**, a four-stitch-seam beak, **two** nine-stitch-seam wings, a six-stitch-seam tail and **two** little feet. The head's closed base sits *inside* the body's open neckline, so you are sewing the surrounding fabric securely—not trying to match an 18-stitch head opening that doesn't exist.

1. **Seat the head.** Slip the closed underside of the head slightly into the **18-stitch opening** at the top of the body. Sew all the way around, catching the top body edge and lower head stitches. Keep the beak-facing side over the front of the belly.
2. **Place the beak.** Sew the four-stitch flattened base of the yellow beak to the face, centered below the eyes. Keep the narrow starting tip pointing forward.
3. **Pin the wings.** Position each pale wing high on the sides of the body, with its rounded outer tip angled toward the tail. Stitch along the nine-stitch flattened edge and tack the underside once if needed, leaving most of the wing visible.
4. **Angle the tail.** Sew its six-stitch closing edge to the upper back. A slight upward angle gives Clover a perky silhouette.
5. **Attach the feet.** Using the starting yarn tails, attach the two small coral legs at the underside toward the front. Leave a small gap between them and point the chain toes forward. Test the bird's balance before tightening the joins.
6. **Add the last details.** Embroider any remaining eyes, or add two tiny optional **lilac and sage surface stitches** along the lower sides of the neck to suggest a pigeon's iridescent sheen. Secure every thread on the inside.

## A few useful troubleshooting notes

- **The pigeon tips backward:** The tail may be angled too low or the feet may be attached too far toward the center. Pin and test their positions before resewing.
- **The head wobbles after joining:** Check whether the closed head base really sits inside the 18-stitch body opening. Add a second round of small securing stitches through the surrounding head fabric; don't pull the neck opening so tightly that it puckers.
- **A pale stripe has a little step:** The tail is made in a continuous spiral, so the change between rounds may not line up perfectly. Check that you still have 18 stitches through round 8 before deciding to undo the color change.
- **The wings look bulky:** Keep them unstuffed. The gently flattened ovals should lie against the round body, not stick out like little balloons.
- **The beak disappears:** Position its *wide* four-stitch edge against the head, leaving the narrow starting tip free.
- **The stripe looks uneven:** Make your color change during the last pull-through of the previous stitch; our [color-change guide](/blog/neater-amigurumi-color-changes/) explains the technique.
- **The two feet point different ways:** Align the three chain toes toward the same front centerline before securing each leg.
