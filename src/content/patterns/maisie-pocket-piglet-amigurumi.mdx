Maisie is a little piglet who believes the best days are the slow ones: a sunny patch of grass, a good nap, and perhaps a strawberry to investigate. Her rounded body, floppy ears, and soft oval snout turn a handful of pink yarn into a cheerful farmyard friend.

This is an **original Thread & Petal design** for a friendly piglet, not a reproduction of the *Zoomigurumi 6* piglet's shaping instructions. The image on this page is **an original illustration, not a photograph of a finished crochet sample**. The stitch counts have been checked, but Maisie has **not yet been physically test-crocheted**, so pin your pieces together and adjust the placement before sewing.

## Before you start

You'll crochet a **separate head and body**, an oval snout, two folded ears, four small trotters, and a curly tail. Most pieces use only single crochet, increases, and decreases. If those stitches are new, visit [our increases and decreases lesson](/learn/crochet-increases-and-decreases/) and [the magic ring guide](/learn/magic-ring-crochet-in-the-round/).

**US crochet abbreviations:** **MR** = magic ring; **sc** = single crochet; **inc** = 2 sc in one stitch; **dec** = decrease two stitches into one; **ch** = chain; **sl st** = slip stitch; **FO** = fasten off. Counts appear in **[brackets]**. Work continuously in a spiral unless you are instructed to turn your work.

**A little safety note:** Embroider all facial details if this is intended for a small child. Avoid plastic safety eyes, removable decorations, or loose ribbons for babies and toddlers.

## Part 1 — Crochet Maisie's round head

Use your main **blush-pink yarn**, starting at the top of the head.

1. **Rnd 1:** Work 6 sc into a magic ring. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Inc in every stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *(sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *(3 sc, inc)* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *(4 sc, inc)* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *(5 sc, inc)* six times. **[42]**
8. **Rnd 8:** Repeat *(6 sc, inc)* six times. **[48]**
9. **Rnd 9–15:** Sc around for seven complete rounds. **[48 per round]**
10. **Rnd 16:** Repeat *(6 sc, dec)* six times. **[42]**
11. **Rnd 17:** Repeat *(5 sc, dec)* six times. **[36]**

**Face checkpoint:** Mark the center front. Embroider two small dark eyes around **Rnd 12**, approximately **10 stitches apart**, and check how the expression looks with the snout pinned below them. You can wait to embroider the eyes until assembly if that feels easier.

12. **Rnd 18:** Repeat *(4 sc, dec)* six times. **[30]**
13. **Rnd 19:** Repeat *(3 sc, dec)* six times. **[24]**

Add small handfuls of fiberfill. Shape the sides gently to keep the head round, without stretching your stitch gaps.

14. **Rnd 20:** Repeat *(2 sc, dec)* six times. **[18]**
15. **Rnd 21:** Repeat *(sc, dec)* six times. **[12]**
16. **Rnd 22:** Work six decreases. **[6]**

FO and use the tail to gather the remaining front loops, closing the hole. Hide the end inside.

**Thread & Petal tip:** If the cheeks seem too flat, add a little stuffing near the sides before closing. If stitches gape, try a slightly smaller hook rather than packing in more fiberfill. Our [amigurumi stuffing guide](/blog/the-little-art-of-stuffing-amigurumi/) walks through this in more detail.

## Part 2 — An adorable oval snout

We'll make a **small oval from a foundation chain** with rose-pink yarn. It's separate from the head, which makes the nostrils easy to embroider.

1. **Foundation:** Ch 5.
2. **Rnd 1:** Starting in the second chain from the hook, make **2 sc** in that chain, **sc in each of the next 2 chains**, and **3 sc in the last chain**. Turn the work to crochet along the underside: **sc in the next 2 underside loops**, then **1 sc in the remaining underside loop**. **[10]**
3. **Rnd 2:** Repeat *(inc, 3 sc)* twice. **[12]**
4. **Rnd 3:** Sc around. **[12]**
5. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *(inc, 5 sc)* twice. **[14]**
6. **Rnd 5:** Sc around. **[14]**

FO with a long sewing tail. Add just a little fiberfill so the snout stays softly raised. Pin the oval **horizontally** below the eyes, around the center front of Rnds 13–17. The snout should cover the center of the face without hiding the eyes.

With dark rose yarn, embroider **two tiny upright oval nostrils** on the front of the snout. Keep them close to the center and use only a few stitches for each. Add a small smile below one side of the snout if you like.

**Count-check corner:** The foundation oval ends with **2 + 2 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 10 stitches**. If it twists, flatten it in your hand before continuing; [our oval-crochet lesson](/learn/crocheting-ovals-and-flat-bases/) is helpful here.

## Part 3 — Crochet the plump little body

Start at the **bottom** in your main pink yarn. The wider base makes a lovely pear-shaped seated body.

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Inc all around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** *(sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** *(3 sc, inc)* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** *(4 sc, inc)* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** *(5 sc, inc)* six times. **[42]**
8. **Rnd 8–12:** Sc around for five rounds. **[42 per round]**
9. **Rnd 13:** *(5 sc, dec)* six times. **[36]**
10. **Rnd 14–15:** Sc around. **[36 per round]**
11. **Rnd 16:** *(4 sc, dec)* six times. **[30]**
12. **Rnd 17:** Sc around. **[30]**
13. **Rnd 18:** *(3 sc, dec)* six times. **[24]**
14. **Rnd 19:** Sc around. **[24]**

FO leaving a long sewing tail. **Do not close the top opening.** Stuff the bottom slightly more firmly than the neck, then press the base onto a flat table to check the shape.

**How should it look?** Think rounded at the belly and gently narrowing at the shoulders. A body that's perfectly spherical can make the head look a little top-heavy; the tapered top helps keep Maisie balanced.

## Part 4 — Make two soft, folded piglet ears

With your main pink yarn, make **two matching ears**.

1. **Rnd 1:** 5 sc in MR. **[5]**
2. **Rnd 2:** *(sc, inc)* twice, sc in the last stitch. **[7]**
3. **Rnd 3:** *(2 sc, inc)* twice, sc in the last stitch. **[9]**
4. **Rnd 4:** *(2 sc, inc)* three times. **[12]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Sc around. **[12]**
6. **Rnd 6:** *(3 sc, inc)* three times. **[15]**
7. **Rnd 7–8:** Sc around. **[15 per round]**

FO with long tails and **do not stuff**. Flatten the ears. Fold one corner of the open base toward the center to give each ear that lovely floppy-piglet shape; use a couple of small stitches to hold the crease.

Pin both ears near the top of the head, about **Rnds 5–7**, with room between them. Angle them slightly outward. Sew only after checking them from the front and side.

For a little color, use a few dusty-pink satin stitches along the inner ear rather than adding a separate layer. Want help making facial embroidery tidy? [Read our embroidered-amigurumi-face lesson](/learn/embroider-amigurumi-faces/).

## Part 5 — Four tiny trotters

Make **four** identical feet in main pink. These are small rounded ovals that sit beneath the body.

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Inc in every stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3–4:** Sc around. **[12 per round]**
4. **Rnd 5:** *(2 sc, dec)* three times. **[9]**
5. **Rnd 6:** Sc around. **[9]**

FO with a long tail for each foot. Lightly stuff **only the rounded toe ends**, leaving the upper openings soft enough to press against the body.

You can embroider a tiny split-hoof detail with darker rose yarn: one short vertical stitch at the toe of each foot is plenty.

**Placement tip:** Pin the front two trotters beneath the chest and the rear pair farther back. Sit Maisie on the table before sewing; all four should touch the surface without tilting her to one side.

## Part 6 — The little curly piglet tail

For an easy springy curl, make a **short chain cord** rather than a stuffed tube.

1. With main pink, **ch 15**.
2. Starting in the **second chain from the hook**, work **2 sc into each of the 14 chains**. **[28 sc]**
3. FO leaving a long sewing tail.

The increase in every chain encourages the strip to spiral. Curl it gently around your finger if necessary. Sew the starting end securely to the **back lower third** of the body, and tuck the finishing yarn end inside the tail or body.

**Tiny tail, big personality:** The curl may come out tighter or looser depending on yarn. Don't pull it hard to force a spiral; simply arrange it and stitch it in place. For a toy intended for very young children, consider replacing the curly projection with a small embroidered swirl.

## Part 7 — Assemble your little farmyard friend

1. **Check the face first.** Pin the oval snout below the eyes, and tilt it very slightly upward for a curious expression. Sew around the edge, adding a touch of stuffing before closing the final gap.
2. **Attach the ears.** Fold and pin them on the upper head, then sew the bases securely. The tips can be soft and floppy.
3. **Join head to body.** Place the closed head on the open 24-stitch neck and sew evenly around with the body tail. Add a little more neck stuffing before closing the last gap so the head doesn't wobble.
4. **Sew on four feet.** Place them at even heights around the lower body. Set the piglet on a table and reposition until she looks balanced from both sides.
5. **Add the curled tail.** Sew it to the lower back, away from the neck seam.
6. **Check every attachment.** Tug gently on each foot and ear, hide all yarn ends, and make sure nothing is loose.

If joining tiny parts is new for you, start with [how to sew amigurumi pieces together](/learn/sew-amigurumi-pieces-together/).

## Troubleshooting — little piglet, little fixes

| What happened? | A gentle fix |
| --- | --- |
| Snout looks too tall | Use less stuffing and flatten the oval before pinning |
| One ear curls more than the other | Make sure both ears have seven rounds after the first two increase rounds, then crease them the same way |
| Piglet tips forward | Move the front feet slightly forward, shift the head back a touch, and adjust the base stuffing |
| A foot looks larger | Check Rnd 5: three repeats should reduce 12 stitches to **9** |
| The tail won't curl | Add one extra sc in a few of the chains or switch to a smaller hook for the tail alone |
| Seams show against the pink body | Sew using the same color yarn and keep the tension firm but not puckered |

For more on shapes that surprise you, read [why your amigurumi looks different](/blog/why-your-amigurumi-looks-different/). A tiny change of yarn, hook, or stuffing can make your piglet look completely different—and still absolutely charming.

## A little more Maisie magic

Try making Maisie in warm peach, dusty rose, or creamy blush. A slightly darker snout adds contrast, while a matching snout gives a gentle one-color look. You could even stitch a small heart on her back to make a sweet handmade gift.

The nicest finishing touch is simple: **let the ears be a little uneven and the tail have its own spring**. Those quiet handmade details are what make each piglet yours.
