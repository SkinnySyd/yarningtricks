Meet **Puddlewick**, a gentle mallard with an olive-green head, a slim golden bill and a soft rose-brown body. His ivory wing rests against his side, his tail tips upward and his rusty feet peek out beneath him. This new color palette and silhouette follow the finished-project illustration shown above.

This is an **independently drafted Thread & Petal pattern**, using a horizontal stuffed body, a separate open-neck head, and separately made wings, bill, tail and feet. It is **not** the one-piece head-and-body construction used in the reference book.

**A note about the images and instructions:** The cover and the small detail images are **crops of one generated styling illustration**. They are helpful for color, silhouette and placement, **not photographs of individual crochet steps**. The instructions and stitch totals are original and have been checked on paper, but **this version has not been physically test-crocheted**. Treat measurements and exact proportions as approximate; follow the written counts when the illustration and stitches differ.

## Before you begin — a little project map

- **US terminology:** Work in continuous rounds unless a section explicitly says *row*. Use a stitch marker to identify the start of each round.
- **Approximate size:** About 12–15 cm (4¾–6 in) tall with DK yarn. This is an estimate, not a measured sample.
- **Body orientation:** Work the body **from the narrow rear toward the rounded front**, then turn it sideways before attaching the upright head. The small tail goes at the starting end.
- **Construction:** One horizontal brown body, one green head with an open ivory neckline, a tapered bill, two flattened ivory wings, one pointed tail, two flat webbed feet and two short ankle tubes.
- **Finish carefully:** For a child’s toy, avoid detachable small parts, embroider features securely, use age-appropriate materials and check relevant toy-safety requirements. A handmade toy is not automatically safety-certified.

### Stitches & abbreviations

| Abbreviation | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **MR** | Magic ring |
| **sc** | Single crochet |
| **inc** | Two sc in the next stitch |
| **dec** | One sc decrease across the next two stitches |
| **ch** | Chain |
| **sl st** | Slip stitch |
| **rnd / row** | Spiral round / turned flat row |

Numbers in **[brackets]** give the stitch count at the end of that round or row. For example, **(5 sc, inc) × 6** works six increases spaced evenly around the round. Review the [magic ring](/learn/magic-ring-crochet-in-the-round/), [single crochet](/learn/single-crochet-stitch/) and [increases and decreases](/learn/crochet-increases-and-decreases/) if you need a refresher.

## Part 1 — Make the long, softly rounded body

Work in **dusty rose-brown**. Instead of starting beneath the belly, make the body horizontally, beginning at the **back**, where the small tail will sit. The widest section will form the plump middle; the closing rounds will become the **front** of the duck.

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Inc in each stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** (1 sc, inc) × 6. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** (2 sc, inc) × 6. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** (3 sc, inc) × 6. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** (4 sc, inc) × 6. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** (5 sc, inc) × 6. **[42]**
8. **Rnd 8:** (6 sc, inc) × 6. **[48]**
9. **Rnds 9–17:** Sc in every stitch for nine rounds. **[48 each round]**
10. **Rnd 18:** (6 sc, dec) × 6. **[42]**
11. **Rnds 19–21:** Sc in every stitch for three rounds. **[42 each round]**
12. **Rnd 22:** (5 sc, dec) × 6. **[36]**
13. **Rnds 23–25:** Sc in every stitch for three rounds. **[36 each round]**
14. **Rnd 26:** (4 sc, dec) × 6. **[30]**
15. **Rnd 27:** Sc in every stitch. **[30]**
16. **Rnd 28:** (3 sc, dec) × 6. **[24]**
17. **Rnd 29:** (2 sc, dec) × 6. **[18]**

Stuff in small wisps as the opening becomes narrow. Keep the underside softly rounded so the body will sit rather than roll.

> **A little shaping secret:** Stuffing does more than fill a shape—it decides how the toy sits. Before the last two decreases, rest the body sideways on a table. If it rocks, gently move a little filling away from the underside and towards the sides rather than packing the bottom harder. You can still smooth the finished piece with your fingers after closing.

18. **Rnd 30:** (1 sc, dec) × 6. **[12]**
19. **Rnd 31:** Dec × 6. **[6]**

Cut the yarn, draw it through the **front loops of the final six stitches** and close the hole. This closing end is the **front**, below the head and bill. Gently massage the stuffing until the long body feels even. When you set it on the table, choose the flattest side as its underside.

## Part 2 — Crochet the olive-green head and ivory collar

Begin at the **top of the head** in soft olive-green yarn. Leave the lower edge open so it can sit securely over the upper front of the body.

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Inc in every stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** (1 sc, inc) × 6. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** (2 sc, inc) × 6. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** (3 sc, inc) × 6. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** (4 sc, inc) × 6. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** (5 sc, inc) × 6. **[42]**
8. **Rnds 8–14:** Sc in every stitch for seven rounds. **[42 each round]**

**Eye placement:** While you can still reach the inside, choose the face's front and position display-only safety eyes approximately **between rounds 11 and 12**, around **10–12 stitches apart** when viewed from the front. Check the bill placement before fastening them. Alternatively, use embroidered eyes after assembly.

9. **Rnd 15:** (5 sc, dec) × 6. **[36]**
10. **Rnd 16:** Sc in every stitch. **[36]**
11. **Rnd 17:** (4 sc, dec) × 6. **[30]**
12. **Change to ivory for Rnds 18–19:** Sc in every stitch for two rounds. **[30 each round]**

Fasten off with a **long sewing tail**. Leave the **30-stitch ivory opening unclosed**. Fill the head lightly enough that its green dome keeps its shape, but the ivory rim still sits flat when pinned over the brown body.

> **Why the little ivory ring matters:** The collar is only two rounds tall, so a lumpy join can hide it. Before sewing, pin the rim in four places—front, back, left and right—then add pins between them. Keep the sewing tension even so the cream band remains visible from both sides. If you want a cleaner color change, pick up the ivory yarn on the last pull-through of the final green stitch in round 17.

## Part 3 — Make the long, pale-gold bill

Use **pale mustard yellow**. Crochet from the **narrow tip toward the wider attachment edge** to make a soft tapered bill; this is a different construction from a flat chain-oval bill.

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Sc in all six stitches. **[6]**
3. **Rnd 3:** (1 sc, inc) × 3. **[9]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Sc in every stitch. **[9]**
5. **Rnd 5:** (2 sc, inc) × 3. **[12]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Sc in every stitch. **[12]**
7. **Rnd 7:** (3 sc, inc) × 3. **[15]**
8. **Rnd 8:** Sc in every stitch. **[15]**
9. **Rnd 9:** (4 sc, inc) × 3. **[18]**
10. **Rnds 10–11:** Sc in each stitch for two rounds. **[18 each round]**

Stuff the bill **very lightly**, especially near the narrow tip. Flatten its 18-stitch opening and close it with **9 sc through both layers**. **[9 closing stitches]** Leave a sewing tail. You'll sew the wider edge to the side/front of the green head, so the narrow end points outward.

> **The difference between a bill and a cone:** The increases create a taper, but the final flattening makes it look like a duck's bill. Press it lightly between your fingers *before* closing the nine-stitch edge. If the tip looks like a round nose, take out some filling rather than adding extra stitches. Pin the bill and check the side view before sewing.

## Part 4 — Make two soft ivory wings

The new mallard's wings are **ivory**, not taupe. They are little padded ovals, laid flat against the brown sides. Work **two identical pieces**.

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Inc in every stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** (1 sc, inc) × 6. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** (2 sc, inc) × 6. **[24]**
5. **Rnds 5–6:** Sc in every stitch. **[24 each round]**
6. **Rnd 7:** (2 sc, dec) × 6. **[18]**
7. **Rnd 8:** Sc in every stitch. **[18]**
8. **Rnd 9:** (1 sc, dec) × 6. **[12]**
9. **Rnd 10:** Sc in every stitch. **[12]**

Do **not stuff**. Flatten the 12-stitch opening and close with **6 sc through both layers**. **[6 closing stitches]** Leave long sewing tails. Smooth each wing into a soft, oval shape before attaching it.

> **Make the wings a matching pair:** Put both flat pieces side by side before you sew. Their six-stitch seams should face the same direction and their rounded ends should match. A single pin through the center of each wing lets you check symmetry on the body without committing to a seam too soon.

## Part 5 — Add a little swept-up tail

The tail is a short, unstuffed **pointed cone** made in a deeper rose-brown shade. Begin at its tip.

1. **Rnd 1:** 4 sc in an MR. **[4]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Sc in each stitch. **[4]**
3. **Rnd 3:** (1 sc, inc) × 2. **[6]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Sc in each stitch. **[6]**
5. **Rnd 5:** (2 sc, inc) × 2. **[8]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Sc in each stitch. **[8]**
7. **Rnd 7:** (3 sc, inc) × 2. **[10]**
8. **Rnd 8:** Sc in each stitch. **[10]**

Flatten the opening and close with **5 sc through both layers**. **[5 closing stitches]** Leave a sewing tail. Pin it at the narrow back end of the body, with the tip angled upward. Sew the flattened base firmly; don't pack the tiny tail with stuffing.

## Part 6 — Two rusty webbed feet and short ankles

In the reference picture, a **little rust-colored ankle** rises from each foot. Make two feet and two ankle tubes in rust-orange yarn. These parts are worked separately so you can check the sitting angle before sewing.

### Make two flat feet

1. **Ch 7. Row 1:** Sc in the second chain from the hook and across. **[6]**
2. **Row 2:** Ch 1, turn. Inc in the first stitch, sc in the next 4 stitches, inc in the last. **[8]**
3. **Row 3:** Ch 1, turn. Sc across. **[8]**
4. **Row 4 — tiny toe loops:** Ch 1, turn. Sc in the first stitch. Then repeat **(sc in each of the next 2 stitches, ch 2, sl st in the second chain from hook)** three times. Sc in the last stitch. **[8 sc plus 3 short toe loops]**

Fasten off with a long tail. The three toe loops should point toward the **front** of the mallard. They are short decorations, not three separately stuffed toes.

### Make two short ankle tubes

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnds 2–5:** Sc in each stitch for four rounds. **[6 each round]**
3. **Rnd 6:** (1 sc, inc) × 3. **[9]**
4. **Rnd 7:** Sc in each stitch. **[9]**

Fasten off with a long tail. Leave the wider **nine-stitch opening** unclosed to sew under the body. Sew the narrow, closed end to the **back of a foot** with a separate yarn strand. The top of each ankle should bend slightly forward when you place the bird on a flat table. Make the second pair to match.

> **A softer sitting pose:** The feet are small decorations on a rounded yarn body, not rigid supports. Set the body on its own first and find where it rests naturally; then pin each ankle so the toes peek out underneath. If the body rocks, adjust its filling and resting angle before moving the feet farther apart.

## Part 7 — Assemble Puddlewick, one gentle stitch at a time

Before sewing, place the brown body horizontally with its **starting-round end at the back** and the closed final rounds at the front. Pin every piece in place; view the mallard from the side before fastening anything permanently. Our [sewing-amigurumi lesson](/learn/sew-amigurumi-pieces-together/) has more help with neat joins.

1. **Head and collar:** Position the **open ivory rim** on the **upper-front side** of the brown body, approximately over body rounds **19–26**. The green head should stand fairly upright, while its lower front curves toward the body. Pin the rim all the way around and sew it securely. This is an approximate placement guide: adjust a stitch or two to fit your yarn and head shape.
2. **Bill:** Pin the **nine-stitch flattened attachment edge** to the front of the green face, below and between the eyes. Check that the bill points forward when viewed in profile, and sew around the attachment so it doesn't tilt sideways.
3. **Ivory wings:** Position one on each side of the broad brown body, with the larger rounded end toward the front. Sew the upper/narrow edge to the body, and add a small anchoring stitch near the lower edge if a wing sticks out too far.
4. **Tail:** Place the small tail on the **narrow back end**, centered and tipped up. Sew the five-stitch base securely.
5. **Feet:** Hold the body on a level table. Pin the **two ankle openings** underneath the front half of the body, evenly spaced. Keep the feet facing forward and the short ankles slightly angled. Adjust until Puddlewick can sit, then sew the ankle openings all the way around.
6. **Finishing touches:** If you are using embroidered eyes, add them symmetrically now. Check the head seam and every other attachment; hide yarn ends securely and remove all temporary markers.

## A few small troubleshooting notes

- **The head tips to one side:** Re-pin the ivory collar rim so the head balances over the upper front of the body. A secure, even seam matters more than adding extra stuffing.
- **The body looks like a standing ball:** Check that you crocheted the rounds in order from **back to front**, and orient the longer stuffed form horizontally before attaching the head.
- **The bill is too round:** Remove a small amount of stuffing and gently flatten the shape from top to bottom before sewing.
- **The ivory collar disappears beneath the head:** The sewing yarn may be pulling the two cream rounds underneath. Loosen the seam, re-pin the 30-stitch rim at four evenly spaced points, and resew with small stitches that don't squash the band.
- **The tail keeps pointing sideways:** Turn its five-stitch closing seam so it lies flat against the center of the back end. Secure both corners of the base; avoid pulling only from one side.
- **One wing looks higher:** Count from a shared point on the body and re-pin both wings before sewing. Their attachment positions should mirror each other.
- **The feet won't support the bird:** Change their angle or spacing before sewing the ankle tubes firmly in place. Our [balance guide](/blog/why-crochet-animal-wont-sit-straight/) explains why small placement changes help.
- **Stuffing peeks through the stitches:** Adjust your hook for firmer fabric or reduce the amount of filling. See [The Little Art of Stuffing Amigurumi](/blog/the-little-art-of-stuffing-amigurumi/).
