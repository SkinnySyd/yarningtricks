A small mallard looks as though someone dipped its head in deep green paint, tied a little cream scarf around its neck and sent it out to enjoy a quiet morning on the pond. Meet **Puddlewick**, a round, friendly crochet duck with soft wings and tiny orange feet.

This **original Thread & Petal design** uses a separately crocheted head and body. That makes the bird easier to position before the final stitches, with no complicated openings or chain extensions in the body. Each piece is worked slowly, then sewn together at the end.

**A note about the picture:** The cover is a generated styling illustration, not a photograph of a physically test-crocheted sample. Shape and proportions may vary with your yarn and tension. The directions and stitch counts below describe the intended construction.

## Before you begin: a quick project guide

- **Terminology:** US crochet terms. Work in continuous spiral rounds unless a row is specifically mentioned.
- **Size:** Approximately 12–15 cm / 4¾–6 in tall with DK yarn, depending on tension and stuffing. This is an estimate, not a tested gauge measurement.
- **Counting:** The number in **[square brackets]** is the stitch count at the end of that round or row. Move a stitch marker at each new round.
- **Construction:** One brown body with an ivory collar, one green head, one mustard bill, two taupe wings, a small tail and two flat orange feet.
- **Safety:** Plastic safety eyes and loosely attached parts are not suitable for babies or young children. Use securely embroidered details when appropriate, secure every seam and check applicable toy-safety requirements.

### Crochet abbreviations

| Short form | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **MR** | Magic ring |
| **ch** | Chain |
| **sc** | Single crochet |
| **sl st** | Slip stitch |
| **inc** | Work 2 sc into the next stitch |
| **dec** | Single-crochet decrease over the next 2 stitches |
| **rnd / row** | A continuous round / a turned flat row |

A sequence such as **(4 sc, inc) × 6** means work four normal single crochets and then one increase, repeating six times. Need help? Visit [single crochet](/learn/single-crochet-stitch/), [the magic ring](/learn/magic-ring-crochet-in-the-round/) or [increases and decreases](/learn/crochet-increases-and-decreases/).

## Part 1 — Make the plump cinnamon body

Begin at the bottom with **cinnamon-brown yarn**. The body grows into a broad oval-looking ball, then narrows into a short neck. The final two ivory rounds become Puddlewick's little collar.

1. **Rnd 1:** Work 6 sc into an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in every stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *(1 sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *(3 sc, inc)* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *(4 sc, inc)* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *(5 sc, inc)* six times. **[42]**
8. **Rnd 8:** Repeat *(6 sc, inc)* six times. **[48]**
9. **Rnd 9:** Repeat *(7 sc, inc)* six times. **[54]**
10. **Rnds 10–15:** Work one sc into each stitch for six rounds. **[54 each round]**
11. **Rnd 16:** Repeat *(7 sc, dec)* six times. **[48]**
12. **Rnd 17:** Work 48 sc. **[48]**
13. **Rnd 18:** Repeat *(6 sc, dec)* six times. **[42]**
14. **Rnd 19:** Work 42 sc. **[42]**
15. **Rnd 20:** Repeat *(5 sc, dec)* six times. **[36]**
16. **Rnd 21:** Work 36 sc. **[36]**
17. **Rnd 22:** Repeat *(4 sc, dec)* six times. **[30]**
18. **Rnd 23:** Repeat *(3 sc, dec)* six times. **[24]**
19. **Rnd 24:** Repeat *(2 sc, dec)* six times. **[18]**

Fill the lower body in small wisps. Keep the top opening easy to access; you can add a little more stuffing after checking the shape. Change to **ivory** for the collar.

20. **Rnds 25–26:** Work 18 sc in each of the next two rounds. **[18 each round]**

Fasten off with a long sewing tail and **leave the 18-stitch opening unclosed**. The green head will be sewn over it.

## Part 2 — Crochet the forest-green head

Make a separate round head in **forest-green yarn**. Begin at the crown, where your magic ring will eventually be hidden in the smooth green dome.

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in every stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *(1 sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *(3 sc, inc)* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *(4 sc, inc)* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnds 7–12:** Make 36 sc in each of six rounds. **[36 each round]**

For display-only plastic eyes, check the placement now, before closing the head. Try the eyes **between rounds 9 and 10, roughly nine stitches apart**, centering them across the front. Temporarily pin the bill first if you need help finding the front. Alternatively, embroider the eyes at the end.

8. **Rnd 13:** Repeat *(4 sc, dec)* six times. **[30]**
9. **Rnd 14:** Repeat *(3 sc, dec)* six times. **[24]**

Stuff the head evenly, with a little less stuffing around the final decreases.

10. **Rnd 15:** Repeat *(2 sc, dec)* six times. **[18]**
11. **Rnd 16:** Repeat *(1 sc, dec)* six times. **[12]**
12. **Rnd 17:** Decrease six times. **[6]**

Cut the yarn, thread the tail through the **front loops of the final six stitches**, draw the small hole closed and hide the end. This closed underside will sit inside the ivory collar's opening when the head is attached.

## Part 3 — Give Puddlewick a golden bill

Use **mustard-yellow yarn**. Instead of a long tube, the bill is a short oval worked around a foundation chain and flattened into a cheerful little paddle.

1. **Ch 5.** Turn the chain so you can work around both its sides.
2. **Rnd 1:** Starting in the second chain from the hook, work 3 sc along the chain. Work 3 sc in the end chain. Continue along the underside with 2 sc, then work 2 sc into the chain where you began. **[10]**
3. **Rnd 2:** Repeat *(4 sc, inc)* twice. **[12]**
4. **Rnd 3:** Work 12 sc. **[12]**

Flatten the oval so its long edge looks like a little duck bill. Work **6 sc through both layers** across the open edge. **[6 closing stitches]** Leave a sewing tail. You can add a tiny wisp of stuffing before closing if you want a fuller bill, but keep it soft enough to lie against the face.

## Part 4 — Make two rounded wings

Use **warm taupe yarn** for the wings. Each one begins with a soft round tip and narrows into an attachment edge. Make **two**.

1. **Rnd 1:** Make 6 sc in an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in every stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *(1 sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Work 18 sc. **[18]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *(4 sc, dec)* three times. **[15]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Work 15 sc. **[15]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *(3 sc, dec)* three times. **[12]**
8. **Rnd 8:** Work 12 sc. **[12]**

Flatten the narrow opening and make **6 sc through both layers**. **[6 closing stitches]** Fasten off with a long tail. Leave the wings unstuffed for a soft, flattened shape, or use only a tiny wisp near the rounded tip if your yarn needs support. Make the second wing to match.

## Part 5 — A tiny tail at the back

The tail peeks out like a small cocoa-colored button. Use **dark cinnamon yarn**.

1. **Rnd 1:** Make 4 sc in an MR. **[4]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in all four stitches. **[8]**
3. **Rnds 3–5:** Work 8 sc for three rounds. **[8 each round]**

Flatten the opening and close it with **4 sc through both layers**. **[4 closing stitches]** Leave enough yarn to sew it to the rear of the body. This piece doesn't need stuffing.

## Part 6 — Two bright orange webbed feet

The feet are worked **flat in turned rows**, which makes them easy to stitch beneath the body. Make **two** with pumpkin-orange yarn.

1. **Ch 5.** Starting in the second chain from the hook, make 4 sc. **[4]**
2. **Row 2:** Ch 1 and turn. Increase in the first stitch, work 2 sc, then increase in the last stitch. **[6]**
3. **Row 3:** Ch 1 and turn. Work 6 sc. **[6]**
4. **Row 4:** Ch 1 and turn. Work 2 sc, **ch 2 and sl st into the first chain of that little ch-2 loop**. Repeat that entire group three times. **[6 sc plus 3 small toe loops]**

Fasten off, leaving a sewing tail. The little loops form a gentle suggestion of three webbed toes; they are not separate stuffed fingers. Shape the feet between your fingers so the toes face forward.

## Part 7 — Assemble your little mallard

Pin all the pieces in place before sewing. For a more detailed joining method, visit [How to Sew Amigurumi Pieces Together](/learn/sew-amigurumi-pieces-together/).

1. **Join head to collar.** Position the green head over the body's **18-stitch ivory opening**, with the closed head end tucked slightly inside. Sew the ivory edge securely to the lower head stitches. Check that the head stands straight before hiding the sewing tail.
2. **Add the bill.** Pin the flattened yellow bill below and between the eyes. Sew its **six-stitch closing edge** to the front of the face so the rounded section projects forward. Don't pull the stitches so tightly that the bill bends sideways.
3. **Set the wings.** Place each taupe wing on an upper side of the brown body. The rounded ends should point toward the front and the flattened attachment edges toward the back. Sew the narrow edges on securely, then tack another small point if the wing flaps too far outward.
4. **Attach the tail.** Sew the four-stitch tail edge to the middle of the back, around the broadest part of the body. Tip it upward for a playful silhouette.
5. **Add the feet.** Sew each foot's **starting-chain edge** to the underside toward the front, leaving enough room between the feet for Puddlewick to sit. The three tiny toe loops point forward.
6. **Finish the expression.** If you chose embroidered eyes, make two short dark stitches symmetrically on the green face. Check every seam, tuck loose tails securely inside and remove the temporary markers.

## A few useful troubleshooting notes

- **The head leans:** Check that the ivory collar opening is sewn all the way around and the body is evenly stuffed. Extra stuffing alone will not fix a crooked seam.
- **The bill looks pointy:** Flatten its oval gently before sewing and use the closing edge, not the rounded end, for the join.
- **The wings don't match:** Count the 15-stitch round carefully; each wing should decrease from 18 to 15 and then to 12.
- **Puddlewick tips forward:** Move the orange feet a stitch or two back before sewing permanently. Our [balance guide](/blog/why-crochet-animal-wont-sit-straight/) may help.
- **You can see the stuffing:** Try a slightly smaller hook on your next piece or remove a small amount of filling. Read [The Little Art of Stuffing Amigurumi](/blog/the-little-art-of-stuffing-amigurumi/).
