Hazel is a little squirrel who loves the sound of leaves underfoot and thinks a tidy basket of acorns makes an excellent autumn decoration. She has a big round head, a pale cozy chest, a pair of tiny listening ears and a **generous curling tail** that gives her a unmistakable squirrel silhouette.

This is a **new Thread & Petal design**, not a rewritten transcription of the book's Ruff the Squirrel. It draws on general squirrel anatomy, but uses independently composed construction, proportions and stitch sequences. The cover is **an original illustration**, not a photograph of a crocheted sample. The counts have been checked on paper, but **this pattern has not been physically test-crocheted**.

## Gather the pieces before you begin

| Make | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| One head and two small ears | A curious, bright-eyed expression |
| One bottom-up body with a cream chest | A plump seated silhouette |
| Two arms and two feet | Small balanced paws |
| One large tapered tail | The soft curve at Hazel's back |

**Terms are US crochet terms.** **MR** = magic ring; **sc** = single crochet; **inc** = 2 sc in the same stitch; **dec** = decrease over two stitches; **ch** = chain; **sl st** = slip stitch; **FO** = fasten off. Counts in **[brackets]** are after the round is complete. Work in a spiral and move your round-start marker every round.

We recommend reviewing [how to control crochet tension](/learn/controlling-crochet-tension/) before making the narrower tail end; a firm but flexible fabric keeps the stuffing hidden without hurting your fingers.

## Part 1 — A round cinnamon head

Make the crown in **rusty orange**.

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in a magic ring. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** *(sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** *(3 sc, inc)* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** *(4 sc, inc)* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** *(5 sc, inc)* six times. **[42]**
8. **Rnd 8:** *(6 sc, inc)* six times. **[48]**
9. **Rnd 9:** *(7 sc, inc)* six times. **[54]**
10. **Rnd 10–17:** Sc around every round. **[54 per round]**
11. **Rnd 18:** *(7 sc, dec)* six times. **[48]**
12. **Rnd 19:** *(6 sc, dec)* six times. **[42]**

**Before the opening narrows:** Mark a front centerline opposite the first-stitch marker. Embroider two dark eyes around Rnds 13–14, approximately 10–12 stitches apart, and pin the cream muzzle just below the eyes. If using installed toy eyes for an adult display ornament, attach the washers now.

13. **Rnd 20:** *(5 sc, dec)* six times. **[36]**
14. **Rnd 21:** *(4 sc, dec)* six times. **[30]**
15. **Rnd 22:** *(3 sc, dec)* six times. **[24]**

Stuff the head in small tufts, gently rounding the cheeks. Try not to pack one large lump at the center.

16. **Rnd 23:** *(2 sc, dec)* six times. **[18]**
17. **Rnd 24:** *(sc, dec)* six times. **[12]**
18. **Rnd 25:** Dec six times. **[6]**

FO and thread the yarn tail through the six front loops, drawing the hole shut. Weave the end inside.

### A little cream cheek-and-muzzle shape

For each of **two cream discs**, make 6 sc in an MR **[6]**, then increase in all stitches **[12]**. FO with a sewing tail. Arrange these next to each other under the eyes so the center seam forms the little snout. Stitch on a brown triangular nose and a short smile, then add a small rosy cheek stitch at each side.

**Expression check:** If the face looks too wide, overlap the discs a little before sewing rather than altering the head stitch total. You can also read [our embroidered-face lesson](/learn/embroider-amigurumi-faces/).

## Part 2 — The rounded little body

Start at the **base** in rusty orange.

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Inc around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** *(sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** *(3 sc, inc)* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** *(4 sc, inc)* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** *(5 sc, inc)* six times. **[42]**
8. **Rnd 8:** *(6 sc, inc)* six times. **[48]**
9. **Rnd 9–13:** Sc around. **[48 per round]**
10. **Rnd 14:** *(6 sc, dec)* six times. **[42]**
11. **Rnd 15–16:** Sc around. **[42 per round]**
12. **Rnd 17:** *(5 sc, dec)* six times. **[36]**
13. **Rnd 18–19:** Sc around. **[36 per round]**
14. **Rnd 20:** *(4 sc, dec)* six times. **[30]**
15. **Rnd 21–22:** Sc around. **[30 per round]**
16. **Rnd 23:** *(3 sc, dec)* six times. **[24]**
17. **Rnd 24:** Sc around. **[24]**

Leave this top edge **open** and FO with a long tail. Stuff more firmly at the rounded bottom and a little less around the neck. You'll sew the 24-stitch opening beneath the already-closed head.

### A soft cream chest

Work a flat cream circle: **Rnd 1: 6 sc [6]; Rnd 2: inc around [12]; Rnd 3: (sc, inc) × 6 [18]; Rnd 4: (2 sc, inc) × 6 [24]; Rnd 5: (3 sc, inc) × 6 [30]**. FO with a long tail.

Pin the circle centered on the front body before sewing. Stretch it into a gentle oval with your fingers if you'd like a longer chest area; the top edge can tuck slightly under the chin.

## Part 3 — Two tiny, attentive ears

Make two in cinnamon yarn.

1. **Rnd 1:** 5 sc in MR. **[5]**
2. **Rnd 2:** *(sc, inc)* twice, sc in the last stitch. **[7]**
3. **Rnd 3:** *(2 sc, inc)* twice, sc in the last stitch. **[9]**
4. **Rnd 4:** *(2 sc, inc)* three times. **[12]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Sc around. **[12]**
6. **Rnd 6:** *(3 sc, inc)* three times. **[15]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Sc around. **[15]**

FO with a sewing tail, **without stuffing**. Flatten gently and pinch each base to make a pointed ear. Add a few peach-pink embroidery stitches to the inner face before sewing at the crown.

**Little symmetry test:** Place both ears on the table with their open edges lined up. Pin them to the head before stitching, ideally high enough to be visible above the cheeks.

## Part 4 — A big, curved squirrel tail

This is the star of the project. You'll crochet a **tapered tube beginning at the narrow tip**, growing gradually fuller toward the attachment end. Once stuffed, you can curve it around the squirrel by **sewing a few small hidden tacking stitches along the inner side**. No wire is required.

1. **Rnd 1:** With rust-orange yarn, 6 sc in MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2–4:** Sc around. **[6 per round]**
3. **Rnd 5:** *(sc, inc)* three times. **[9]**
4. **Rnd 6–7:** Sc around. **[9 per round]**
5. **Rnd 8:** *(2 sc, inc)* three times. **[12]**
6. **Rnd 9–10:** Sc around. **[12 per round]**
7. **Rnd 11:** *(3 sc, inc)* three times. **[15]**
8. **Rnd 12–13:** Sc around. **[15 per round]**
9. **Rnd 14:** *(4 sc, inc)* three times. **[18]**
10. **Rnd 15–17:** Sc around. **[18 per round]**
11. **Rnd 18:** *(5 sc, inc)* three times. **[21]**
12. **Rnd 19–21:** Sc around. **[21 per round]**
13. **Rnd 22:** *(6 sc, inc)* three times. **[24]**
14. **Rnd 23–25:** Sc around. **[24 per round]**
15. **Rnd 26:** *(7 sc, inc)* three times. **[27]**
16. **Rnd 27–30:** Sc around. **[27 per round]**
17. **Rnd 31:** *(8 sc, inc)* three times. **[30]**
18. **Rnd 32–34:** Sc around. **[30 per round]**
19. **Rnd 35:** *(3 sc, dec)* six times. **[24]**
20. **Rnd 36:** Sc around. **[24]**

Stuff gradually, leaving the final couple of rounds **less full** so the opening can sit flat against the body. FO with a long sewing tail.

**Shape it gently:** Before sewing, hold the narrow tip above the body and the wider base at the rear hip. The tail should curl up, not stick straight out. A few hidden tacking stitches on the inside bend will hold the curve, though very firm stuffing makes this harder. If the tail is too stiff, take a little fiberfill out.

Want to vary your yarn? Our Journal note about [how yarn weight changes amigurumi](/blog/how-yarn-weight-changes-amigurumi/) explains why a thicker yarn can make this tail noticeably heavier.

## Part 5 — Small hugging arms and sitting feet

### Arms (make two)

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Inc around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3–5:** Sc around. **[12 per round]**
4. **Rnd 6:** *(2 sc, dec)* three times. **[9]**
5. **Rnd 7–14:** Sc around. **[9 per round]**

Stuff each rounded paw very lightly and flatten the shoulder opening. FO with long tails.

### Feet (make two)

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Inc around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** *(sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5–7:** Sc around. **[24 per round]**
6. **Rnd 8:** *(2 sc, dec)* six times. **[18]**
7. **Rnd 9–10:** Sc around. **[18 per round]**
8. **Rnd 11:** *(4 sc, dec)* three times. **[15]**
9. **Rnd 12:** Sc around. **[15]**

Stuff the toes, flatten the ends, and leave sewing tails.

## Part 6 — Sew Hazel into a woodland companion

1. **Attach the cream chest patch** to the body while its top remains open, keeping the circle's center toward the front.
2. **Sew the body to the head**, using the body’s 24-stitch opening to make a neat seam beneath the lower head. Check that the muzzle faces the center of the cream chest.
3. **Pin both arms** at the upper sides of the body, letting the hands reach toward the chest.
4. **Position the feet forward** at the bottom, then set Hazel on a flat surface to check the balance before sewing.
5. **Place the large tail at the lower back.** Sew the open 24-stitch end firmly to the body. Curve the tail upward at one side and use hidden supporting stitches so the weight does not tip the squirrel over.
6. **Sew on the ears** and add any final embroidered details.

If you're new to attaching a heavy tail, work a little extra reinforcement inside the seam and keep the tail's top half less stuffed. The [amigurumi assembly lesson](/learn/sew-amigurumi-pieces-together/) has more joining tips.

## Small troubles, gentle fixes

| Problem | Try this |
| --- | --- |
| The tail falls backward | Reduce stuffing near the base and secure the inner curve with two or three concealed stitches |
| Hazel won't sit without support | Shift the feet a little farther forward and lower the tail seam |
| One ear leans oddly | Pin both together, then sew a second anchoring stitch at the base |
| The cream chest puckers | Avoid pulling the sewing yarn too tightly; spread the disc smoothly over the body |
| The face seems too serious | Move the muzzle discs slightly closer and adjust the embroidered smile |

## Make Hazel your own

Swap cinnamon for hazelnut brown, try a cream tip on the tail or add a leaf-shaped embroidered brooch to the chest. If you'd like a very small autumn accent, crochet a tiny leaf with a short chain and a few shaping stitches, then sew it directly to the body rather than leaving it loose.

A squirrel is a little creature of curiosity. Let this one remind you that even a familiar stitch can make something surprising.
