There's something cheerful about a curious little seagull — especially one made from yarn. This friendly bird has a softly rounded cream-white body, a sunny yellow beak and feet, and two grey wings finished with darker tips. It makes a charming coastal decoration, a handmade gift, or a satisfying introduction to shaping amigurumi.

This free, editorially rewritten adaptation follows the construction of **Scraps the Seagull**, originally designed by **Crochetbykim** and published in *Zoomigurumi 7: 15 Cute Amigurumi Patterns by 11 Great Designers* (Amigurumipatterns.net / Meteoor bvba, 2018). Thread & Petal's site owner reports receiving permission to adapt and publish the design. **Design credit remains with Crochetbykim.**

**About the images:** The seaside views here are newly AI-generated styling illustrations. They are **not** photographs of a completed, test-crocheted sample, and the visible details may differ from the written pattern. **Follow the stitch instructions and counts, not the illustration, for construction.**

## Before you begin: a quick project guide

- **Skill level:** Easy, as classified in the original book; some familiarity with crocheting in a spiral will help.
- **Finished height:** Approximately **20 cm / 8 in** when using the source's recommended materials. Your result may differ with yarn, hook and tension.
- **Terminology:** US crochet terms throughout. In UK terms, the US *single crochet* is called *double crochet*.
- **Construction:** One continuous white head-and-body piece, one yellow beak, two black-and-grey wings, two yellow legs, and two black tail feathers.
- **How the rounds work:** Crochet continuously in a spiral unless a step says otherwise. Place a marker at the start of each round and move it upward as you work.

Want a quick refresher? Visit our [magic-ring and working-in-the-round lesson](/learn/magic-ring-crochet-in-the-round/) or [increases and decreases guide](/learn/crochet-increases-and-decreases/). These lessons are free and you can come straight back here afterward.

### Crochet abbreviations

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **MR** | Magic ring |
| **ch** | Chain |
| **sc** | Single crochet (US) |
| **sl st** | Slip stitch |
| **inc** | Work 2 single crochets into the same stitch |
| **dec** | Work 2 stitches together as a single-crochet decrease |
| **st / sts** | Stitch / stitches |
| **Rnd / Rnds** | Round / rounds |
| **( … ) × 6** | Repeat the instructions in parentheses six times |

Numbers in **[square brackets]** give the stitch count you should have at the end of the round. If a round doesn't match, count again before starting the next one. Need help with the notation? See [How to Read Crochet Patterns](/learn/how-to-read-crochet-patterns/).

> **Toy safety:** Plastic safety eyes can detach and are not suitable for toys intended for babies or children under three. For a child-safe version, use securely embroidered facial features and check every seam for loose parts. This is a craft pattern, not a tested toy-safety certification.

## Part 1 — Crochet the white head and body

Use **white worsted-weight yarn**. This entire shape is made as **one piece**, starting at the top of the head, widening into the body, then closing underneath. There is **no separate head to sew on**.

### Rounds 1–7: make the upper dome

1. **Rnd 1:** Place 6 sc into an adjustable magic ring. Tighten the center. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Make an increase in every stitch around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *(1 sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *(3 sc, inc)* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *(4 sc, inc)* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *(5 sc, inc)* six times. **[42]**

You should now have a rounded top with **42 stitches** in the final round. See our [single-crochet tutorial](/learn/single-crochet-stitch/) if you need to revisit the basic stitch.

### Rounds 8–23: grow the tall head

For **each round from 8 through 23**, work **1 sc in every stitch**. Keep **42 stitches per round**. These 16 unshaped rounds build the head's tall, slightly cylindrical profile.

### Round 24: make the body extension

This is the unusual shaping step that gives the bird its distinctive silhouette. **Count carefully**, because the final stitch total changes from 42 to 61.

1. After completing round 23, **chain 10** from the working loop.
2. Starting in the **second chain from the hook**, make **2 sc in that chain**.
3. Work **1 sc into each of the next 8 chains**.
4. Continue around the existing white piece with **1 sc in each of the next 41 body stitches**.
5. Working along the **opposite side of your new chain**, place **1 sc into each of the next 8 chain positions**.
6. Finish with **2 sc in the remaining chain position**. **[61]**

**Checkpoint:** You should have **61 stitches** around this newly extended outline. This chain extension becomes part of the bird's long body/tail profile. Use a stitch marker to identify your starting point before moving on.

### Eyes and rounds 25–35: shape the body

The original design places **8 mm safety eyes between rounds 12 and 13**, leaving **two stitches between them**. Before fixing their washers, look at the direction of the extended body and place the eyes symmetrically on the face. Once safety-eye washers are attached, they cannot easily be moved.

- **Rnd 25:** Work 1 sc, inc, 57 sc, inc, 1 sc. **[63]**
- **Rnds 26–30:** Work 63 sc in each of these five rounds. **[63]**
- **Rnd 31:** Work 3 sc, dec twice, 23 sc, dec once, 27 sc, dec twice. **[58]**
- **Rnd 32:** Work 27 sc, dec twice, 27 sc. **[56]**
- **Rnd 33:** Work one sc in each of the 56 stitches. **[56]**
- **Rnd 34:** Work dec twice, 22 sc, dec twice, 22 sc, dec twice. **[50]**
- **Rnd 35:** Crochet 50 sc around. **[50]**

Begin **stuffing with fiberfill** once the body's basic silhouette is established, and add more as the opening narrows. Try to fill the form without stretching the stitches so much that the stuffing shows through.

### Rounds 36–43: close the underside

- **Rnd 36:** Work dec, 46 sc, dec. **[48]**
- **Rnd 37:** Repeat *(6 sc, dec)* six times. **[42]**
- **Rnd 38:** Repeat *(5 sc, dec)* six times. **[36]**
- **Rnd 39:** Repeat *(4 sc, dec)* six times. **[30]**
- **Rnd 40:** Repeat *(3 sc, dec)* six times. **[24]**
- **Rnd 41:** Repeat *(2 sc, dec)* six times. **[18]**
- **Rnd 42:** Repeat *(1 sc, dec)* six times. **[12]**
- **Rnd 43:** Make six decreases. **[6]**

Finish the stuffing before the opening becomes too small. Fasten off and secure the remaining opening neatly with your yarn needle, then hide the tail inside the body.

**Progress check:** You now have the single white head-and-body piece. The next parts add the face and the characteristic grey-and-black wings.

## Part 2 — Make the yellow beak

Using **yellow yarn**, crochet a small tube:

1. **Rnd 1:** Work 6 sc in a magic ring. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in every stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnds 3–8:** Work 12 sc around for each of these six rounds. **[12]**

Fasten off, saving a **long sewing tail**. Put a modest amount of fiberfill into the beak so it keeps its shape. Position it **one round below the eyes** and sew it securely to the head.

For the original friendly expression, embroider small **black eyebrows across rounds 10–11**. Facial placement is easier if you pin the beak in place before you start sewing.

## Part 3 — Crochet two two-tone wings

Make **two identical wings**. Begin each one in **black yarn**, then change to **grey after round 6**. The black starting section becomes the dark wing tip.

### Black tip: rounds 1–6

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in a magic ring. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** 6 sc, with no increases. **[6]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Increase in every stitch. **[12]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Work 12 sc. **[12]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *(1 sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Work 18 sc. **[18]**

Change to **grey yarn** for the rest of the wing.

### Grey wing: rounds 7–22

- **Rnd 7:** Repeat *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
- **Rnd 8:** Work 24 sc. **[24]**
- **Rnd 9:** Repeat *(3 sc, inc)* six times. **[30]**
- **Rnds 10–14:** Work 30 sc around in each of these five rounds. **[30]**
- **Rnd 15:** Repeat *(3 sc, dec)* six times. **[24]**
- **Rnd 16:** Work 24 sc. **[24]**
- **Rnd 17:** Repeat *(2 sc, dec)* six times. **[18]**
- **Rnds 18–19:** Work 18 sc in each round. **[18]**
- **Rnd 20:** Repeat *(1 sc, dec)* six times. **[12]**
- **Rnds 21–22:** Work 12 sc in each round. **[12]**

Fasten off each wing, retaining a long tail to attach it later. **Do not stuff the wings**; leaving them flat lets them lie naturally against the body.

## Part 4 — Make the yellow feet and legs

Work **two** of the following pieces in yellow yarn. Each begins with a rounded, stuffed foot and continues into a narrow, **unstuffed** leg.

1. **Rnd 1:** Crochet 6 sc into a magic ring. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in every stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *(1 sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnds 5–8:** Work 24 sc in each round. **[24]**
6. **Rnd 9:** Work 6 sc, then six decreases, then 6 sc. **[18]**
7. **Rnd 10:** Work 3 sc, then six decreases, then 3 sc. **[12]**
8. **Rnds 11–20:** Work 12 sc in each of these ten rounds. **[12]**

Fasten off, leaving enough yarn to sew the leg to the bird. **Stuff the wide foot with fiberfill, but leave the narrow leg empty**, as the source pattern specifies.

Make the second foot and leg in exactly the same way. The source design creates its rounded feet by shaping the tube with decreases rather than by crocheting individual toes.

## Part 5 — Make two black tail feathers

Use **black yarn** and make the following flat feather pieces **twice**:

1. **Rnd 1:** Crochet 6 sc into a magic ring. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase all the way around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *(1 sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *(3 sc, inc)* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *(4 sc, inc)* six times. **[36]**

Fold each circular piece in half to make a semicircle. For **round 7**, crochet **18 sc through both folded layers** along the curved edge. **[18]**

Fasten off with a sewing tail. The doubled-over fabric gives the feathers structure without needing stuffing.

## Part 6 — Assemble your seagull

Gather the body, beak, two wings, two leg-and-foot pieces and two tail feathers. Position the parts with pins before sewing; small placement adjustments can change the expression of your bird.

1. **Feet first.** Sew both yellow legs under the body, leaving **two stitches between their attachment points**, as directed in the original pattern. Keep the rounded feet pointing toward the front.
2. **Add the tail.** Attach the two black folded tail feathers to the back of the extended body. Spread them slightly so both remain visible.
3. **Place the wings.** Pin a wing against each side of the body at about **round 24**. Angle the black tips gently backward, toward the tail, then sew them in place. The source uses the word *row* at this point, but the body is worked in rounds.
4. **Finish the expression.** If you haven't already, confirm that the yellow beak is secured one round below the eyes and the black eyebrows are embroidered over rounds 10–11.
5. **Make a tiny tuft.** Add a few short grey yarn strands to the top of the head. Secure and trim them to form a playful little crest.
6. **Check the seams.** Tug gently at each attached piece to make sure the sewing is firm, and weave in every remaining yarn end.

**A little styling idea:** Place your finished bird on a shelf with a seashell, a smooth beach stone or a little handmade wave. For photographs, turn it to face the left or the right — the finished toy can tell a different story from every angle.

## A few useful troubleshooting notes

- **The body doesn't reach 61 stitches in round 24:** Check the chain extension carefully. The round includes stitches along both sides of the chain *and* 41 stitches around the existing body.
- **Your wing tips are the wrong color:** The first **six** wing rounds are black. Change to grey **before round 7**.
- **The stuffing shows through:** Try a firmer crochet tension or adjust the hook to suit your yarn, without making the fabric painfully tight to work.
- **The feet are too floppy:** The wider foot section is stuffed, but the narrow leg section remains unstuffed in the source design. Double-check the assembly placement before sewing permanently.
- **Your seams look untidy:** Use matching yarn and smaller joining stitches. Our [finishing lesson](/learn/fix-crochet-mistakes-and-finish/) walks you through secure attachments and weaving ends.
