Meet a little reindeer who's dressed for a snowy afternoon. With his rounded beige face, dark chocolate antlers, little brown hooves and an extra-long blue scarf, he makes the sweetest winter companion. The best part is watching him come together: one small crocheted shape at a time, until those ears, antlers and tiny tail give him his own personality.

This project is worked in separate pieces and joined at the end. You'll make the head and face first, build two antlers from paired branches, then work the sweater-shaped body, arms, legs and scarf. The pieces use familiar stitches, so the main challenge is keeping track of your rounds and taking your time with the finishing.

<h2 id="before-you-begin">Before you begin</h2>

- **Finished size:** approximately **13 in / 33 cm tall** when using the recommended yarn and hook. Your size can vary with tension.
- **Construction:** a separate head, muzzle, nose, pair of ears, pair of branching antlers, body, two arms, two legs, tail and removable scarf.
- **Terminology:** US crochet terms. If you learned UK terminology, our [reading crochet patterns guide](/learn/how-to-read-crochet-patterns/) explains the differences.
- **Working method:** continuous spiral rounds for the stuffed pieces; two turned rows for the scarf.
- **Little preparation tip:** leave long tails wherever a piece needs sewing, and keep a stitch marker in the first stitch of every round.

Want to practice a technique first? Our [magic-ring lesson](/learn/magic-ring-crochet-in-the-round/), [single crochet guide](/learn/single-crochet-stitch/) and [shaping lesson](/learn/crochet-increases-and-decreases/) are handy refreshers.

<h2 id="stitch-key">Stitch key (US terms)</h2>

| Short form | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **MR** | Magic ring / adjustable loop |
| **sc** | Single crochet |
| **inc** | Work 2 sc into one stitch |
| **dec** | Work 2 stitches together as 1 sc decrease |
| **BLO** | Back loop only |
| **ch** | Chain |
| **hdc** | Half double crochet |
| **st / sts** | Stitch / stitches |
| **Rnd / Rnds** | Round / rounds |
| **( … ) × 6** | Repeat the instructions in parentheses six times |

The stitch number in **[brackets]** tells you how many stitches should remain when that round is complete. Stopping to count at the end of every increase or decrease round makes assembly much easier later.

> **For little hands:** If you're making a toy for a young child, securely embroidered eyes are a better choice than detachable plastic eyes. Make sure all pieces and yarn ends are firmly attached.

<h2 id="make-the-pieces">Let's make the pieces</h2>

## Part 1 — Make the rounded reindeer head

Begin with **beige yarn** at the top of the head. First, increase evenly to build a soft dome, then work several plain rounds to add height. The little dip and second increase section shape the face before the opening narrows at the neck.

### Rounds 1–8: open out the crown

1. **Rnd 1:** Make a magic ring and work 6 single crochets into the centre. Pull the ring snug. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Crochet 2 sc into every stitch around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 sc, then an increase in the next stitch* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *2 sc, then an increase* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *3 sc, then an increase* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *4 sc, then an increase* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *5 sc, then an increase* six times. **[42]**
8. **Rnd 8:** Repeat *6 sc, then an increase* six times. **[48]**

**Checkpoint:** Your little beige circle should now hold **48 stitches**. If it looks slightly curved rather than perfectly flat, that's exactly what you want for a rounded head.

### Rounds 9–24: give the face its shape

9. **Rnds 9–14:** Work 1 sc into every stitch for six complete rounds, keeping **48 stitches** in each. **[48]**
10. **Rnd 15:** Repeat *6 sc, then a decrease* six times. **[42]**
11. **Rnd 16:** Single crochet once in every stitch around. **[42]**
12. **Rnd 17:** Repeat *6 sc, then an increase* six times. **[48]**
13. **Rnd 18:** Repeat *7 sc, then an increase* six times. **[54]**
14. **Rnds 19–24:** Crochet 1 sc per stitch for six rounds, leaving **54 stitches** in every round. **[54]**

The wider lower section gives the face its gentle, full-cheeked look. Before you close the head, position the **12 mm eyes between rounds 15 and 16, with seven stitches between the eyes**. Check both eyes from the front to make sure they are level, then secure them.

### Rounds 25–29: narrow the neck opening

15. **Rnd 25:** Repeat *7 sc, then a decrease* six times. **[48]**
16. **Rnd 26:** Repeat *6 sc, then a decrease* six times. **[42]**
17. **Rnd 27:** Repeat *5 sc, then a decrease* six times. **[36]**
18. **Rnd 28:** Repeat *4 sc, then a decrease* six times. **[30]**
19. **Rnd 29:** Repeat *3 sc, then a decrease* six times. **[24]**

Fill the head evenly with fiberfill, especially around the cheeks. Aim for a pleasantly rounded shape without stretching the stitches so far that the stuffing shows through. Fasten off and secure the working end. You'll attach the head to the body through its **24-stitch opening** later.

## Part 2 — Crochet the cream muzzle

The muzzle is a small, shallow cup that sits just below and between the eyes. Use **white yarn**, and leave an extra-long sewing tail to attach the edge neatly to the face.

1. **Rnd 1:** Place 6 sc inside a magic ring. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in each stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Work *1 sc, 1 increase* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Work *2 sc, 1 increase* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Work *3 sc, 1 increase* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnds 6–7:** Crochet 30 sc per round for two rounds. **[30]**

Fasten off with a long tail. Keep the muzzle open for now; you'll add a small amount of stuffing as you sew its edge to the head.

## Part 3 — Add the chocolate-brown nose

Make a separate rounded nose in **dark brown yarn**. Its slightly gathered end will sit against the cream muzzle, adding a sweet raised detail.

1. **Rnd 1:** Crochet 6 sc into a magic ring. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase all the way around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 sc, then an increase* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Crochet one sc in each of the 18 stitches. **[18]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *1 sc, then a decrease* six times. **[12]**

Stuff the nose firmly enough to hold a soft, rounded button shape. Cut the yarn with a long tail so you can sew the nose onto the muzzle during assembly.

## Part 4 — Grow two little branching antlers

These are the most unusual pieces in the project. **Each antler is made from two slim tubes**, one tall and one short, which join into a forked shape. You'll repeat the full process for the second antler, using **dark brown yarn** throughout.

### Make the tall branch

1. **Rnd 1:** Begin with 7 sc in an adjustable magic ring. **[7]**
2. **Rnds 2–11:** Work one sc in each of the seven stitches for ten rounds. **[7]**

Fasten off, leaving a length of yarn for closing the little opening between branches later.

### Make the shorter branch

1. **Rnd 1:** Crochet 7 sc into a magic ring. **[7]**
2. **Rnds 2–7:** Crochet one sc in every stitch for six rounds. **[7]**

**Keep the yarn attached to the short branch.** You're ready to join the two tubes into one antler.

### Join the branches and shape the base

1. **Rnd 8:** Working from the short branch, crochet across all **7 stitches of the tall branch**, then across all **7 stitches of the short branch**. You now have one continuous round around both tubes. **[14]**
2. **Rnd 9:** Work 1 sc in each of the 14 stitches around. **[14]**
3. **Rnd 10:** Make four decreases, then crochet 1 sc into the next six stitches. **[10]**
4. **Rnds 11–15:** Single crochet in each of the 10 stitches for five rounds to create the stem that attaches to the head. **[10]**

Fill both branches and the base lightly as you work so the antler holds its shape, but avoid making the joined area too bulky. Fasten off with a long sewing tail. Use the yarn tail you saved on the tall branch to stitch closed any little gap where the two tubes meet.

**Repeat everything for the other antler.** Before sewing them onto the head, lay both out side by side so the longer branches can point inward symmetrically.

## Part 5 — Make the soft beige ears

You'll need **two ears**. They are worked as short oval-like tubes, then flattened and gently pinched at their bases. Unlike the antlers, **the ears are not stuffed**.

1. **Rnd 1:** Start with 5 sc in a magic ring. **[5]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Crochet 1 sc in each of the five stitches. **[5]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Place an increase in every stitch. **[10]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Work 10 sc around. **[10]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *1 sc, then an increase* five times. **[15]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *2 sc, then an increase* five times. **[20]**
7. **Rnds 7–11:** Work 20 sc around for five rounds without changing the stitch count. **[20]**
8. **Rnd 12:** Decrease ten times around to narrow the opening. **[10]**
9. **Rnd 13:** Crochet 10 sc around. **[10]**

Fasten off and leave a long tail. Flatten each ear between your fingers without adding stuffing; the folded edge will help it keep that little pointed look when you pinch and sew the base.

## Part 6 — Crochet the reindeer's sweater-shaped body

Now for the little blue sweater! The body begins with a **beige base**, gets a narrow **grey stripe** and then changes to **dark blue** for the sweater. Keep the color changes tidy by completing the final pull-through of the preceding stitch with the new color.

### Beige base: rounds 1–11

1. **Rnd 1:** Make a magic ring and work 6 sc into it. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in every stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 sc, then an increase* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *2 sc, then an increase* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *3 sc, then an increase* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *4 sc, then an increase* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *5 sc, then an increase* six times. **[42]**
8. **Rnd 8:** Repeat *6 sc, then an increase* six times. **[48]**
9. **Rnds 9–11:** Single crochet in all 48 stitches for three rounds. **[48]**

### Grey trim and the dark blue sweater

10. **Rnds 12–13:** Switch to **grey yarn** and work 48 sc around in each round. **[48]**
11. **Rnds 14–18:** Change to **dark blue yarn** and work 48 sc in each of these five rounds. **[48]**

**Checkpoint:** You should see the beige body below a narrow grey band and a wider section of blue. The next decreases shape the shoulders so the neck will support the head.

### Narrow the upper body

12. **Rnd 19:** Repeat *6 sc, then a decrease* six times. **[42]**
13. **Rnd 20:** Work one sc in each stitch. **[42]**
14. **Rnd 21:** Repeat *5 sc, then a decrease* six times. **[36]**
15. **Rnd 22:** Crochet one sc in every stitch around. **[36]**
16. **Rnd 23:** Repeat *4 sc, then a decrease* six times. **[30]**
17. **Rnd 24:** Work 30 sc around. **[30]**
18. **Rnd 25:** Repeat *3 sc, then a decrease* six times. **[24]**
19. **Rnd 26:** Finish with one single crochet in each of the 24 stitches. **[24]**

Stuff the body until it has a softly rounded, firm shape. Fasten off with a generous sewing tail for attaching the head. The body should end with **24 stitches**, matching the head opening.

## Part 7 — Make the little brown hooves and blue arms

Make **two arms**. You'll start at the dark brown hoof, add a little grey cuff and finish with the long dark blue sleeve. The opening gradually becomes narrower toward the shoulder.

### Brown hoof

1. **Rnd 1:** Put 6 sc into a magic ring using **dark brown yarn**. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in each stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 sc, then an increase* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *2 sc, then an increase* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Work **one sc into the back loop only** of each stitch, creating a neat bend at the edge of the hoof. **[24]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Crochet 24 regular sc around. **[24]**

### Grey cuff and blue sleeve

7. **Rnds 7–8:** Switch to **grey yarn** and work 24 sc in each round. **[24]**
8. **Rnd 9:** Change to **dark blue yarn**. Repeat *10 sc, then a decrease* twice. **[22]**
9. **Rnd 10:** Work 22 sc around. **[22]**
10. **Rnd 11:** Repeat *9 sc, then a decrease* twice. **[20]**
11. **Rnd 12:** Crochet 20 sc around. **[20]**
12. **Rnd 13:** Repeat *8 sc, then a decrease* twice. **[18]**
13. **Rnd 14:** Work 18 sc around. **[18]**
14. **Rnd 15:** Repeat *7 sc, then a decrease* twice. **[16]**
15. **Rnd 16:** Single crochet in each of the 16 stitches. **[16]**
16. **Rnd 17:** Repeat *6 sc, then a decrease* twice. **[14]**
17. **Rnd 18:** Crochet 14 sc around. **[14]**
18. **Rnd 19:** Repeat *5 sc, then a decrease* twice. **[12]**
19. **Rnd 20:** Crochet once in each of the 12 stitches. **[12]**
20. **Rnd 21:** Repeat *4 sc, then a decrease* twice. **[10]**
21. **Rnd 22:** Work 10 sc around to finish the sleeve. **[10]**

Cut the yarn, keeping a long tail for sewing. **Stuff the brown hand/hoof firmly but the upper sleeve only lightly**; that gives the arms a rounded end without making them stiff. Work the second arm in the same way.

## Part 8 — Crochet the chunky feet and legs

The feet are meant to be lovely and rounded, while the narrow beige legs stay softer so the finished reindeer can sit comfortably. Make **two identical pieces**, beginning with dark brown for the hooves and changing to beige farther up.

### Form each dark brown hoof

1. **Rnd 1:** Begin with 6 sc inside a magic ring. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in each stitch around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 sc, then an increase* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *2 sc, then an increase* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *3 sc, then an increase* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Single crochet through the **back loops only** of all 30 stitches to turn the hoof upwards. **[30]**
7. **Rnds 7–9:** Work one sc in every stitch for three rounds. **[30]**

### Switch to beige and taper the legs

8. **Rnd 10:** Change to **beige yarn** and work 30 sc around. **[30]**
9. **Rnd 11:** Repeat *13 sc, then a decrease* twice. **[28]**
10. **Rnd 12:** Crochet once in each of the 28 stitches. **[28]**
11. **Rnd 13:** Repeat *12 sc, then a decrease* twice. **[26]**
12. **Rnd 14:** Work 26 sc around. **[26]**
13. **Rnd 15:** Repeat *11 sc, then a decrease* twice. **[24]**
14. **Rnd 16:** Single crochet in every stitch around. **[24]**
15. **Rnd 17:** Repeat *10 sc, then a decrease* twice. **[22]**
16. **Rnd 18:** Work 22 sc around. **[22]**
17. **Rnd 19:** Repeat *9 sc, then a decrease* twice. **[20]**
18. **Rnd 20:** Crochet one sc in each of the 20 stitches. **[20]**
19. **Rnd 21:** Repeat *8 sc, then a decrease* twice. **[18]**
20. **Rnd 22:** Work 18 sc around. **[18]**
21. **Rnd 23:** Repeat *7 sc, then a decrease* twice. **[16]**
22. **Rnd 24:** Crochet 16 sc around. **[16]**
23. **Rnd 25:** Repeat *6 sc, then a decrease* twice. **[14]**
24. **Rnd 26:** Work one sc in every stitch to finish the leg. **[14]**

Fasten off with a long sewing tail. **Fill the wide brown feet firmly, then use only a little stuffing toward the top of each beige leg.** If you fill the entire leg tightly, the reindeer may have trouble keeping his relaxed seated pose.

Make your second leg, then place both side by side to check that the brown hooves are the same height before attaching them to the body.

## Part 9 — Add a tiny two-tone tail

Even a small detail can change the whole character! The reindeer's tail starts with a white tip and finishes in beige. Make just **one**.

1. **Rnd 1:** Work 6 sc in a magic ring using **white yarn**. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Make one sc in each of the six stitches. **[6]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 sc, then an increase* three times. **[9]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Crochet 9 sc around. **[9]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *2 sc, then an increase* three times. **[12]**
6. **Rnds 6–9:** Switch to **beige yarn** and work 12 sc around for four rounds. **[12]**
7. **Rnd 10:** Decrease six times to draw in the opening. **[6]**

Start adding a small amount of stuffing before the opening gets too narrow. Fasten off with a long tail that you can use to sew the tail onto the lower back.

## Part 10 — Make the extra-long winter scarf

The scarf is the easiest part to personalize. Work it in **light blue yarn**, using **half double crochet**, and finish both ends with tassels. If you're new to the stitch, take a look at our [half double crochet lesson](/learn/half-double-crochet-stitch/).

1. **Foundation:** Chain **102**.
2. **Row 1:** Beginning in the **third chain from the hook**, work one hdc into each of the remaining 100 chains. You should have **100 hdc** across the row. Chain 2, then turn your work. **[100]**
3. **Row 2:** Work one hdc into each of the 100 stitches across the row. **[100]**

Fasten off and weave the ends into the scarf. Cut **eight short lengths of blue yarn**. Use four strands for a small tassel at one end and the remaining four at the other end. Trim them to an even length once attached.

**Little styling tip:** Wrap the scarf gently around the neck when everything is assembled; you can let one tasseled end hang longer than the other for a relaxed winter look.

## Part 11 — Put your reindeer together

This is the part where all those little pieces become one character. **Pin the pieces in place first** and take a moment to look at the reindeer from the front and both sides. The original design's placement points below are useful guides, but the final expression depends on how your fabric and stuffing settle.

1. **Set the muzzle on the face.** Centre the cream muzzle between the eyes, running approximately from **head rounds 16 to 27**. Sew most of the way around, add a little fiberfill, then close the remaining gap.
2. **Attach the brown nose.** Place it on the muzzle across approximately **muzzle rounds 4 to 7**, and sew evenly around the edge. Check that it points straight ahead before hiding the yarn tail.
3. **Give him eyebrows.** Use dark brown yarn to embroider one little diagonal stitch above each eye, spanning approximately **head rounds 14 and 15**.
4. **Position the antlers.** Set the two antlers on the crown at about **head rounds 6 to 8**, with the **longer branches facing inward**. Pin them first, adjust until they look balanced, then sew them firmly in place.
5. **Add the ears.** Pinch the base of each unstuffed ear so the tip stays gently rounded. Stitch the base shut and attach each ear beneath an antler, near **head rounds 11 and 12**.
6. **Join the head and body.** Match the 24-stitch head opening to the top of the 24-stitch body. Add a little more stuffing if necessary to support the neck, then sew securely around the join.
7. **Sew on the blue arms.** Attach them near **body rounds 25 and 26**, leaving roughly **seven stitches between the front attachment points**. Check that the hooves point forward.
8. **Add both legs.** Flatten the top of each beige leg, then sew the flattened edge to the underside of the body over approximately **body rounds 2 to 7**. Angle the hooves forward if you'd like your reindeer to sit comfortably.
9. **Finish the tail.** Sew the little beige-and-white tail to the lower back, around **body rounds 6 and 7**.
10. **Wrap the scarf.** Place it around the neck and arrange the two tassels. Give the seams one last check, then weave in any remaining ends.

## Troubleshooting and finishing touches

- **My antlers lean to one side.** Check that both bases contain similar amounts of stuffing. Pin them again from the front before sewing the final stitches.
- **The head feels wobbly.** Add a little extra stuffing at the top of the body and use small, evenly spaced sewing stitches around the neck join.
- **One leg is longer than the other.** Confirm that both legs ended with 26 rounds and 14 stitches; make sure the flattened openings are sewn at the same height.
- **My sweater stripe looks uneven.** Keep the start-of-round marker in place through the grey-to-blue change and carry the yarn tail neatly inside the body.
- **The scarf is curling.** Gently smooth the strip by hand before tying the tassels. Avoid pulling it tightly around the neck.
