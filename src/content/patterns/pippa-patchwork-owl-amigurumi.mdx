Meet **Pippa**, a quietly curious little owl whose cream-colored face peeks out beneath a taupe crown. Her tiny paired tufts stand up like little punctuation marks, and her peach-and-mauve tummy looks like a miniature patchwork quilt.

The cheerful secret is that almost the whole owl is made with **single crochet**. The challenge comes from deciding *when* to change colors, keeping the stitches counted, and attaching small pieces with care.

**About this design and the pictures:** This is a **newly drafted Thread & Petal construction inspired by the uploaded owl styling images**. It is not a transcription of the owl instructions in the reference book: the stitch counts, belly chart, wings and tufts have been redesigned. The uploaded pictures are **AI-generated crochet-look illustrations**, not photos of a test-made sample and not a guaranteed round-by-round depiction. **This pattern has not yet been physically test-crocheted.** The numbers below were checked for arithmetic, but yarn, tension and shaping can change the finished proportions. If an illustration differs from the directions, follow the written stitch count.

## Before you begin {#before-you-begin}

- **Difficulty:** Intermediate — the basic shapes are approachable; switching colors frequently is the part to practice first.
- **Finished size:** Approximately **14–18 cm / 5½–7 in** with worsted or light-worsted yarn and a firm fabric. This is an **estimate**, not a measurement of a finished sample.
- **Construction:** One continuous head-and-body piece, two softly shaped wings, four little ear-tuft pieces, a small beak, two round feet and an optional tail.
- **Terms:** US crochet terminology. **MR** = magic ring; **sc** = single crochet; **inc** = 2 sc in the same stitch; **dec** = decrease over the next two stitches; **ch** = chain; **sl st** = slip stitch; **st(s)** = stitch(es); **FO** = fasten off.
- **Rounds:** Work in a continuous spiral, without joining at the end, unless stated otherwise. Move your stitch marker into the first stitch of every new round.
- **Counts:** The number in **[brackets]** is how many stitches you should have after completing that round.

A comfortable stitch is more useful than an impossibly tight one. If you can easily see gaps when you gently stretch the fabric, try a smaller hook; if it hurts to enter stitches, give yourself a little more room. Our [tension lesson](/learn/controlling-crochet-tension/) and [changing-yarn-colors tutorial](/learn/changing-yarn-colors-crochet/) are good companions.

### Little project map

| Work first | Then | Finally |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crochet the head, plan the face and install or embroider the eyes | Work the narrower neck and patchwork body | Stuff and close the base |
| Make two wings and four ear-tuft pieces | Make the beak and two feet | Pin, check symmetry, and sew |

**Toy-safety note:** Plastic eyes can loosen and are inappropriate for babies and young children. Embroider facial features securely for a child-oriented project; inspect seams and follow applicable toy-safety requirements. A pattern does not certify a finished toy as safe.

## Part 1 — A rounded taupe crown and creamy face {#make-the-pieces}

Begin at the **top center** with taupe yarn. This section expands evenly before the two cream face areas appear.

1. **Rnd 1:** Make 6 sc into an MR. Pull the ring closed. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in each stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *(1 sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *(3 sc, inc)* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *(4 sc, inc)* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *(5 sc, inc)* six times. **[42]**
8. **Rnd 8:** Repeat *(6 sc, inc)* six times. **[48]**
9. **Rnd 9:** Repeat *(7 sc, inc)* six times. **[54]**

For the next rounds, **write the color sequence across the full 54-stitch round**. The beginning-of-round marker should sit roughly at the back; the cream area will be opposite it, at the front. The central little strip of taupe makes the top of the cream face form a gentle heart shape.

10. **Rnd 10:** Taupe **20 sc**; cream **6 sc**; taupe **2 sc**; cream **6 sc**; taupe **20 sc**. **[54]**
11. **Rnd 11:** Taupe **18 sc**; cream **8 sc**; taupe **2 sc**; cream **8 sc**; taupe **18 sc**. **[54]**
12. **Rnd 12:** Taupe **16 sc**; cream **10 sc**; taupe **2 sc**; cream **10 sc**; taupe **16 sc**. **[54]**
13. **Rnd 13:** Taupe **14 sc**; cream **12 sc**; taupe **2 sc**; cream **12 sc**; taupe **14 sc**. **[54]**
14. **Rnds 14–21:** For **each of eight rounds**, crochet **12 taupe, 30 cream, 12 taupe**. **[54 per round]**

**Count check:** The cream sections grow from two 6-stitch patches into one broad 30-stitch front panel. Each color sequence always adds up to 54. Don't increase the round to make the heart point wider.

**A note on color changes:** To start a new color neatly, stop the previous stitch when **two loops remain on the hook**, then draw the new color through those loops. The next complete stitch belongs to the new color. On stuffed toys, keep floats loose on the inside and secure longer carries rather than pulling them tightly behind the face.

### Pause for Pippa's expression

Before narrowing the head, mark its front center. The face area is roughly **the middle of the cream panel**. Temporarily position the eyes **between Rounds 17 and 18**, approximately **14–16 stitches apart**, with the beak centered between them. Move the markers until the expression looks balanced from both sides.

If using plastic display eyes, lock the washers **now**, while you can reach inside the head. If using embroidered eyes, you can stitch them later, but mark the points now.

Continue the lower face in **cream**, without carrying the taupe yarn farther down:

15. **Rnd 22:** Repeat *(7 sc, dec)* six times. **[48]**
16. **Rnd 23:** Repeat *(6 sc, dec)* six times. **[42]**
17. **Rnd 24:** Repeat *(5 sc, dec)* six times. **[36]**

At this stage the head is an open rounded shape that narrows to **36 stitches** at the neck. Add stuffing to the head **now**: begin at the top and work outward in little wisps so the cream cheeks don't bulge unevenly.

**Quick fix:** If the face patch looks slanted, first inspect your stitch marker position and whether every color block totaled 54. Continuous spiral stitches naturally lean a little; that is not automatically an error.

## Part 2 — Build the patchwork belly

Change back to **taupe**. We increase just enough to make a smaller, rounded body underneath the big head.

18. **Rnd 25:** Repeat *(5 sc, inc)* six times. **[42]**

For each of the next nine rounds, work:

**10 taupe sc + a 22-stitch patchwork panel + 10 taupe sc = 42 stitches.**

The front panel is worked stitch by stitch. Here is an **independent, repeatable chart** for this version. The letters tell you which color to use for **each stitch in the 22-stitch front panel**, reading from left to right as worked:

- **C** = cream
- **P** = peach / apricot
- **M** = dusty mauve

| Panel | Color sequence (22 stitches) | Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | P×5, C×4, P×4, C×4, P×5 | 22 |
| **B** | P×4, M×3, C×3, P×2, C×3, M×3, P×4 | 22 |
| **C** | C×4, P×4, C×6, P×4, C×4 | 22 |
| **D** | M×4, P×4, C×6, P×4, M×4 | 22 |

**Read each line as a whole round:** 10 taupe stitches, then the selected 22-stitch panel sequence, then 10 taupe stitches.

19. **Rnd 26:** Panel **A** between the two taupe borders. **[42]**
20. **Rnd 27:** Panel **B**. **[42]**
21. **Rnd 28:** Panel **C**. **[42]**
22. **Rnd 29:** Panel **D**. **[42]**
23. **Rnd 30:** Panel **A**. **[42]**
24. **Rnd 31:** Panel **B**. **[42]**
25. **Rnd 32:** Panel **C**. **[42]**
26. **Rnd 33:** Panel **D**. **[42]**
27. **Rnd 34:** Panel **A**. **[42]**

You can cut yarn between distant color changes if carrying it would make a tangled nest inside Pippa. Keep enough tail to secure ends. If you want an easier first try, work the entire **22-stitch front panel in cream** for the same nine rounds. The shaping will be identical; only the colors change.

### Shape and close the base

Change to **taupe throughout**.

28. **Rnds 35–36:** Work 42 sc in each of two rounds. **[42 per round]**
29. **Rnd 37:** Repeat *(5 sc, dec)* six times. **[36]**
30. **Rnd 38:** Repeat *(4 sc, dec)* six times. **[30]**
31. **Rnd 39:** Repeat *(3 sc, dec)* six times. **[24]**

Add fiberfill to the body gradually. Keep the bottom softly rounded so Pippa can sit without becoming rigid. Finish closing:

32. **Rnd 40:** Repeat *(2 sc, dec)* six times. **[18]**
33. **Rnd 41:** Repeat *(1 sc, dec)* six times. **[12]**
34. **Rnd 42:** Decrease six times. **[6]**

Fasten off with a tail. With your tapestry needle, gather the **front loops of the last six stitches**, pull closed, and secure the yarn inside.

**The useful check:** From a front view, the face, beak position and 22-stitch belly panel should share the same vertical centerline. If the spiral shifted a little, adjust the visual alignment with wing and foot placement before sewing.

## Part 3 — A soft wing on each side (make 2)

The pictured owl has round, slightly drooping wings rather than pointed feathers. We will make two separate, gently tapered pieces in **taupe**.

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc into an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase all six stitches. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *(1 sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnds 4–6:** Work 18 sc in each of three rounds. **[18 per round]**
5. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *(4 sc, dec)* three times. **[15]**
6. **Rnds 8–9:** Work 15 sc in each round. **[15 per round]**
7. **Rnd 10:** Repeat *(3 sc, dec)* three times. **[12]**
8. **Rnds 11–12:** Crochet 12 sc per round. **[12 per round]**
9. **Rnd 13:** Repeat *(2 sc, dec)* three times. **[9]**
10. **Rnd 14:** Crochet 9 sc. **[9]**

Leave a long sewing tail. **Do not pack the wing full of stuffing.** A tiny wisp near the rounded tip is enough, or leave it unstuffed for a naturally flat look. Flatten the 9-stitch opening and keep it ready for sewing; you don't need to force an odd number of stitches into a closed crocheted seam.

Make the second wing identical and compare them side by side before attaching.

## Part 4 — Four little ear tufts

What looks like one ear on either side is actually a **pair of small tubes**: one taller, one shorter. Make two of each in **taupe**.

### Taller tuft (make 2)

1. **Rnd 1:** 5 sc in an MR. **[5]**
2. **Rnd 2:** 1 sc, inc, 1 sc, inc, 1 sc. **[7]**
3. **Rnds 3–7:** Make 7 sc per round. **[7 per round]**

### Shorter tuft (make 2)

1. **Rnd 1:** 5 sc in an MR. **[5]**
2. **Rnd 2:** 1 sc, inc, 1 sc, inc, 1 sc. **[7]**
3. **Rnds 3–5:** Crochet 7 sc per round. **[7 per round]**

Fasten off each piece with a long attachment tail. Leave the tubes unstuffed, or use only the smallest tuft of filling near their tips. Flatten their open ends gently and arrange one tall and one short together for each corner of the head.

## Part 5 — Two little feet

Make **two** matching feet in taupe.

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Inc in every stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnds 3–4:** Work 12 sc each round. **[12 per round]**
4. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *(1 sc, dec)* four times. **[8]**

Add a small wisp of stuffing. Fasten off with a sewing tail and pinch the opening closed with a few small stitches. The feet should look like little rounded pebbles, not tall boots.

## Part 6 — A rose-pink beak

Make one tiny beak in **rose pink**.

1. **Rnd 1:** Work 5 sc into an MR. **[5]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Crochet 5 sc. **[5]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Increase every stitch. **[10]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Crochet 10 sc. **[10]**

Do **not** stuff. Flatten lightly. Fasten off with a long tail for sewing. The narrow starting tip points down; the flattened wider end sits against the center of the face. If you prefer a shorter beak, you may omit Round 4; it will be flatter and shorter.

## Part 7 — Optional tiny tail

A tail can help your owl look finished from behind. It is not needed for the front-view photos.

1. With taupe, make 6 sc in an MR. **[6]**
2. Increase all six stitches. **[12]**
3. Work 12 sc for two rounds. **[12 per round]**

Flatten and fasten off with a tail. Sew the flattened edge low on the back, a few rounds above the base, without allowing it to tip the owl forward.

## Part 8 — Pin, check, then sew

1. **Start with the face.** Pin the pink beak centrally onto the cream patch at approximately **Rounds 17–21**, below and between the eyes. Check symmetry before sewing. Add two little blush-colored horizontal embroidery marks beneath the outer corners of the eyes.
2. **Arrange the tufts.** Pin one tall and one short tuft at each top corner of the head, approximately along **Rounds 3–7**. Let the pairs fan apart a little. Check from the front **and** from above.
3. **Place the wings.** Position each narrow, flattened wing edge on the side of the body near the lower cheeks/upper body, around **Rounds 24–28**. Let the fuller end droop downward. The wings should frame, not hide, the colorful front panel.
4. **Add the feet.** Pin them near the bottom front over approximately **Rounds 36–40**, evenly spaced around the panel's centerline.
5. **Optional tail.** Place it on the back, then check that Pippa still sits securely.
6. **Sew gradually.** Use the existing long tails to stitch each piece firmly through the surface loops. Pause after a few stitches, compare both sides, and hide the tails securely.

## Little fixes for common owl mishaps

| What you're seeing | What to try first |
| --- | --- |
| The cream face doesn't look symmetrical | Check that you made the full color sequence on every 54-stitch round; center the beak between the marked eye positions |
| The color panel is crooked | Find the **start-of-round marker** and the 10-taupe / 22-pattern / 10-taupe sequence; check for unplanned increases at color changes |
| Mauve or peach yarn shows through the taupe sides | Shorten or secure long carries on the inside rather than pulling them across the whole tube |
| The beak sticks out too far | Leave it unstuffed and sew the broad edge flatter against the face |
| One wing is longer | Compare round counts, especially Rounds 8–12, before sewing either wing |
| The ears flop over | Avoid heavy stuffing in the tufts; reinforce the bases with a second row of tiny stitches |
| Stuffing peeks through the head | Check fabric density with [our tension guide](/learn/controlling-crochet-tension/) rather than simply pulling the yarn harder |
| The owl rolls forward | Shift a little stuffing toward the back of the base and re-check the feet before securing them |

## A few ideas to make Pippa your own

Try a **moss-green crown**, a **blue-and-cream belly**, or a completely **solid-color body**. The shaping stays the same because every round uses the same stitch totals. For a smaller owl, a lighter yarn and suitably small hook can help, but dimensions and eye sizes will change; see [how yarn weight changes amigurumi](/blog/how-yarn-weight-changes-amigurumi/).

And if Pippa looks a little different from the illustration, read [why your amigurumi looks different from the picture](/blog/why-your-amigurumi-looks-different/). Crochet stitch height, stuffing and finishing all make subtle changes to a character's expression.

**Thread & Petal's little reminder:** Count the stitches, trust the sewing pins, and give those tiny wings a moment to settle. The personality appears in the finishing touches.
