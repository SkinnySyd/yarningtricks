One small flower can brighten a tote bag, gift wrap or handmade accessory. This one uses a repeating petal shape, so after you finish the first petal, you'll know exactly what to do for the remaining four.

This is written in **US crochet terms**. You'll need *ch* (chain), *dc* (double crochet, called **treble** in UK terms), and *sl st* (slip stitch). See [How to Double Crochet](/learn/double-crochet-stitch/) or [Read Crochet Patterns](/learn/how-to-read-crochet-patterns/) if you want a refresher.

## Get your center ready

1. Make a **magic ring**, leaving a yarn tail long enough to tighten and weave in securely afterward.
2. Keep the ring open while you work the petals through its center.

If the adjustable ring is unfamiliar, follow our [Magic Ring & Crochet in the Round](/learn/magic-ring-crochet-in-the-round/) lesson first. Then return here to make the flower.

## The five-petal pattern

**Petal 1:**

1. **Chain 2** to begin the petal.
2. Work **2 double crochets into the center of the ring**.
3. **Chain 2** again to bring the petal back down.
4. Work **1 slip stitch into the ring** to anchor the petal.

**Petals 2–5:** Repeat the **same four steps four more times**, always working the double crochets and the anchoring slip stitch into the ring's center.

You should now see **five little petal fans**, each with **2 double crochets** in the middle. Keep the working yarn relaxed as you make the chain stitches, so each petal has room to open.

## Close the center and finish

Gently pull the magic-ring tail until the center closes and the petals sit close together. Fasten off, then weave both tails back and forth under several stitches on the wrong side so the flower stays secure.

If you plan to attach the flower to a bag or hat, leave a long tail for sewing instead of weaving it all in immediately.

## Troubleshooting a tiny flower

- **Only four petals?** Count each anchoring slip stitch into the ring. You need five petal repeats.
- **A big hole in the center?** Hold the petals steady while you tighten the magic-ring tail; weave that tail in securely.
- **Petals curling tightly?** Try a slightly larger hook or loosen the chain stitches at the start and end of each petal.
- **Uneven petal sizes?** Count **2 dc in each petal** and practice keeping the chain tension consistent.

## Make a little bouquet

Try two colors of yarn or make several flowers in different sizes by changing your yarn and hook. Attach them to gift tags, clothing or accessories, and use your yarn label's washing guidance for the finished item.
