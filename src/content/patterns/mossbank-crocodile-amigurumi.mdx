Mossbank is a little crocodile who has never been in a hurry. You'll find him floating beside the reeds, enjoying the warm morning sun and thinking about his next afternoon nap. His wide smile, rounded eyes and tiny little back ridges make him a very friendly pond-side companion.

This is an **original Thread & Petal pattern** based on general crocodile shapes, not an adaptation of the published Charlie the Crocodile's individual round instructions. The page's image is **illustration rather than a photograph of a finished crochet sample**. Stitch totals have been checked arithmetically, but the design **has not been test-crocheted** and you may need small fit adjustments during assembly.

## Your stitch basket

- **Level:** Intermediate, mainly because of the separately sewn snout, jaw and tail.
- **US terms:** **MR** = magic ring, **sc** = single crochet, **inc** = 2 sc in one stitch, **dec** = decrease over 2 stitches, **ch** = chain, **sl st** = slip stitch, **FO** = fasten off.
- **Construction:** Separate round head, long oval upper muzzle, flat lower jaw, bottom-up body with cream tummy, four paws, tapering tail and embroidered back markings.
- **Round counts:** Numbers in **[brackets]** show the stitch total after each round. Work in a continuous spiral unless directions explicitly switch to rows.
- **Size:** The finished size depends on your yarn and hook; no sample measurement is available.

Keep the [crochet tension lesson](/learn/controlling-crochet-tension/) handy. This pattern benefits from a snug stitch, especially around the muzzle where you'll add just enough stuffing to keep the shape.

## Part 1 — A round, friendly crocodile head

Start at the crown in **sage green**.

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** *(sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** *(3 sc, inc)* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** *(4 sc, inc)* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** *(5 sc, inc)* six times. **[42]**
8. **Rnd 8:** *(6 sc, inc)* six times. **[48]**
9. **Rnd 9:** *(7 sc, inc)* six times. **[54]**
10. **Rnd 10–16:** Sc all around. **[54 per round]**
11. **Rnd 17:** *(7 sc, dec)* six times. **[48]**
12. **Rnd 18:** *(6 sc, dec)* six times. **[42]**

**Face checkpoint:** With the beginning-of-round marker toward the back, mark the face center on the opposite side. The two eyes will sit **above** the muzzle, rather than inside it. Embroider little black eyes near Rnds 12–14, checking that the front is level. Any eye bumps can be added after stuffing.

13. **Rnd 19:** *(5 sc, dec)* six times. **[36]**
14. **Rnd 20:** *(4 sc, dec)* six times. **[30]**
15. **Rnd 21:** *(3 sc, dec)* six times. **[24]**

Stuff the crown evenly. Gently spread the filling toward the sides; an overstuffed head makes it harder to sew on a flat jaw.

16. **Rnd 22:** *(2 sc, dec)* six times. **[18]**
17. **Rnd 23:** *(sc, dec)* six times. **[12]**
18. **Rnd 24:** Dec six times. **[6]**

FO with a tail, close by threading through the remaining front loops, and weave the tail inside.

### Two raised eye bumps

Make **two**, in sage green:

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Inc all around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Sc around. **[12]**
4. **Rnd 4:** *(2 sc, dec)* three times. **[9]**

FO leaving long sewing tails. Put the eye bumps just above the snout on either side of the head; add one small dark embroidered pupil on each. These are optional if you prefer a flatter face, but they help create the little crocodile silhouette in our cover illustration.

## Part 2 — A broad oval smiling muzzle

Crocheting around a foundation chain creates a true **oval**, rather than a circle. With sage green:

1. **Foundation:** Ch **9**.
2. **Rnd 1:** In the **second chain from the hook**, work 2 sc; sc in the next **6 chains**; work 3 sc in the final chain. Rotate around the foundation and work 1 sc in each of the next **6 underside loops**, then **1 sc in the underside of the first worked chain**. **[18]**
3. **Rnd 2:** Sc in each stitch. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *(inc, 7 sc)* twice. **[20]**
5. **Rnd 4–7:** Sc around. **[20 per round]**

FO leaving a long tail. Add a **very light** amount of stuffing to the wide end, then press the open edge into a gentle oval with your fingers. Pin the opening horizontally across the *lower front* of the head, keeping the center of the snout aligned with the eyes.

**Count check:** The first oval lap uses **2 + 6 + 3 + 6 + 1 = 18** stitches; the next increase round turns 18 into **20** without leaving a stitch unworked. The snout should extend outward, not look like a flat disc. Try placing the opening against the head before sewing and adjust the amount of fill.

### A flat pale lower jaw

With cream, make a **flat oval panel** using rows:

1. **Row 1:** Ch 11; starting in the second chain, sc across **10 stitches**. Ch 1 and turn. **[10]**
2. **Rows 2–4:** Sc 10. Ch 1 and turn after each of the first two rows. **[10 per row]**
3. **Row 5:** Dec, sc 6, dec. **[8]**
4. **Row 6:** Sc 8. **[8]**

FO with a sewing tail. Pin this panel *under* the upper muzzle with the narrow end toward the face. Sew along its side edges to the snout and the front of the head, leaving the front lip edge gently curved. This is a **soft decorative lower jaw**, not a movable hinged mouth.

For a more dimensional look, tuck a tiny bit of fiberfill between jaw and upper muzzle before closing. A slight curve is lovely; a tightly stuffed wedge will look too stiff.

### Four tiny teeth

Instead of loose tiny pieces, **embroider** two small ivory triangles along each side of the seam where the jaw meets the muzzle. Keep the triangles short and firm, with no projecting ends. The book's four-tooth idea is a familiar crocodile feature; here the technique is simplified to avoid tiny removable pieces.

For the nostrils, embroider two short forest-green ovals near the **top front** of the muzzle. Check symmetry from above.

## Part 3 — A round, seated body

Work the body upward from the base in sage green.

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Inc all around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** *(sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** *(3 sc, inc)* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** *(4 sc, inc)* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** *(5 sc, inc)* six times. **[42]**
8. **Rnd 8:** *(6 sc, inc)* six times. **[48]**
9. **Rnd 9–13:** Sc all around. **[48 per round]**
10. **Rnd 14:** *(6 sc, dec)* six times. **[42]**
11. **Rnd 15–16:** Sc all around. **[42 per round]**
12. **Rnd 17:** *(5 sc, dec)* six times. **[36]**
13. **Rnd 18–19:** Sc all around. **[36 per round]**
14. **Rnd 20:** *(4 sc, dec)* six times. **[30]**
15. **Rnd 21–22:** Sc all around. **[30 per round]**
16. **Rnd 23:** *(3 sc, dec)* six times. **[24]**
17. **Rnd 24:** Sc around. **[24]**

FO with a **long sewing tail**, leaving the top opening **unclosed** for joining to the head. Stuff the lower part more firmly and the neck more lightly.

### Soft cream belly piece

Make a flat cream oval by working in rows:

1. **Row 1:** Ch 7; beginning in the second chain, sc across **6**. Ch 1 and turn. **[6]**
2. **Row 2:** Inc, sc 4, inc; ch 1, turn. **[8]**
3. **Row 3:** Inc, sc 6, inc; ch 1, turn. **[10]**
4. **Row 4:** Inc, sc 8, inc; ch 1, turn. **[12]**
5. **Rows 5–11:** Sc 12 in each row; ch 1 and turn between rows. **[12 per row]**
6. **Row 12:** Dec, sc 8, dec; ch 1, turn. **[10]**
7. **Row 13:** Dec, sc 6, dec; ch 1, turn. **[8]**
8. **Row 14:** Dec, sc 4, dec. **[6]**

FO leaving a sewing tail. Pin the pale panel vertically at the front of the body, with its upper narrow end tucked beneath the head seam. Sew all around the edge using small stitches.

**Helpful tip:** If the panel bunches, ease it along the natural curve of the body rather than pulling the fabric tight. You can also read our note on [stuffing amigurumi neatly](/blog/the-little-art-of-stuffing-amigurumi/).

## Part 4 — Four rounded crocodile paws

### Front paws (make two)

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Inc around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** *(sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4–5:** Sc around. **[18 per round]**
5. **Rnd 6:** *(4 sc, dec)* three times. **[15]**
6. **Rnd 7–10:** Sc around. **[15 per round]**
7. **Rnd 11:** *(3 sc, dec)* three times. **[12]**
8. **Rnd 12:** Sc around. **[12]**

Stuff the rounded paw ends a little, leaving their upper ends flatter. FO with long sewing tails.

### Hind paws (make two)

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Inc around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** *(sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5–7:** Sc around. **[24 per round]**
6. **Rnd 8:** *(2 sc, dec)* six times. **[18]**
7. **Rnd 9–10:** Sc around. **[18 per round]**
8. **Rnd 11:** *(4 sc, dec)* three times. **[15]**
9. **Rnd 12:** Sc around. **[15]**

Lightly stuff the toe ends and flatten each opening. FO with tails.

**Little detail:** Embroider three short pale-green lines at the rounded toe of each paw for soft nails. Embroidered lines are less fiddly and more secure than crocheting twelve tiny nail pieces.

## Part 5 — A tapered tail

Start **at the narrow tip** in sage green. This means the opening grows larger as you work toward its attachment point.

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2–3:** Sc around. **[6 per round]**
3. **Rnd 4:** *(sc, inc)* three times. **[9]**
4. **Rnd 5–6:** Sc around. **[9 per round]**
5. **Rnd 7:** *(2 sc, inc)* three times. **[12]**
6. **Rnd 8–9:** Sc around. **[12 per round]**
7. **Rnd 10:** *(3 sc, inc)* three times. **[15]**
8. **Rnd 11–12:** Sc around. **[15 per round]**
9. **Rnd 13:** *(4 sc, inc)* three times. **[18]**
10. **Rnd 14–15:** Sc around. **[18 per round]**
11. **Rnd 16:** *(5 sc, inc)* three times. **[21]**
12. **Rnd 17–18:** Sc around. **[21 per round]**
13. **Rnd 19:** *(6 sc, inc)* three times. **[24]**
14. **Rnd 20–22:** Sc around. **[24 per round]**
15. **Rnd 23:** *(7 sc, inc)* three times. **[27]**
16. **Rnd 24–26:** Sc around. **[27 per round]**
17. **Rnd 27:** *(8 sc, inc)* three times. **[30]**
18. **Rnd 28–30:** Sc around. **[30 per round]**

Stuff a little at a time, keeping the final round near the attachment softer. FO leaving a long tail. Flatten the opening slightly and pin to the back of the lower body.

**Balance check:** A long, heavy tail can tip the crocodile backward. Try it pinned on first, then adjust the hind feet and reduce the tail stuffing if needed. You can use hidden stitches to curve the tail slightly to one side.

### Two rows of soft back ridges

This simplified detail uses **embroidery rather than separate pieces**.

With dark green yarn, embroider a line of small upward-pointing **V stitches** along the upper side of the tail, continuing onto the back. Make a second staggered line beside the first. Work each V through neighboring crochet stitches so the ridges stay firmly attached.

If you're decorating a toddler's toy, avoid any plastic eyes or loosely attached components. Use well-secured embroidered details and inspect all seams.

## Part 6 — Put Mossbank together

1. **Pin and sew the cream tummy panel** onto the body.
2. **Join the head to the body**, using the open 24-stitch body edge at the underside of the finished head. Sew gradually around and add a small wisp of stuffing near the neck before closing the last gap.
3. **Attach the muzzle and flat jaw** to the center front of the head. Align the top of the muzzle below the eyes, and pin the whole assembly before sewing. Add nostrils and embroidered teeth once its position looks right.
4. **Sew the front paws** just behind the muzzle's sides, around the upper half of the body.
5. **Attach the hind paws** low on the sides and angled a little forward, then stand Mossbank on a flat table to test his balance.
6. **Attach the tail** securely at the lower rear and add the two rows of dark-green embroidered ridges.
7. Recheck each seam and hide all yarn ends inside the body.

For an extra refresher, try [joining amigurumi pieces](/learn/sew-amigurumi-pieces-together/) before sewing on the long muzzle and tail.

## Troubleshooting at the pond edge

| If you notice… | Try this |
| --- | --- |
| The snout points down | Pin it slightly higher before sewing and keep its stuffing light |
| The jaw puckers | Remove a little filling, flatten the cream panel and stitch along its edges loosely |
| The belly edge curls | Use small sewing stitches through neighboring body stitches without pulling them tight |
| One paw is larger | Count the even-round stretches; the two front paws must match, as must the two hind paws |
| The tail pulls the body backward | Leave the tail's base lightly filled and move hind paws forward for support |

## Your own little crocodile

Make Mossbank a darker forest green, choose pale blue for the belly or embroider sleepy crescent eyes. The pattern gives you a gentle starting shape; your stitches will make the expression your own.

If the finished result doesn't exactly resemble the cover illustration, remember that [yarn, hook size and stuffing all affect amigurumi shape](/blog/why-your-amigurumi-looks-different/). Pause to pin and adjust before sewing—those quiet finishing decisions matter just as much as the rounds themselves.
