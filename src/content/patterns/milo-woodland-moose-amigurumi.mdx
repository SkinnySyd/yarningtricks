With his enormous antlers, gently drooping ears and the cutest blue bow, **Milo the Woodland Moose** looks ready for a little forest party. The shape is full of character: a rounded head with a soft beige face, a blue body made for sitting, and two curved front hooves resting beside his tummy.

This project is especially satisfying if you like seeing small, simple parts turn into something quite expressive. We'll build the head, shape the branching antlers, layer the ears, make the body and limbs, and finish with a bow that makes the entire moose feel dressed for an occasion. The trickiest moment is probably positioning the antlers—**the stitch markers help us work out where each branch belongs**.

**About the photographs:** The project pictures show a **warm beige-and-brown alternative colorway** with a blue bow. The round-by-round directions below follow the book's original **beige face, light-blue head and body, medium-blue bow and soft-brown ear lining**. Use those colors for the classic result, or substitute warmer shades for a moose resembling the pictures. The photographed yarn colors aren't additional instructions.

## Your moose at a glance

- **Finished height:** approximately **15 cm / 6 in**, using the yarn and hook suggested in the source.
- **Skill level:** **intermediate**. Most pieces use single crochet, but the head's directional shaping, ear joins and multi-part antlers take some concentration.
- **Hook:** **2.5 mm (US B-1)**.
- **Yarn weight:** **fingering weight**. A heavier yarn and larger hook will give you a bigger toy, but the measurements will change.
- **Construction:** head worked from its beige muzzle toward the light-blue crown; two main antlers plus four smaller branches; separately stuffed body, two arms, two seated legs, paired inner and outer ears, one tail and a two-piece bow.
- **Terms:** **US crochet terminology**. Work in a continuous spiral unless a section specifically tells you to turn or flatten the work. The number in **[square brackets]** is your stitch total at that point.

Keep a stitch marker in the first stitch of every round. For this moose, a *second* marker will come in handy when we mark the sides of the face and the top of the antlers. If spiral rounds are new, our [magic-ring tutorial](/learn/magic-ring-crochet-in-the-round/) is a good companion.

## Materials and colors

| Color | Where you'll use it |
|---|---|
| **Beige** | Lower head/muzzle, large main antlers and both side branches, hands and feet |
| **Light blue** | Upper head, body, upper arms, upper legs, outer ears and tail |
| **Soft brown** | Inner ear layers |
| **Medium blue** | Bow tie and its center band |
| **Black embroidery thread** | Tiny angled eyebrows |

You'll also need two **7.5 mm safety eyes**, fiberfill, a stitch marker, additional removable markers or pins, scissors and a tapestry needle. Safety eyes have small parts and are not appropriate for toys for children under three; embroider eyes instead where needed.

## Stitch abbreviations

| Short form | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **MR** | Magic ring |
| **sc** | Single crochet |
| **inc** | Place 2 sc into the same stitch |
| **dec** | Single-crochet decrease (preferably invisible) |
| **ch** | Chain |
| **sl st** | Slip stitch |
| **rnd / row** | Round / row |
| **st / sts** | Stitch / stitches |
| **[n]** | Stitch count after this instruction |

For shaping help, see [crochet increases and decreases](/learn/crochet-increases-and-decreases/). When you find a sequence such as *5 sc, inc* repeated eight times, work the five singles and one increase eight times in total.

## Part 1 — Make the beige muzzle and blue head

Unlike moose patterns with a separate sewn-on snout, Milo's **muzzle grows directly into his head**. Begin with **beige yarn** at the rounded front of his face. We work outward until the head reaches 56 stitches, taper it a little along its sides, and then switch to light blue for the wider upper head.

### Start at the front of the muzzle

1. **Rnd 1:** Form an MR and make **8 sc** into its center. Pull it closed. **[8]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Place an increase in all 8 stitches. **[16]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 sc, inc* eight times. **[24]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *2 sc, inc* eight times. **[32]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *3 sc, inc* eight times. **[40]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *4 sc, inc* eight times. **[48]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *5 sc, inc* eight times. **[56]**
8. **Rnds 8–13:** Crochet one sc into each of the 56 stitches for **six straight rounds**. **[56 each round]**

You'll now have a generous, open cup. The next few rounds take **one decrease at each side**, rather than decreasing evenly all around. That makes the face look slightly flattened from the front and provides a helpful guide when you position the eyes.

9. **Rnd 14:** Crochet **13 sc**, then make **1 dec and mark it**. Work **26 sc**, make another **dec and mark it**, and finish with **13 sc**. You have two side markers and **54 stitches**. **[54]**
10. **Rnd 15:** Work one sc in every stitch. **[54]**
11. **Rnd 16:** Crochet **13 sc, dec, 25 sc, dec, 12 sc**. **[52]**
12. **Rnd 17:** Crochet **12 sc, dec, 24 sc, dec, 12 sc**. **[50]**
13. **Rnd 18:** Crochet **12 sc, dec, 23 sc, dec, 11 sc**. **[48]**

**Check before changing colors:** The **two markers placed in Round 14** identify the sides of the head. They will help you find the front and keep the eyes symmetric. Don't remove both markers yet.

### Widen and finish the head in light blue

Switch to **light-blue yarn** for the remainder of the head.

14. **Rnd 19:** Repeat *11 sc, inc* four times. **[52]**
15. **Rnd 20:** Repeat *12 sc, inc* four times. **[56]**
16. **Rnd 21:** Make **14 sc**, repeat *6 sc, inc* four times, then make **14 sc**. **[60]**
17. **Rnd 22:** Work **6 sc, inc, 7 sc**; repeat *7 sc, inc* four times; then work **7 sc, inc, 6 sc**. **[66]**
18. **Rnd 23:** Repeat *10 sc, inc* six times. **[72]**
19. **Rnds 24–30:** Crochet 72 sc for **seven rounds**, without shaping. **[72 each round]**

**Add the expression while you can still reach inside.** Using the side markers as a guide, place the **7.5 mm safety eyes between Rounds 19 and 20**, with about **five stitches separating them**. Check the face from directly in front before attaching the washers. With black thread, embroider two short, diagonal eyebrows across **Round 21**; each brow should be approximately **three stitches long**.

Let's round off the back of the head:

20. **Rnd 31:** Repeat *10 sc, dec* six times. **[66]**
21. **Rnd 32:** Repeat *9 sc, dec* six times. **[60]**
22. **Rnd 33:** Repeat *8 sc, dec* six times. **[54]**
23. **Rnd 34:** Repeat *7 sc, dec* six times. **[48]**
24. **Rnd 35:** Crochet 48 sc, keeping the head's shape steady for one round. **[48]**
25. **Rnd 36:** Repeat *6 sc, dec* six times. **[42]**
26. **Rnd 37:** Repeat *5 sc, dec* six times. **[36]**
27. **Rnd 38:** Repeat *4 sc, dec* six times. **[30]**
28. **Rnd 39:** Repeat *3 sc, dec* six times. **[24]**

Fill the head with fiberfill now, starting at the broad beige front so it stays rounded rather than creased. Continue adding a little stuffing as the opening gets smaller.

29. **Rnd 40:** Repeat *2 sc, dec* six times. **[18]**
30. **Rnd 41:** Repeat *1 sc, dec* six times. **[12]**
31. **Rnd 42:** Work six decreases. **[6]**

Slip stitch once in the next stitch and fasten off. Run the yarn tail through the remaining **front loops**, pull the small opening closed and hide the end inside the head.

**Little reminder:** The beige area is already the muzzle. You **don't** need to crochet an extra snout and sew it over the head.

## Part 2 — Build the two branching antlers

Each side of Milo's head needs **three pieces**: one long main antler, one medium branch and one small branch. Make **two complete sets in beige**. The main antler's gentle bend is formed with carefully placed decreases, so keep the marker showing which side is the top.

### Main antler (make 2)

1. **Rnd 1:** Make **6 sc** in an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in every stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 sc, inc* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *2 sc, inc* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *3 sc, inc* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnds 6–10:** Work 30 sc in each of the next five rounds. **[30]**
7. **Rnd 11:** Crochet **10 sc, dec, 2 sc, dec, 2 sc, dec, 10 sc**. Place a removable marker in the **middle decrease**—that stitch identifies the **top of the antler** for assembly. **[27]**
8. **Rnd 12:** Work **11 sc, dec, 1 sc, dec, 11 sc**. **[25]**
9. **Rnd 13:** Make **7 sc**, repeat *dec, 1 sc* four times, and finish with **6 sc**. **[21]**

Start adding fiberfill to the wider end of the antler. Use small wisps so the piece curves naturally instead of becoming rigid.

10. **Rnd 14:** Make **7 sc**, repeat *dec, 1 sc* three times, then make **5 sc**. **[18]**
11. **Rnd 15:** Crochet 18 sc. **[18]**
12. **Rnd 16:** Make **7 sc**, repeat *dec, 1 sc* twice, then work **5 sc**. **[16]**
13. **Rnds 17–31:** Continue with **16 sc in each of the next 15 rounds**. **[16 each round]**

Keep the long stem evenly stuffed. Slip stitch once and fasten off with a generous sewing tail. Repeat for the matching antler.

### Medium antler branch (make 2)

The first side branch has a gently widened tip. Start another piece in beige:

1. **Rnd 1:** Work 6 sc into an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase all the way around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Crochet 12 sc. **[12]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *5 sc, inc* twice. **[14]**
5. **Rnds 5–8:** Make 14 sc in each of four rounds. **[14 each round]**

Add a little stuffing, slip stitch, and leave a long yarn tail for attaching the piece to its main antler.

### Small antler branch (make 2)

This branch is narrower and sits closer to the tip of the main antler.

1. **Rnd 1:** Crochet 6 sc into an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in all 6 stitches. **[12]**
3. **Rnds 3–7:** Work 12 sc in every round for five rounds. **[12 each round]**

Lightly stuff, slip stitch and fasten off with a sewing tail.

### Turn the pieces into a pair of antlers

- Pin **one medium branch** against the **main antler, across Rounds 17–22**.
- The marker you added to **Round 11** indicates which side should point upward, so check that the branch angles correctly before sewing.
- Position **one small branch across Rounds 25–29**, nearer the main antler's slender end.
- Sew the two branches to the main piece with small, firm stitches. Keep the open ends fully enclosed and hide all tails inside.
- Repeat to make the second antler, reversing its angle so both sides look balanced.

We'll attach the finished antlers to the head during assembly, once the ears are ready.

## Part 3 — Crochet Milo's rounded blue body

The body is a simple, generously stuffed pear shape: broad at the base and narrower where the head will sit. Pick up **light-blue yarn**. Work from the bottom upward.

1. **Rnd 1:** Crochet **6 sc** into a magic ring. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase all six stitches. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 sc, inc* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *2 sc, inc* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *3 sc, inc* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *4 sc, inc* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *5 sc, inc* six times. **[42]**
8. **Rnd 8:** Repeat *6 sc, inc* six times. **[48]**
9. **Rnd 9:** Repeat *7 sc, inc* six times. **[54]**
10. **Rnd 10:** Repeat *8 sc, inc* six times. **[60]**
11. **Rnd 11:** Repeat *9 sc, inc* six times. **[66]**
12. **Rnd 12:** Repeat *10 sc, inc* six times. **[72]**
13. **Rnds 13–18:** Crochet 72 sc in each of six rounds. **[72 each round]**

That forms the roomy sitting base. From here, decreases occur in **four groups**, not the usual six. This gives the body a gentle, gradual taper.

14. **Rnd 19:** Repeat *16 sc, dec* four times. **[68]**
15. **Rnd 20:** Crochet 68 sc. **[68]**
16. **Rnd 21:** Repeat *15 sc, dec* four times. **[64]**
17. **Rnd 22:** Crochet 64 sc. **[64]**
18. **Rnd 23:** Repeat *14 sc, dec* four times. **[60]**
19. **Rnd 24:** Crochet 60 sc. **[60]**
20. **Rnd 25:** Repeat *13 sc, dec* four times. **[56]**
21. **Rnd 26:** Repeat *12 sc, dec* four times. **[52]**
22. **Rnd 27:** Repeat *11 sc, dec* four times. **[48]**
23. **Rnd 28:** Repeat *10 sc, dec* four times. **[44]**
24. **Rnd 29:** Repeat *9 sc, dec* four times. **[40]**
25. **Rnd 30:** Work 40 sc evenly. **[40]**
26. **Rnd 31:** Repeat *8 sc, dec* four times. **[36]**
27. **Rnd 32:** Crochet 36 sc. **[36]**
28. **Rnd 33:** Repeat *7 sc, dec* four times. **[32]**
29. **Rnd 34:** Crochet 32 sc. **[32]**
30. **Rnd 35:** Repeat *6 sc, dec* four times. **[28]**
31. **Rnd 36:** Work 28 sc. **[28]**

Slip stitch into the next stitch, then fasten off with a **long sewing tail**. Stuff the whole body firmly, especially the base; this will help your moose sit without collapsing. The **28-stitch opening stays open** so it can be secured against the lower side of the head later.

## Part 4 — Make two arms with rounded beige hands

The arms start at the **beige hands** and continue up into slender **light-blue sleeves**. Make two identical pieces. Each one has a slight curved shape at the wrist and ends with a flattened seam.

1. **Rnd 1:** Make 6 sc in an MR using beige. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in every stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 sc, inc* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *2 sc, inc* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnds 5–8:** Work 24 sc per round for four rounds. **[24 each round]**
6. **Rnd 9:** Make **2 dec, 16 sc, 2 dec**. **[20]**
7. **Rnd 10:** Make **1 dec, 16 sc, 1 dec**. **[18]**
8. **Rnd 11:** Crochet **1 sc, dec, 13 sc, dec**. **[16]**
9. **Rnd 12:** Work **1 sc, dec, 11 sc, dec**. **[14]**

Change from beige to **light blue**. Leave the beige yarn end tucked inside the arm and keep your tension snug at the color join.

10. **Rnds 13–30:** Crochet 14 sc in each of the next **18 rounds**. **[14 each round]**

Fill the hand firmly, but give the slender blue arm only a **small amount of stuffing**. A soft upper arm lies more naturally at the moose's sides.

11. **Rnd 31 (partial round):** Crochet **9 sc only**, then stop. **[9 worked; do not finish the round]**
12. **Rnd 32 (flattening seam):** Press the arm opening flat, line up the front and back layers, then work **7 sc through both layers together**. **[7 joining stitches]**

Fasten off with a long tail. Repeat for the second arm. **The final two instructions deliberately aren't ordinary full rounds**—they position and close the arm for sewing.

## Part 5 — Crochet the two seated legs

Make **two legs**, starting in **beige** at the foot and changing to **light blue** for the upper leg. The design shapes a fuller front foot before tapering and expanding into a rounded upper thigh. Keep the increase placements as written to get that curve.

1. **Rnd 1:** Work 6 sc in an MR with beige yarn. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in each stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 sc, inc* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *2 sc, inc* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *3 sc, inc* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnds 6–10:** Make 30 sc in each of the next five rounds. **[30 each round]**
7. **Rnd 11:** Work **9 sc**, repeat *dec, 1 sc* four times, and finish with **9 sc**. **[26]**
8. **Rnd 12:** Work **7 sc**, repeat *dec, 1 sc* four times, then **7 sc**. **[22]**
9. **Rnd 13:** Work **8 sc**, repeat *dec, 1 sc* twice, then **8 sc**. **[20]**
10. **Rnd 14:** Crochet 20 sc. **[20]**

Change to **light blue** for the upper part of the leg.

11. **Rnds 15–22:** Work 20 sc in each of the next eight rounds. **[20 each round]**
12. **Rnd 23:** Work **11 sc, inc, inc, 7 sc**. **[22]**
13. **Rnd 24:** Work **11 sc, inc, 2 sc, inc, 7 sc**. **[24]**
14. **Rnds 25–26:** Crochet 24 sc in each round. **[24]**

Stuff the foot generously, then fill the upper leg so the thigh rounds out without feeling hard. Continue the decreases:

15. **Rnd 27:** Repeat *2 sc, dec* six times. **[18]**
16. **Rnd 28:** Repeat *1 sc, dec* six times. **[12]**
17. **Rnd 29:** Work six decreases. **[6]**

Slip stitch once, cut the yarn with a **long tail**, and pass it through the six final loops to pull the end closed. Then bring the long tail out on the **inside of the leg between Rounds 21 and 22**. This is the sewing thread you'll use to secure the shaped upper thigh to the body. Repeat for the second leg.

**Pairing tip:** Mark the *inside* of each leg before placing it against the body. You'll want the bulk of the heel facing the ground and the rounded thigh resting against the sides of Milo's belly.

## Part 6 — Make the soft, two-colored ears

Each ear has a **soft-brown inner oval** and a **light-blue outer oval**. Both pieces grow to 42 stitches before being worked together. You won't stuff these; the ear's shape comes from flattening and joining the layers.

### Inner ear (make 2 in soft brown)

1. **Rnd 1:** Make 6 sc in an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in every stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 sc, inc* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *2 sc, inc* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *3 sc, inc* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *4 sc, inc* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *5 sc, inc* six times. **[42]**

Slip stitch once, fasten off and neatly hide the yarn ends. Make a second inner oval.

### Outer ear (make 2 in light blue)

1. **Rnd 1:** Work 6 sc into an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in all six stitches. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 sc, inc* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *2 sc, inc* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *3 sc, inc* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *4 sc, inc* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *5 sc, inc* six times. **[42]**

**Do not fasten off the outer ear.** Lay one brown inner ear against the blue outer ear, with the **back sides touching**. You should see brown on one side and blue on the other. Match the stitch edges before continuing.

8. **Rnd 8 (joining the layers):** From the blue outer ear, make **1 ch**, then work **42 sc around the edge**, catching **both layers** as you go so that the inner piece stays attached. **[42]**
9. **Rnd 9 (forming the base):** Fold the paired ear in half. Work **2 sc through both folded layers**, then **1 sl st through the layers**. **[3 joining stitches]**

Fasten off with a long yarn tail for sewing. This last instruction forms the narrow attachment at the base—it **does not** mean crocheting a complete circular ninth round. Repeat to make the other ear.

## Part 7 — Add a tiny blue tail

Use **light-blue yarn**. Start with the little tip and widen into a small nub that will sit at Milo's lower back.

1. **Rnd 1:** Make 4 sc in an MR. **[4]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Repeat *1 sc, inc* twice. **[6]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 sc, inc* three times. **[9]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *2 sc, inc* three times. **[12]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Work 12 sc. **[12]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *1 sc, inc* six times. **[18]**
7. **Rnds 7–8:** Work 18 sc in each round. **[18 each round]**
8. **Rnd 9:** Repeat *4 sc, dec* three times. **[15]**
9. **Rnd 10:** Crochet 15 sc. **[15]**
10. **Rnd 11:** Repeat *3 sc, dec* three times. **[12]**
11. **Rnd 12:** Crochet 12 sc. **[12]**
12. **Rnd 13:** Repeat *2 sc, dec* three times. **[9]**

**Do not stuff the tail.** Flatten the opening, then:

13. **Rnd 14 (closing seam):** Crochet **4 sc through both layers together**. **[4 joining stitches]**

Fasten off with a sewing tail. The result is a soft, flattened stub rather than a round pom-pom.

## Part 8 — Crochet the little medium-blue bow tie

This is the detail that makes Milo ready for the woodland ball. You'll crochet **one long rectangular strip** and **one narrower center band**, then fold and secure the larger piece into a bow. Both are worked in rows using **medium-blue yarn**.

### Main bow strip

Leave a long starting yarn tail and **ch 55**.

1. **Row 1:** Beginning in the second chain from the hook, work **54 sc**. **[54]**
2. **Rows 2–10:** At the start of each row, **ch 1 and turn**, then crochet **54 sc** across. Repeat until you have ten rows total. **[54 per row]**

Fasten off, keeping another long sewing tail. The strip should look like a narrow, flexible rectangle.

### Center band

Leave a long starting tail and **ch 11**.

1. **Row 1:** Crochet **10 sc** starting in the second chain from the hook. **[10]**
2. **Rows 2–3:** **Ch 1, turn**, then work **10 sc** across. **[10 per row]**

Fasten off with a long tail.

### Shape the bow

Bring the **two short ends** of the longer strip together and sew them neatly with whip stitches to form a loop. Keep that seam against the body when the bow is attached.

Pinch the center so the fabric forms two rounded bow loops. Wrap the smaller band around the middle, cover the seam, and sew its ends together on the back. Put a couple of stitches through the bow itself so the center band can't slide. Keep your bow aside until the rest of Milo has been assembled.

## Part 9 — Assemble Milo, one detail at a time

Everything becomes much easier when you **pin all of the pieces first**. Put Milo on a flat table, choose his front and check each position from head-on and from the side.

1. **Finish the branching antlers.** If you haven't already, attach the medium branches across **Rounds 17–22** of the main antlers and the small branches across **Rounds 25–29**. Use the marker from **main-antler Round 11** to locate the top edge. Make the finished pair mirror each other.
2. **Sew on the antlers.** Position the main stems against both sides of the head across **head Rounds 29–33**. Counting between **head Rounds 30 and 31**, leave roughly **20 stitches between their inner edges**. Pin first so they don't tilt in opposite directions, then stitch securely.
3. **Attach the head to the body.** Seat the body opening against **head Rounds 20–28**. A tiny sideways tilt gives Milo a shy expression, but check the angle before sewing. Sew around the whole neck opening using the body yarn tail and add a little extra filling if needed.
4. **Sew the arms.** Place their flattened upper edges against **body Rounds 35–36**, keeping approximately **8 stitches between the arms on the front**. Turn the beige hands gently inward toward the tummy.
5. **Add the ears.** Sew the folded base of each two-layer ear to **head Rounds 26–29**, with the **front edges roughly 23 stitches apart**. Brown should face outward at the front of the ear; blue is the back.
6. **Position the legs.** Each rounded thigh rests against a side of the lower body. The **top of the thigh belongs near body Rounds 16–17**, while its rear extends a little behind the body's centerline. Sew around the curved top of the leg, then make extra small stitches around the *inside* where it touches the body so those stitches won't show from outside.
7. **Stitch on the tail.** Attach the flat tail edge to the back between **body Rounds 13 and 14**, centered between the two legs.
8. **Dress your moose.** Sew the finished bow tie onto the upper chest **just beneath the head**, making sure the gathered center faces forward and the seam stays hidden.
9. **Final check.** Tug each attachment gently to check it is secure, tidy away yarn tails, and fluff the ears and bow until the shape looks balanced.

**Important placement note:** The antler positions above refer to the **light-blue part of the head**, not the beige nose. The body, feet and ear instructions use their own round numbers, so keep those sections separate while placing pieces.

## Troubleshooting the fiddly parts

**My antlers aren't symmetrical.** Check the direction of the decreases in main-antler **Rounds 11–16**. The marker in Round 11 indicates the top, and the branches are attached on specifically numbered rounds; rotating one stem while sewing can make the pair look uneven.

**My head seems too long.** That's intentional up to a point. The first **18 rounds** form an integrated beige muzzle, and the light-blue head grows over it. Keep the side-decrease markers so you don't accidentally rotate the eyes to one side.

**The arms won't lie flat.** The top is flattened using a **partial Round 31 and a joining Round 32**. If you work all 14 stitches in Round 31, the arm seam ends up in the wrong place.

**One leg looks rounder than the other.** Compare the off-center decrease placement in **Rounds 11–13** and the two increases in **Rounds 23–24**. Those are shaping instructions, not decorative details.

**The ears seem to have too many stitches.** Both individual ovals finish with 42 stitches. Joining Round 8 is worked around those same 42 edge stitches; the three stitches in Round 9 close only the folded base. Don't invent another full round after folding.

**The bow looks like a rectangle.** The long strip is meant to be folded into a loop, then pinched and wrapped with the short center band. It becomes a bow during assembly, not while crocheting the rows.

**Milo falls forward when seated.** Before sewing the head permanently, position the legs and check the angle of the body. Spread the feet slightly wider and add stuffing to the base if the head and antlers make the toy front-heavy.
