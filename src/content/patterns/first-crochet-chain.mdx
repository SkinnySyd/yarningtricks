Every crochet project begins with one loop. In this short lesson you'll make a slip knot, create a chain of **15 stitches**, and learn how to tell whether your stitches are the right size. No previous experience is needed.

This guide uses **US crochet terms**. The chain stitch is called **chain (ch)** in both US and UK patterns. If you're completely new to yarn and hooks, visit [Crochet Supplies for Beginners](/learn/crochet-supplies-for-beginners/) first.

## Step 1 — Make a slip knot

1. Unwind a little yarn, leaving a tail approximately **15 cm / 6 in** long.
2. Cross the working yarn (the strand connected to the ball) over the tail to form a loop.
3. Reach through the loop and pull a small **loop of the working yarn** through. Don't pull the whole strand through.
4. Slide that new loop onto your hook. Gently pull the yarn to snug the knot up, but **leave the loop loose enough to glide on the hook**.

**Check your work:** you should have **one working loop** on the hook. The slip knot is not counted as your first chain stitch.

## Step 2 — Find a comfortable grip

Hold your hook like a pencil or a small knife—whichever feels more natural. Let the other hand guide the working yarn and hold the knot just below the hook so it doesn't twist.

Try not to grip the yarn too tightly. The loop on your hook should slide easily over the widest part of the hook's shaft.

## Step 3 — Make your first chain stitch

1. With one loop already on the hook, catch the working yarn with the hook. This is a **yarn over (yo)**.
2. Pull the caught yarn **through the loop that's already on the hook**.
3. One new loop stays on your hook, and **one chain stitch (ch)** sits just beneath it.
4. Repeat the same motion until you have a chain of **15 stitches**.

**Check your count:** look for little **V shapes** along the front of the chain. Each completed V is a chain stitch. **Do not count the working loop on your hook or the slip knot.**

## Step 4 — Practice an even chain

Make a second chain of 15 stitches. Compare the two: can you see the Vs more easily now? Do some stitches look larger than others? Aim for loops that are comfortable to work into—not squeezed tight against your hook.

**If your chain is very tight:** relax your yarn hand and form each new loop on the fuller part of your hook rather than at its tip.

**If your chain is loose or uneven:** keep the yarn moving steadily through your fingers, and hold your growing chain close to the hook.

**If your chain twists:** lay it flat and find the row of V shapes. Twisting is common while you're learning; you can straighten the chain before starting the next row.
