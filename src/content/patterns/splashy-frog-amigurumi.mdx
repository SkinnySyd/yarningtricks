Meet Splashy Frog: a long-legged, bright-eyed little friend who's always dressed for a dip! Its cheerful charm comes from the details: those funny eyes sitting high above the head, softly rounded hands with tiny fingers, blue-striped swimming shorts and a pair of sunshine-yellow flippers. It's a wonderful project if you've already crocheted a few round amigurumi pieces and are ready for something a bit more adventurous.

You'll create the **head and body as one continuous shape**, beginning with two small eye bumps. Later, you'll split the bottom into two long legs, crochet the arms and flippers separately, and add the shorts as their own garment. Work through one section at a time and let the stitch counts guide you.

## Before you pick up your hook

- **Finished size:** approximately **26 cm / 10.5 in tall** with the recommended yarn and tension.
- **Level:** intermediate. The eye-joining step, dividing the legs and making the shorts take a little concentration.
- **Terminology:** US crochet terms. In UK terminology, US *single crochet* is called *double crochet*.
- **Rounds:** work in a continuous spiral, using a marker, unless a section gives other instructions.
- **A note about the outfit:** the design includes a red-and-white jacquard underlayer beneath the separately crocheted shorts. This tutorial uses the designer's **solid-color alternative** for those covered rounds; it does not invent a replacement chart. You'll still make the blue-and-white striped shorts.

If you're unsure how to start or shape a round, our [magic ring tutorial](/learn/magic-ring-crochet-in-the-round/) and [increases and decreases lesson](/learn/crochet-increases-and-decreases/) are handy to keep open.

## Your stitch key (US terms)

| Abbreviation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **MR** | Magic ring |
| **sc** | Single crochet |
| **inc** | Two single crochet stitches in the same stitch |
| **dec** | Single crochet two stitches together |
| **ch** | Chain |
| **sl st** | Slip stitch |
| **BLO** | Back loop only |
| **st / sts** | Stitch / stitches |
| **rnd** | Round |
| **[ ]** | Total stitch count after the round |

When a step says to *repeat* a pair of stitches, complete that pair the stated number of times. Keep your stitches snug enough that the stuffing won't show through, but not so tight that they're difficult to work into.

## Part 1 — Crochet the white eye patches

Begin by preparing **two little white circles**. These are the whites that sit on top of the frog's raised green eyes.

1. **Rnd 1:** Make 6 single crochets into a magic ring. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in each of the six stitches. **[12]**

Slip stitch into the following stitch and fasten off, leaving a yarn tail long enough to sew each white circle down. Position a **10 mm safety eye in the center** of each circle, but **wait before attaching the washers**. You'll secure them after the green head is far enough along to check their placement.

For a child under three, use tightly embroidered eyes instead of plastic safety eyes; small detachable parts can pose a choking hazard.

## Part 2 — Grow the two green eye bumps

We're going to make two identical little rounded shapes and join them **without sewing**. This creates that fun frog silhouette with the eyes rising from the corners of its head.

With **sage-green yarn**, make the **first eye**:

1. **Rnd 1:** Crochet 6 sc into a magic ring. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in all six stitches. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 sc, then an increase in the next stitch* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnds 4–5:** Crochet 18 sc in each round without increasing. **[18]**

Fasten off the first eye and hide its yarn end. Make the **second eye** using the same five rounds, but **do not fasten off** this time. Leave your hook in the live loop: you'll use that yarn to join the two eyes next.

## Part 3 — Join the eyes and shape the head

**Rnd 6 is the special joining round.** Lay the eye bumps next to each other, keeping the openings facing down and the smooth sides forward. Make **6 chains** from the second eye to bridge the gap. Then work around both eye bumps and the chains:

1. **Rnd 6:** Chain 6. Work 1 sc into the last stitch of the first eye, then 1 sc into each of its next 17 stitches. Crochet 1 sc into each of the 6 connecting chains. Continue with 18 sc around the second eye, then crochet 1 sc into the remaining 6 chain loops on the opposite side of the bridge. **[48]**
2. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *7 sc, then an increase* six times. **[54]**
3. **Rnds 8–14:** Work one sc in each of the 54 stitches for seven full rounds. **[54]**

**Stop to add the eyes.** Place the safety-eye stems through the centers of the white circles and position them over **rounds 4–5 of the green eye bumps**. Check that both are looking in the same direction, fasten the washers inside the green fabric, and sew the white circles securely around the edges. Add a few small pink embroidery stitches to either cheek if you'd like.

Now continue widening the frog's lower face:

4. **Rnd 15:** Repeat *8 sc, then an increase* six times. **[60]**
5. **Rnds 16–26:** Crochet 60 sc in each round. These eleven even rounds turn the face into the frog's long, rounded torso. **[60]**
6. **Rnds 27–33:** Work 60 sc per round in **white** for a simple, solid underlayer. **[60]**

That last seven-round section is later covered by the separate striped shorts. The original designer also offers a red-and-white jacquard version, but a single color is an explicitly supported alternative.

**Counting checkpoint:** You should have **60 stitches** around the open bottom. Don't close it—we're about to divide those stitches into two legs. Stuff the upper head and torso gradually so they hold their shape.

## Part 4 — Make two long, slim green legs

This is the second unusual construction step: **the legs grow directly out of the body**, rather than being crocheted separately and sewn on.

At the end of Round 33, divide the 60 available stitches using removable markers: **15 stitches for the first leg**, **15 for the second**, and **15 stitches for each side of the gap between them**. Align the legs with the face before continuing. If the marker placement makes them look off-center, work a few extra sc—or undo a few—to adjust the start point.

### First leg

Bring together the front and back edges of the first 15-stitch leg opening. Work a sc to join across the opening, counting it as the first stitch of Round 34, and continue in a spiral.

1. **Rnd 34:** Work 15 sc around the first leg opening in **white**. **[15]**
2. **Rnd 35:** Change to **sage green** and crochet 15 sc through the **back loops only**. **[15]**
3. **Rnds 36–54:** Work 15 sc in each of these nineteen rounds. This is what gives Splashy Frog its long, skinny legs. **[15]**

Stuff the leg firmly enough to support the toy, then fasten off and leave a yarn tail for closing or securing the end.

### Second leg

Find the **16th unworked stitch at the back of Round 33**, as the source construction specifies, and attach **white yarn** there.

1. **Rnd 34:** Work 15 sc around the second opening, joining the back and front edges as you did for the first leg. **[15]**
2. **Rnd 35:** Change to **sage green**, working all 15 sc through the back loops only. **[15]**
3. **Rnds 36–54:** Continue with 15 sc in each round. **[15]**

Stuff to match the first leg. Sew the unworked stitches between the legs neatly closed, taking care not to pull the legs toward each other. The matching round counts help both sides finish at the same length.

**Fit tip:** Look at the frog from the front before making the shorts. If the legs lean noticeably in different directions, adjust the shaping and stuffing now; it's much easier before the garment goes on.

## Part 5 — Crochet the sunny yellow flippers

Each flipper begins with a narrow tip, gradually widens, and then becomes a flat foot. Make **two** identical pieces in **yellow yarn**.

1. **Rnd 1:** Crochet 5 sc in a magic ring. **[5]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in each stitch. **[10]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Crochet one sc in every stitch. **[10]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *1 sc, then an increase* five times. **[15]**
5. **Rnds 5–7:** Work 15 sc in each round. **[15]**
6. **Rnd 8:** Repeat *2 sc, then an increase* five times. **[20]**
7. **Rnds 9–18:** Crochet 20 sc in each of the next ten rounds. **[20]**

Fasten off with a long sewing tail. **Do not stuff the flippers.** Flatten them so they form broad, paddle-like feet, then stitch the open edge closed using your yarn needle. Make the second flipper in the same way and sew one to the end of each green leg.

## Part 6 — Crochet the long arms

These simple tubes hang at the frog's sides. Work **two arms in sage green**.

1. **Rnd 1:** Make 5 sc inside a magic ring. **[5]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in every stitch. **[10]**
3. **Rnds 3–20:** Work one sc in each stitch for eighteen rounds. **[10]**

Fasten off, leaving a long tail for sewing. Add a little fiberfill to each arm—the tubes should hold their shape without feeling bulky. Pin the arms on either side of the frog's upper body **between Rounds 14 and 15**; once you're happy with their position, sew them securely.

### Add the three little fingers to each hand

Using **sage-green yarn**, insert the hook through a stitch on the starting round of an arm and pull up a loop:

1. Chain 6.
2. Beginning in the **second chain from your hook**, slip stitch along the next 5 chains to return to the hand. **[5 sl sts]**
3. Attach with a slip stitch in the next available stitch on the hand.
4. Make another chain of 6 and repeat the five-slip-stitch return. Do this once more for **three fingers in total**.

Fasten off and hide the yarn tails inside the arm. Repeat for the other hand. The chains will hang naturally, like little webbed fingers.

## Part 7 — Make the striped swimming shorts

The shorts are a **separate garment**. You'll crochet a tube, widen it a little, then split the final stitches into two shorts legs. Prepare your **white and blue** yarn and change colors after every round for a classic seaside stripe.

### First make the waistband and shorts body

Chain **60** in white. Check that the chain hasn't twisted, then slip stitch into the first chain to form a ring. Work in a continuous spiral and **change yarn every round**, alternating white and blue.

1. **Rnds 1–8:** Work 60 sc per round. **[60]**
2. **Rnd 9:** Repeat *9 sc, then an increase* six times. **[66]**
3. **Rnds 10–12:** Work 66 sc in each round. **[66]**

You now have a wider striped tube. Try it over the frog's lower body before dividing it; the shorts should slip over the hips without stretching out the stripes.

### Divide the shorts into two leg openings

Mark **18 stitches for each shorts leg**, leaving **15 stitches across the front** and **15 across the back** for the center gap. Join the first 18-stitch opening by bringing its front and back edges together, working the joining stitch as the first sc of its round.

1. **Rnds 13–16:** Work 18 sc in each round, keeping the white-and-blue stripe sequence. **[18]**
2. **Rnd 17:** Make a slip stitch in each of the 18 stitches to create a neat leg edge. **[18]**

Fasten off and weave in the ends. Reattach **white yarn at the 16th unworked stitch on the back of Round 12** and repeat Rounds 13–17 for the second leg. Sew the remaining middle gap closed.

### Finish the waistband

With **white yarn**, attach at the first stitch of the original foundation round. Work **60 slip stitches** all the way around the top of the shorts. **[60]** Fasten off and tuck away any loose ends. Dress the frog in its new outfit, guiding the long legs through the separate openings.

## Part 8 — Put your frog together

A little planning makes all the difference in finishing. Arrange the parts before you stitch anything permanently, and check the overall balance from the front and side.

1. **Face:** Secure the white eye circles around the green eye bumps. Make sure the safety-eye washers are locked inside before the head is finished, or choose embroidered eyes for a little child's toy.
2. **Cheeks:** Add small pink embroidered marks below the raised eyes if you want extra character.
3. **Stuffing:** Check that the head and torso are firm but not overstuffed; add filling to the legs and arms as specified.
4. **Hands:** Finish the three chain fingers on each arm and hide all yarn ends.
5. **Arms:** Sew the arms to the sides of the upper body between Rounds 14 and 15.
6. **Feet:** Flatten both yellow flippers, close their openings and stitch them firmly to the lower legs.
7. **Shorts:** Close the shorts' inner seam, slip the garment over both legs, and settle the waistband around the frog's lower torso.
8. **Last check:** Stand or sit the frog on a flat surface and adjust the flippers so they point in a balanced direction. Weave away every remaining tail.

## If something looks a little wonky

**My eye bumps aren't even.** Count the final 18 stitches on each green eye before joining. If one has an extra stitch, correct it before working the six-chain bridge.

**There's a hole between the eyes.** Check that you crocheted along both sides of all six connecting chains. Tighten the join with the yarn tail if you see a gap.

**The legs don't line up.** Reposition the stitch markers on the 60-stitch bottom edge so the leg openings sit evenly under the head. Do this *before* completing the first leg.

**The shorts feel tight.** Try them on after Round 12, before dividing the leg openings. Keep the foundation chain relaxed and avoid carrying unused yarn too tightly inside the stripes.

**The flippers won't lie flat.** Don't add stuffing. Flatten the pieces before closing the openings and attach them at matching angles.
