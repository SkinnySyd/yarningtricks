Somewhere between the tide pools and the cliffs, a little puffin has settled into a cozy corner of the sewing table. Meet **Bracken**: a gently rounded bird with a charcoal head, a soft ivory chest, little cream cheeks and a sunset-red beak edged in yellow around a slate-grey center.

This is an **original Thread & Petal pattern**, made with separate, approachable pieces that can be pinned and adjusted before assembly: a stuffed body and head, a flat belly, small cheek patches, a little bill, wings, tail and two tiny ankle-and-foot sets. It uses a different construction from the bird designs in the reference book.

**About the pictures:** The cover, front view and detail close-ups are generated **handmade-style illustrations**, not photos of a test-crocheted puffin or a verified construction sequence. They intentionally show a puffin sitting comfortably, with uneven tension here and there and tiny stray fibers that match the yarn color. **Follow the written stitch counts and sewing directions** for construction, not the stitches depicted in the images. This original pattern has not been physically test-crocheted.

## Before you begin: a little puffin checklist

- **Crochet terms:** US. Work continuous spiral rounds unless you see a **Row** instruction. Mark your first stitch of each round.
- **Estimated size:** About 12–15 cm / 4¾–6 in tall in DK yarn. Size depends on tension and stuffing; this is not a measured test sample.
- **Construction:** Charcoal body and head, sewn-on cream belly and two face patches, one beak, two wings, one tail, and two small legs with flattened feet.
- **Pattern counts:** Square brackets show completed stitches at the end of the round or row. A chain or decorative toe-loop is counted separately.
- **Safety:** Plastic craft eyes and detachable small decorations pose risks for young children. Choose firmly embroidered facial features when appropriate, check all seams and use materials suitable for the intended recipient. A handmade pattern is not a toy-safety certification.

| Abbreviation | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **MR** | Magic ring |
| **ch** | Chain |
| **sc** | Single crochet |
| **sl st** | Slip stitch |
| **inc** | Make 2 sc in the next stitch |
| **dec** | Single-crochet decrease across the next 2 stitches |
| **rnd** | Round worked in a continuous spiral |
| **row** | Flat row; turn where instructed |

## Part 1 — Crochet Bracken's round charcoal body

Begin at the **bottom of the body** in charcoal. Build a broad base, continue straight for several rounds and then narrow the top to make a sturdy neck opening.

1. **Rnd 1:** Work 6 sc into an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in all 6 stitches. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *(1 sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *(3 sc, inc)* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *(4 sc, inc)* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *(5 sc, inc)* six times. **[42]**
8. **Rnd 8:** Repeat *(6 sc, inc)* six times. **[48]**
9. **Rnd 9:** Repeat *(7 sc, inc)* six times. **[54]**
10. **Rnds 10–15:** Make 54 sc for each of six rounds. **[54 each round]**
11. **Rnd 16:** Repeat *(7 sc, dec)* six times. **[48]**
12. **Rnd 17:** Work 48 sc. **[48]**
13. **Rnd 18:** Repeat *(6 sc, dec)* six times. **[42]**
14. **Rnd 19:** Work 42 sc. **[42]**
15. **Rnd 20:** Repeat *(5 sc, dec)* six times. **[36]**
16. **Rnd 21:** Repeat *(4 sc, dec)* six times. **[30]**
17. **Rnd 22:** Repeat *(3 sc, dec)* six times. **[24]**
18. **Rnd 23:** Work 24 sc. **[24]**

Stuff the body gradually, focusing on an even rounded base. A slightly soft base is better for Bracken’s relaxed sitting pose than packing him so tightly that he becomes rigid. **Leave the 24-stitch neck opening unclosed** and fasten off with a long sewing tail. If the base rolls, gently redistribute the filling before proceeding.

## Part 2 — Make the soft ivory belly panel

Work **flat rows** in ivory. The panel is wider in the middle and gently tapered at both ends, a little like a soft oval scarf for the puffin's front.

1. **Row 1:** Ch 11; sc in the second chain from the hook and across. **[10]**
2. **Row 2:** Ch 1, turn. Inc, 8 sc, inc. **[12]**
3. **Row 3:** Ch 1, turn. Inc, 10 sc, inc. **[14]**
4. **Row 4:** Ch 1, turn. Inc, 12 sc, inc. **[16]**
5. **Row 5:** Ch 1, turn. Inc, 14 sc, inc. **[18]**
6. **Row 6:** Ch 1, turn. Inc, 16 sc, inc. **[20]**
7. **Rows 7–12:** Ch 1 and turn before each row; work 20 sc across. **[20 each row]**
8. **Row 13:** Ch 1, turn. Dec, 16 sc, dec. **[18]**
9. **Row 14:** Ch 1, turn. Dec, 14 sc, dec. **[16]**
10. **Row 15:** Ch 1, turn. Dec, 12 sc, dec. **[14]**
11. **Row 16:** Ch 1, turn. Dec, 10 sc, dec. **[12]**
12. **Row 17:** Ch 1, turn. Dec, 8 sc, dec. **[10]**

Fasten off with a long tail for sewing. Gently smooth the panel; don't stretch it into a pointed shape. Pin its upper edge below the body's neck opening, then spread the sides smoothly over the front. If the panel is too large for your yarn, check the pin placement before changing stitch counts.

**Don't sew it on yet:** it's easier to check whether the belly is centered when the head is temporarily positioned above it.

## Part 3 — Make the little charcoal head

Use charcoal yarn, starting at the crown. The head stays **open at the bottom**, with 24 stitches to meet the body's 24-stitch neck.

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Inc in every stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** *(1 sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** *(3 sc, inc)* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** *(4 sc, inc)* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnds 7–12:** Work 36 sc in every round. **[36 each round]**
8. **Rnd 13:** *(4 sc, dec)* six times. **[30]**
9. **Rnd 14:** *(3 sc, dec)* six times. **[24]**

Stop and hold the head over the body. Mark the center front with a contrasting strand of yarn. If you're using display-only craft eyes, you'll need to determine their placement together with the cheek patches **before securing the washers**. The embroidery alternative can be added later.

Stuff the head, leaving the 24-stitch opening accessible. Fasten off with a long tail, **without closing the opening**.

## Part 4 — Two little ivory cheeks

Make **two** matching discs in ivory. They'll be gently shaped into ovals when sewn onto the sides of the face.

1. **Rnd 1:** Make 6 sc in an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in every stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** *(1 sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**

Fasten off with a sewing tail. Make the second cheek identical. Pin them on the front sides of the head, with their inner edges leaving space for the bill. Slightly flatten the discs into soft ovals as you sew.

If you are embroidering eyes, use a small secure black stitch or satin-stitch dot near the upper middle of each cheek. For adult display-only craft eyes, make sure the washer can fully lock through the joined layers of cheek and head; if it cannot, choose embroidery instead. Position and secure this hardware **before you close access to the head's interior**.

## Part 5 — Shape the grey, gold and russet puffin bill

The new picture has an earthy **slate-grey center**, a narrow **marigold-yellow ring**, and a soft **rust-orange outer edge**. To echo that little layered beak without a rigid insert, make a flat crocheted circle and fold it into a half-moon.

1. **Rnd 1:** With **slate-grey yarn**, make 6 sc in an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in all six stitches. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *(1 sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Change to **marigold yellow**; repeat *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Change to **rust-orange**; repeat *(3 sc, inc)* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Work 30 sc in rust-orange. **[30]**

Keep the rust-orange working yarn attached for the fold. Lay the circle flat and gently fold it in half, keeping the slate-grey, yellow and rust-orange bands visible on the outside. Pair the **30 stitches of the outer round** across the two layers, then use the working yarn to make **15 slip stitches through both layers** along the curved edge. **[15 joining sl sts]** Fasten off with a long sewing tail. The **straight folded edge** stays free for sewing to the head; the 15 slip stitches are a closing seam, **not** an eight- or fifteen-stitch attachment row. Don't stuff the beak or insert wire.

Pin the **straight folded edge** between the cream cheek patches. With the long tail, take small, even stitches through this folded edge and the head fabric; secure both ends so the bill cannot twist. The slate-grey center should sit closest to the face, with a narrow yellow stripe and rust-orange curved rim showing beyond it. Check the shape from the side before weaving in the tail.

## Part 6 — Crochet a pair of charcoal wings

Make **two** soft oval wings in charcoal. These are simple, unstuffed pieces with one narrow edge for sewing onto the sides of the body. For a natural handmade drape, avoid over-tightening the closing seam.

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in each stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** *(1 sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnds 4–5:** Work 18 sc each round. **[18 each round]**
5. **Rnd 6:** *(4 sc, dec)* three times. **[15]**
6. **Rnd 7:** Work 15 sc. **[15]**
7. **Rnd 8:** *(3 sc, dec)* three times. **[12]**
8. **Rnd 9:** Work 12 sc. **[12]**

Flatten the opening and make **6 sc through both layers** to close the end. **[6 closing stitches]** Leave a long tail. Work another identical wing and keep both unstuffed.

## Part 7 — The smallest tail

With charcoal yarn, make a short little bump for the back of the puffin.

1. **Rnd 1:** Work 6 sc in an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in each stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnds 3–4:** Make 12 sc each round. **[12 each round]**

Flatten the open end and close it with **6 sc through both layers**. **[6 closing stitches]** Leave a sewing tail. Don't stuff it.

## Part 8 — Little coral feet and ankles

Bracken's bright feet are worked flat, with a short narrow ankle behind each one. Make **two feet** and **two ankles**. They help a soft seated puffin rest on his base; they are not intended to hold his weight in an unsupported standing pose.

### Make the feet (two)

1. With coral-orange yarn, **ch 5**. Starting in the second chain, sc across. **[4]**
2. **Row 2:** Ch 1, turn; inc, 2 sc, inc. **[6]**
3. **Row 3:** Ch 1, turn; make 6 sc. **[6]**
4. **Row 4:** Ch 1, turn. Work 2 sc, ch 2 and sl st into the second chain from the hook to make a tiny toe; repeat this *(2 sc, ch 2, sl st into the second chain)* group two more times. **[6 sc + 3 small toe loops]**

Fasten off, leaving a tail for joining. The wider edge with three little loops faces forward.

### Make the ankles (two)

1. With coral-orange yarn, make 6 sc in an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnds 2–4:** Work 6 sc each round. **[6 each round]**

Leave the top of each small tube open. Flatten slightly and sew the **closed magic-ring end** to the narrow starting-chain edge of one foot. Leave the open six-stitch edge free for attaching to the body. Repeat for the second foot.

## Part 9 — Bring Bracken together

### Before you sew: count your pieces

Spread out the finished pieces and compare them with this short checklist. It helps catch a missing part before it is hidden under a seam.

| Piece | Number | Joining checkpoint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Charcoal body and head | 1 of each | Both openings have **24 stitches** |
| Ivory belly | 1 | The broadest rows have **20 stitches** |
| Ivory cheek patches | 2 | Each disc finishes at **24 stitches** |
| Folded three-color bill | 1 | **15 slip stitches** close the curved edge; sew the straight fold to the face |
| Charcoal wings | 2 | Each closes with **6 sc through both layers** |
| Charcoal tail | 1 | **6 sc** close its opening |
| Coral feet and ankles | 2 of each | Each ankle has an **open six-stitch end** to sew beneath the body |

Before sewing, place all the pieces nearby and give each one a gentle shape with your fingers. A couple of removable stitch markers and a quiet moment of checking symmetry will make this part much easier. Our [amigurumi assembly lesson](/learn/sew-amigurumi-pieces-together/) has more detailed joining tips.

1. **Find the front.** Mark the center of the body and temporarily place the ivory belly panel on it. Position the head opening above the panel so the front aligns.
2. **Sew the belly.** Beginning just below the neck opening, stitch around the ivory panel's outer edge onto the front of the stuffed body. Keep the stitches loose enough to prevent puckering; secure the panel all the way around.
3. **Join head and body.** Align the head's **24-stitch opening** with the body's **24-stitch opening**. Sew them together evenly, stitch for stitch, checking that the head stays upright. Add or redistribute stuffing before closing the seam completely, if necessary.
4. **Secure the cheeks and eyes.** Check each cream patch sits at the same height and distance from the bill's centerline. If you haven't already sewn on the patches, sew their perimeters carefully. Finish with securely embroidered eyes if not using appropriate preinstalled display-only eyes.
5. **Add the bill.** Center its **straight folded edge** between the cheeks and sew it onto the front. This is separate from the **15-slip-stitch seam around the curved edge**. The slate-grey area belongs nearest the face; the gold stripe and rust-orange rim project gently outward. Check that the beak is balanced from both sides.
6. **Attach the wings.** Pin the narrow edge of a wing high on each side. Check both are level from the front, then sew the edges in place, leaving the rounded wing tips loose.
7. **Add the tail.** Sew its six-stitch closing edge to the lower back of the body, pointing gently outward.
8. **Give him his feet.** Attach each open ankle to the underside of the body near the front, a few stitches apart. Tuck the ankles partly underneath so his belly can rest on the surface, and let the toes angle outward comfortably. Set Bracken on a flat surface to check his **sitting** balance before tying the sewing tails firmly.
9. **Finish gently.** Tuck every loose end securely into the toy, inspect the seams and make any last adjustments to the stuffing and posture.

## A few friendly fixes along the way

- **Bracken leans forward:** Try temporarily shifting the foot attachments a stitch or two before sewing them down, or read [why crochet animals won't sit straight](/blog/why-crochet-animal-wont-sit-straight/).
- **The ivory belly puckers:** Loosen the sewing tension or move one pin. The panel should sit over the body, not pull it inward.
- **The cheek patches don't match:** Count both discs again and mark their centers before adjusting either one. The two **24-stitch** outer rounds should be equal.
- **The bill looks long or floppy:** The folded bill is **unstuffed**. Check that its **straight folded edge** is secured evenly against the face; the **15 slip stitches** belong on the curved closing seam, not the face attachment.
- **The wings point in different directions:** Check whether the six-stitch closing edges are attached at the same height on each side.
- **Stuffing peeks through:** Work with a firmer crochet fabric, and avoid overfilling your pieces. Our [stuffing guide](/blog/the-little-art-of-stuffing-amigurumi/) can help.
