Meet **Mossy**, a cheerful little sloth who believes that there's always time for one more cup of tea and one more row of crochet. With a friendly rounded face, a moss-green striped middle and long, floppy limbs, this woodland companion looks especially at home beside a basket of yarn.

This is an **intermediate** amigurumi project: the single-crochet shaping is familiar, but the facial color changes, four separate limbs and little claw pieces ask for patient counting and careful placement.

**A note about the design and images:** These are freshly written Thread & Petal construction instructions using the supplied sloth images as visual references. The pictures show the *intended appearance*, not a verified physical sample made from these exact written rounds. **The pattern has not yet been test-crocheted.** Stitch counts have been checked arithmetically, but finished size and proportion will depend on the yarn and your tension.

## Before you begin

- **Terms:** US terminology. **MR** = magic ring, **sc** = single crochet, **inc** = 2 sc in one stitch, **dec** = single-crochet decrease, **BLO** = back loops only, **sl st** = slip stitch, **FO** = fasten off.
- **Construction:** Crochet a single head-and-body piece from the crown down. Make two larger legs, two slimmer arms, twelve small cream digits, a rounded gray muzzle, two eye patches and two pink cheeks separately.
- **Technique:** Unless a step says otherwise, work in **continuous spirals**. Put a removable marker in the *first stitch* of every round and move it up each time.
- **Stitch totals:** Counts in **[brackets]** are the number of stitches after that round.
- **Size:** Expect a small-to-medium seated sloth depending on materials; no physically measured sample is available yet, so don't rely on a fixed height.

A snug fabric helps keep the stuffing tucked away. If you're unsure which hook suits your yarn, visit our [tension lesson](/learn/controlling-crochet-tension/) before beginning. For multiple yarns and sweater stripes, the [color-changing lesson](/learn/changing-yarn-colors-crochet/) is especially helpful.

### Your little project map

| Make first | Then | Finish with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Head and cream face area | Striped chest and brown lower body | Stuff and close the body |
| Eye patches, muzzle and cheeks | Two arms and two legs | Add twelve cream claws |
| Pin the face and limbs | Adjust their angles | Sew slowly and hide tails |

**For a young child's toy:** use securely embroidered eyes and other features, not detachable plastic eyes. Inspect every seam and attachment carefully. A written pattern does not certify a finished toy as safe.

## Part 1 — Head and body: make the round little sloth

Begin at the **top of the head** in brown. The first rounds build the dome; the following ones shape the cream-colored center of the face. Keep the beginning-of-round marker toward the back.

### Make the brown crown

1. **Rnd 1:** Make 6 sc in a magic ring. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in each stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *(sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *(3 sc, inc)* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *(4 sc, inc)* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *(5 sc, inc)* six times. **[42]**
8. **Rnd 8:** Repeat *(6 sc, inc)* six times. **[48]**
9. **Rnd 9:** Repeat *(7 sc, inc)* six times. **[54]**
10. **Rnd 10:** Repeat *(8 sc, inc)* six times. **[60]**
11. **Rnd 11–12:** Sc once in every stitch of each round. **[60 per round]**

**Count checkpoint:** There are 60 stitches at the crown's widest point. If yours differs, recount before introducing the cream. Fixing an extra stitch now is much easier than realigning a face later.

### Shape the cream face

Switch colors on the **last draw-through of the stitch before** the next colored section begins. Use the [color-change tutorial](/learn/changing-yarn-colors-crochet/) if you'd like a visual refresher. In each line below, the middle section sits at the front of the head.

12. **Rnd 13:** Brown **16 sc**, cream **28 sc**, brown **16 sc**. **[60]**
13. **Rnd 14:** Brown **14 sc**, cream **32 sc**, brown **14 sc**. **[60]**
14. **Rnd 15–19:** Brown **12 sc**, cream **36 sc**, brown **12 sc** in every round. **[60 per round]**
15. **Rnd 20:** Brown **14 sc**, cream **32 sc**, brown **14 sc**. **[60]**
16. **Rnd 21:** Brown **16 sc**, cream **28 sc**, brown **16 sc**. **[60]**
17. **Rnd 22:** Work one complete round in brown. **[60]**

**Pause for the face:** The cream section should appear wide and centered. The eye patches will add the darker expression later, so don't worry if the plain cream shape looks very different from the finished sloth at this stage.

### Narrow the neck

18. **Rnd 23:** Repeat *(8 sc, dec)* six times. **[54]**
19. **Rnd 24:** Repeat *(7 sc, dec)* six times. **[48]**
20. **Rnd 25:** Repeat *(6 sc, dec)* six times. **[42]**
21. **Rnd 26:** Repeat *(5 sc, dec)* six times. **[36]**
22. **Rnd 27:** Repeat *(4 sc, dec)* six times. **[30]**
23. **Rnd 28:** Sc all around. **[30]**

**Before the neck closes further:** Temporarily pin the eye patches and muzzle (instructions below) to check the expression. Add securely attached eyes at this stage if using toy eyes with backs, and then **stuff the head firmly but gently**. The flatter cream area should still read as a face instead of bulging out.

### Make the striped sweater section

The body grows wider below the narrow neck. Use **moss green** and **cream** in two-round bands: Rounds 29–30 green, 31–32 cream, 33–34 green, 35–36 cream, 37–38 green, 39–40 cream, 41–42 green, and 43–44 cream. The color change belongs at the beginning of the new round.

24. **Rnd 29:** Repeat *(4 sc, inc)* six times in moss green. **[36]**
25. **Rnd 30:** Repeat *(5 sc, inc)* six times. **[42]**
26. **Rnd 31:** Sc all around in cream. **[42]**
27. **Rnd 32:** Repeat *(6 sc, inc)* six times. **[48]**
28. **Rnd 33–44:** Work 48 sc in every round, changing yarn by the two-round stripe sequence above. **[48 per round]**

![Moss-green and cream sweater rounds above the warm brown lower body.](/images/tutorials/mossy-morning-sloth-amigurumi/sloth-body.avif)

*The stripes belong around the body. This image is a visual guide; the written rounds determine the exact color sequence.*

**Little colorwork tip:** In a continuous spiral, the meeting point of two stripes can create a small visible step. That's normal. Keep that meeting point at the back and resist inserting an extra stitch to straighten it—your stitch total matters more than a perfectly flat-looking stripe.

### Round the brown lower body

Switch to brown. The next round creates a subtle edge where the sweater ends.

29. **Rnd 45:** Work 48 sc **in back loops only**. **[48]**
30. **Rnd 46–50:** Sc once in each stitch. **[48 per round]**
31. **Rnd 51:** Repeat *(6 sc, dec)* six times. **[42]**
32. **Rnd 52:** Repeat *(5 sc, dec)* six times. **[36]**
33. **Rnd 53:** Repeat *(4 sc, dec)* six times. **[30]**
34. **Rnd 54:** Repeat *(3 sc, dec)* six times. **[24]**

Add fiberfill as you close. Keep the bottom gently rounded, not so tightly packed that the rows open and show the filling.

35. **Rnd 55:** Repeat *(2 sc, dec)* six times. **[18]**
36. **Rnd 56:** Repeat *(sc, dec)* six times. **[12]**
37. **Rnd 57:** Make six decreases. **[6]**

Cut the yarn with a sewing tail. Pass the needle through the front loop of each remaining stitch, pull the opening snug and weave the end inside.

## Part 2 — The two long, hugging arms

Use brown and make **two**. These are slimmer than the legs and should have a little movement after assembly.

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *(sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *(5 sc, inc)* three times. **[21]**
5. **Rnd 5–8:** Sc all around. **[21 per round]**
6. **Rnd 9:** Repeat *(5 sc, dec)* three times. **[18]**
7. **Rnd 10–13:** Sc all around. **[18 per round]**
8. **Rnd 14:** Repeat *(4 sc, dec)* three times. **[15]**
9. **Rnd 15–19:** Sc all around. **[15 per round]**
10. **Rnd 20:** Repeat *(3 sc, dec)* three times. **[12]**
11. **Rnd 21–25:** Sc all around. **[12 per round]**

Stuff the wider end lightly, leaving the upper attachment end softer. Flatten the last opening and FO, leaving a long tail for sewing. The arms should drape rather than point stiffly outward.

## Part 3 — Two longer, rounded legs

Work **two** in brown. Compared with the arms, the legs start rounder and remain fuller for longer.

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *(sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5–8:** Sc all around. **[24 per round]**
6. **Rnd 9:** Repeat *(6 sc, dec)* three times. **[21]**
7. **Rnd 10–12:** Sc all around. **[21 per round]**
8. **Rnd 13:** Repeat *(5 sc, dec)* three times. **[18]**
9. **Rnd 14–18:** Sc all around. **[18 per round]**
10. **Rnd 19:** Repeat *(4 sc, dec)* three times. **[15]**
11. **Rnd 20–22:** Sc all around. **[15 per round]**
12. **Rnd 23:** Repeat *(3 sc, dec)* three times. **[12]**
13. **Rnd 24–26:** Sc all around. **[12 per round]**

Fill the toes-end and body of each leg evenly, without making the upper joint rigid. Flatten the opening and fasten off with a long sewing tail.

![Four brown crocheted limbs, with little cream claws on their ends.](/images/tutorials/mossy-morning-sloth-amigurumi/sloth-pieces.avif)

*Lay matching limbs side by side. The legs are fuller, while the arms are a little narrower and meant to hang gently.*

## Part 4 — Twelve tiny cream claws

Each hand and foot gets **three separate cream digits**. Make **12 total**.

For each digit, with cream yarn:

1. **Rnd 1:** Crochet 4 sc into a magic ring. **[4]**
2. **Rnd 2–5:** Work 4 sc around in each round. **[4 per round]**
3. FO, leaving a tail to attach the digit. **Do not stuff.**

Sew three claws in a small fan at each rounded limb end. Instead of gathering all three tails into the same stitch, anchor them one at a time across adjacent positions to avoid a bulky knot.

**Tiny-piece rescue:** If your hook struggles to enter the four-stitch round, try a thinner yarn and suitably smaller hook for the digits, or relax your grip. [Crocheting tiny pieces](/blog/crocheting-tiny-amigurumi-pieces/) has more ways to keep these details manageable.

## Part 5 — Make the friendly face

The reference sloth has warm darker patches around the eyes, a soft gray muzzle and rosy cheeks. The pieces below are applied **after the main face colorwork**, so you can adjust their position before committing.

### Two warm-brown eye patches

Use darker brown and make **two oval patches**.

1. **Foundation:** Chain 6.
2. **Rnd 1:** Work 2 sc in the second chain from the hook, sc in each of the next 3 chains, 4 sc in the last chain. Turn the work around the chain and crochet into the underside: sc in the next 3 underside loops and 2 sc in the underside of the starting chain. **[14]**
3. **Rnd 2:** Increase in each of the first 2 stitches; sc in the next 3; increase in each of the next 4; sc in the next 3; increase in each of the final 2. **[22]**
4. FO, leaving a long sewing tail. Flatten and gently shape each patch into an oval.

Pin one oval on either side of the center of the cream face. Angle the inner ends slightly downward to give Mossy that sleepy, friendly expression. Check symmetry before sewing. If using safety eyes, position them through the patches and head fabric **before** closing and stuffing the head; otherwise embroider small dark eyes after sewing the patches.

### Gray rounded muzzle

With warm gray:

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase all around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *(sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4–5:** Sc around. **[18 per round]**
5. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *(sc, dec)* six times. **[12]**

Fasten off with a sewing tail. Add only a wisp of stuffing—or leave the muzzle unstuffed for a flatter expression. Before attaching it, embroider a small dark oval nose and a gentle smiling curve. Sew it over the center of the face, between the two eye patches.

### Two little pink cheeks

Make two in blush-pink yarn:

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in an MR. **[6]**
2. Join with a sl st, FO and leave a tail for sewing.

Pin a cheek beneath each eye patch, level with the sides of the muzzle. Secure them with small stitches around the edge.

![Soft gray crocheted sloth muzzle and two separate blush-pink cheek circles.](/images/tutorials/mossy-morning-sloth-amigurumi/sloth-face.avif)

*This little trio carries most of the expression. Sew it on only after you've checked the face straight on and from both sides.*

**Face placement tip:** Your eyes and muzzle should sit on the *front*, not migrate toward one side of the continuous spiral. Fold the head gently in half along the cream section to find a visual centerline, then mark it with a short strand of contrasting yarn. Our [embroidered faces lesson](/learn/embroider-amigurumi-faces/) can help you plan the smile.

## Part 6 — Bring your sloth together

Now for the part that transforms crocheted tubes into a little companion.

1. **Check the head's expression.** Look at it from the front, both sides and slightly above. Secure the eye patches, muzzle and cheeks.
2. **Pin both arms** near the top of the striped torso, approximately around **rounds 30–33**. They should have room to drape down the sides.
3. **Pin both legs** low along the brown body, approximately around **rounds 45–49**. Decide whether your sloth will sit with the legs spread or tucked forward.
4. **Sew the arms and legs** with their long tails, taking small, firm stitches through both the limb edge and the body fabric. Check alignment after a few stitches.
5. **Give each limb three cream claws** if you haven't already attached them. Check that each trio faces outward and feels securely anchored.
6. **Hide the remaining yarn tails** inside the body and gently shape the stuffing with your hands.

A small adjustment can make a huge difference here. Pin the limbs temporarily, set Mossy on a flat surface, and check whether the body leans. If so, shift the leg positions or redistribute stuffing before closing the last seam.

For a more detailed finishing refresher, visit [sewing amigurumi pieces together](/learn/sew-amigurumi-pieces-together/).

## Small troubles, gentle fixes

| If you notice… | Try this first |
| --- | --- |
| The two arms aren't the same length | Compare the **round counts**, not just their appearance after stuffing |
| The cream face looks off-center | Recheck your beginning-of-round marker and the 60-stitch color sequences |
| The sweater has a small stair-step at the back | That's common in spiral stripes; don't add stitches to correct it |
| Claws spread in different directions | Pin each trio before sewing and anchor their bases across neighboring stitches |
| The sloth won't sit comfortably | Loosen the upper leg stuffing, adjust where the legs attach, and check the lower body's fill |
| Stuffing shows between stitches | Test slightly smaller hook size rather than pulling painfully hard on the yarn |

## Make Mossy your own

Try swapping sage green for dusty blue, soft lilac or sunny mustard. For a simpler look, make the sweater in one color; for a more woodland feel, use a darker olive stripe.

You can also make a *sleepy sloth* by embroidering closed eyes instead of using shiny safety eyes. The expression changes beautifully with just two soft curved lines.

If you change the yarn weight, the finished size can change quite a bit. Our Journal note on [how yarn weight changes amigurumi](/blog/how-yarn-weight-changes-amigurumi/) explains why.

And if your first version looks different from the photo, that's a very normal part of working with yarn: read [why your amigurumi looks different](/blog/why-your-amigurumi-looks-different/) before deciding to rework a whole piece.

### One last little stitch

Mossy is a reminder that good things don't have to happen in a hurry. Take the time to shape the face, line up the claws and enjoy the simple rhythm of crochet. Your little sloth will have its own personality—and that's part of the lovely thing about making by hand.
