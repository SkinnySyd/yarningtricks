Meet **Clover**, a lavender bunny who takes dressing for a slow Sunday very seriously: a cozy striped top, mustard little shorts and bright pink suspenders. Her long ears are wonderfully floppy, while her soft round face only needs a few careful stitches to look cheerful.

This is an **original Thread & Petal construction** written to accompany the images you supplied. The general idea of a clothed bunny connects with the rabbit section of *Animal Friends of Pica Pau 3* (pages 50–55), but these are independently drafted stitch counts, garment pieces and explanations—not a transcription of the book pattern. **The pictures are appearance references, not photographs verifying these exact instructions. This version has not yet been test-crocheted.** Check sizing as you work, especially for the shorts.

## A little project map

- **Skill:** Intermediate. Most stitches are single crochet; the ear shaping, shirt stripes and separate outfit require a little patience.
- **Terms:** US terminology. **MR** = magic ring; **sc** = single crochet; **inc** = two sc in one stitch; **dec** = one single-crochet decrease over two stitches; **ch** = chain; **sl st** = slip stitch; **BLO** = back loops only; **st** = stitch.
- **Construction:** One continuous head and striped torso, two purple arms, two legs, two long ears and pale ear inserts, muzzle and cheeks, plus a separate pair of shorts and two suspender straps.
- **Rounds:** Unless stated otherwise, work in a continuous spiral without slip-stitch joins. Put a marker in the first stitch of each new round.
- **Numbers:** The **[bracketed]** number after each round is its completed stitch total.

New to colorwork? Keep the [color-change lesson](/learn/changing-yarn-colors-crochet/) nearby, and use [crochet-tension tips](/learn/controlling-crochet-tension/) if your hands start struggling with the smaller pieces.

## Part 1 — A rounded lavender head

Start at the top in **lavender**.

1. **Rnd 1:** Place 6 sc inside an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase into all six stitches. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** *(sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** *(3 sc, inc)* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** *(4 sc, inc)* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** *(5 sc, inc)* six times. **[42]**
8. **Rnd 8:** *(6 sc, inc)* six times. **[48]**
9. **Rnd 9:** *(7 sc, inc)* six times. **[54]**
10. **Rnd 10:** *(8 sc, inc)* six times. **[60]**
11. **Rnd 11–19:** Sc all around each round. **[60 per round]**
12. **Rnd 20:** *(8 sc, dec)* six times. **[54]**
13. **Rnd 21:** *(7 sc, dec)* six times. **[48]**
14. **Rnd 22:** *(6 sc, dec)* six times. **[42]**
15. **Rnd 23:** *(5 sc, dec)* six times. **[36]**

**Before the opening gets smaller:** Hold the head upright with the marked spiral seam toward the back. Place the eyes roughly between rounds 15 and 16, about 11–13 stitches apart, then temporarily pin the cream muzzle just below them. You can move these positions to suit the actual shape. Install eye backs *before* closing, or plan to embroider the eyes later.

16. **Rnd 24:** *(4 sc, dec)* six times. **[30]**
17. **Rnd 25:** *(3 sc, dec)* six times. **[24]**
18. **Rnd 26:** *(2 sc, dec)* six times. **[18]**

Stuff the head in small amounts, particularly at the cheeks. Stop if the spaces between stitches begin to open.

**Thread & Petal tip:** Resist the urge to make the head perfectly hard. A little springiness gives the cheeks their friendly rounded shape. The [stuffing guide](/blog/the-little-art-of-stuffing-amigurumi/) explains how to spread the fiberfill evenly.

## Part 2 — The striped sweater and rounded body

We'll continue directly from the head so there's no bulky neck seam.

19. **Rnd 27:** In cream, *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
20. **Rnd 28:** In orange, *(3 sc, inc)* six times. **[30]**
21. **Rnd 29:** In cream, *(4 sc, inc)* six times. **[36]**
22. **Rnd 30:** In orange, *(5 sc, inc)* six times. **[42]**
23. **Rnd 31:** In cream, *(6 sc, inc)* six times. **[48]**
24. **Rnd 32–39:** Work 48 sc each round, alternating **orange for two rounds, then cream for one round**: orange Rnds 32–33, cream 34, orange 35–36, cream 37, orange 38–39. **[48 per round]**
25. **Rnd 40:** Change to lavender. Sc around in BLO to create a slight shirt hem. **[48]**
26. **Rnd 41–43:** Sc all around. **[48 per round]**
27. **Rnd 44:** *(6 sc, dec)* six times. **[42]**
28. **Rnd 45:** *(5 sc, dec)* six times. **[36]**
29. **Rnd 46:** *(4 sc, dec)* six times. **[30]**
30. **Rnd 47:** *(3 sc, dec)* six times. **[24]**
31. **Rnd 48:** *(2 sc, dec)* six times. **[18]**

Add stuffing to the torso, shaping a gently rounded belly. The separate shorts will sit over its lower half.

32. **Rnd 49:** *(sc, dec)* six times. **[12]**
33. **Rnd 50:** Six decreases. **[6]**

Close the remaining loops with a tapestry needle and hide the end. **Count checkpoint:** The widest section of the sweater should be 48 stitches; the separate shorts below use a 48-chain foundation as a *starting fit*, not a universal size guarantee.

## Part 3 — Long ears with pale inner panels

Make **two lavender ears**, starting at the rounded tips.

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Inc around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** *(3 sc, inc)* three times. **[15]**
4. **Rnd 4:** *(4 sc, inc)* three times. **[18]**
5. **Rnd 5–16:** Sc 18 each round. **[18 per round]**
6. **Rnd 17:** *(4 sc, dec)* three times. **[15]**
7. **Rnd 18–22:** Sc 15 each round. **[15 per round]**
8. **Rnd 23:** *(3 sc, dec)* three times. **[12]**
9. **Rnd 24–25:** Sc 12 each round. **[12 per round]**

Fasten off with long tails, leaving the ears **unstuffed**. Flatten each one, then pinch the bottom into a gentle fold.

**Inner ear panels — make two:** With pale peach, chain 5; sc in the second chain from the hook and the next 3 chains **[4]**. Chain 1 and turn. Work 4 sc for **Rows 2–12**, then make **Row 13: dec twice [2]** and **Row 14: dec once [1]**. Fasten off and sew one narrow panel down the front center of each flattened ear. This flat decorative insert is intentionally slimmer than the ear itself.

Pin the ears high on opposite sides of the head. They should hang gently beside the face rather than stand rigidly upright.

## Part 4 — Arms, feet and the soft expression

### Arms — make two

In lavender:

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Inc all around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** *(3 sc, inc)* three times. **[15]**
4. **Rnd 4–13:** Sc 15 per round. **[15]**
5. **Rnd 14:** *(3 sc, dec)* three times. **[12]**
6. **Rnd 15–18:** Sc 12 per round. **[12]**

Stuff the hands lightly but leave the upper few rounds flexible. Flatten the open ends and leave yarn tails for sewing. If you like, wrap two shallow bands of cream and orange around the top of each arm using duplicate stitches to echo the shirt.

### Legs — make two

In lavender, make a rounded toe first:

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Inc around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** *(sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5–8:** Sc 24 each round. **[24]**
6. **Rnd 9:** *(6 sc, dec)* three times. **[21]**
7. **Rnd 10–12:** Sc 21 each round. **[21]**
8. **Rnd 13:** *(5 sc, dec)* three times. **[18]**
9. **Rnd 14–17:** Sc 18 each round. **[18]**
10. **Rnd 18:** *(4 sc, dec)* three times. **[15]**
11. **Rnd 19–20:** Sc 15 each round. **[15]**

Stuff the rounded feet and lower legs, leave the tops softer, and flatten the openings. You'll attach these after positioning the shorts.

### Muzzle and rosy cheeks

For the **cream muzzle**, work 6 sc in an MR **[6]**, increase around **[12]**, then repeat *(sc, inc)* six times **[18]**. Work one even round **[18]** and finish with a sewing tail. Add the smallest tuft of stuffing under the center when attaching; embroider a dark oval nose and two curved smile lines.

For each **pink cheek**, make 6 sc in an MR **[6]**, join with a slip stitch and leave a tail. Flatten and sew just below the outer corners of the eyes.

A few tiny cream or gold straight stitches on the forehead give Clover a charming handmade detail.

## Part 5 — Mustard shorts with pink suspenders

The shorts are a **separate piece** so you can check their width on the actual crocheted body before attaching the legs. This is an original, **52-stitch waistband / 56-stitch hip** design; it isn't a row-for-row copy of the reference book.

### Crochet the waistband and hip section

With mustard yarn, **ch 52**. Lay the chain flat, make sure it isn't twisted, and join to the first chain with a slip stitch. Mark the beginning of the round.

1. **Rnd 1–2:** Sc around the joined chain. **[52 per round]**
2. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *(12 sc, inc)* four times, spacing the increases around the shorts. **[56]**
3. **Rnd 4–8:** Sc in each stitch. **[56 per round]**

**Fit checkpoint:** The waistband should sit gently around the lavender body near Rnd 40, while the 56-stitch hip section has a little more room around the tummy. **Try the tube on before dividing it.** Crochet gauge differs between people, so the stitch total is a starting size, not a guaranteed fit. If the 52-stitch waist can't pass comfortably around the body, remake the foundation with **four extra chains** and add the same four evenly spaced increases: you'll have **60 hip stitches** and **26 stitches in each leg opening**.

### Mark the crotch — this is the important step

You have **56 stitches** around the open lower edge, and we need two separate cuffs.

1. Flatten the tube with its front and back facing each other. Adjust the fold so the middle front of the shorts lines up with the middle back.
2. Mark **four neighboring stitches at the front center** and the **four matching stitches at the back center**. These eight stitches will form the small bridge between the legs.
3. **Count the unmarked stitches:** You should have **24 stitches on the left** and **24 on the right**. The check is **4 front + 4 back + 24 left + 24 right = 56**.
4. Holding the front and back center groups together, use a tapestry needle and mustard yarn to **sew each of the four front stitches to its matching back stitch**, forming a short horizontal crotch seam. Don't crochet through the seam stitches again. Now the lower edge has **two distinct openings of 24 unsewn stitches each**.

**Why sew first?** It removes ambiguity about where the leg rounds connect. The seam belongs between the openings, not down either side of the shorts.

### Crochet each little short leg

- **Left cuff, Rnds 9–11:** Join mustard yarn at the inner edge of the left opening, **sc once in each of the 24 unsewn stitches**, and work three complete rounds. **[24 per round]** Finish with a loose round of **24 sl st** if you enjoy a tidy edge.
- **Right cuff, Rnds 9–11:** Repeat around the second opening. **[24 per round]** Finish the same way.
- Fasten off and weave in both tails.

The cuffs must remain open, with enough room for the lavender legs. If one cuff has 23 or 25 stitches, go back to the eight crotch markers before continuing rather than forcing the count.

### Add the pink waistband and two straps

With dusty pink, **ch 53**, sc in the second chain from the hook and each chain across **[52]**. Lay this narrow band along the **top 52-stitch edge** of the shorts, then sew it down with small, even stitches. If you adjusted the foundation, make the band the same number of stitches as your adjusted waistband.

Make **two suspender straps**. For each, begin with **ch 27**, slip stitch into the second chain and along the remaining 25 chains **[26]**, and leave a sewing tail at each end. These are **adjustable lengths**, not fixed measurements: pin one end at the front waistband, pass it over the bunny's shoulder, and trim or add chains until the strap lies flat before sewing its other end at the back.

![Hands sewing pink suspender details onto the bunny's colorful outfit.](/images/tutorials/cozy-lavender-bunny-amigurumi/assembly.avif)

*Pin and compare both straps from the front and back. Matching placement matters more than pulling the straps tight.*

## Part 6 — Assembly, with a little patience

1. Pin the **ears** at the top corners of the head. Check that the pale inserts face forward and that both ears fall at similar angles.
2. Sew on the **muzzle and cheeks**, then finish the eyes and any little forehead stitches.
3. Attach the two **arms** below the head, near torso Rnds 29–32, so the striped top is still visible.
4. **Put the finished shorts on the bunny's lower body before attaching the legs.** Align the crotch seam centrally and position the waistband near the lower shirt edge. You can slide the shorts down slightly while sewing to reach the body.
5. Pass each **lavender leg through its own short-leg opening**, then pin the upper end of each leg to the lower body in a comfortable seated position. Sew the leg to the body through its lavender fabric, not only through the shorts.
6. Slide the shorts into their final position and tack the waistband and cuffs at a few hidden points if you want them permanently in place. Attach the **pink straps last** so they lie naturally over the shoulders.

If the shorts cannot fit over the lower body *before* you attach the legs, adjust the waist and hip counts rather than trying to stretch the fabric over fully stuffed limbs. For help with secure seams, visit [sewing amigurumi pieces together](/learn/sew-amigurumi-pieces-together/).

## Little fixes that make a difference

| If you notice… | Try this |
| --- | --- |
| One ear hangs lower | Compare the unstuffed ears by row count, then pin at the same head height |
| The muzzle looks too large | Flatten the disc before sewing; don't stuff it into a round ball |
| The shorts are too snug | Start with four more waistband chains, distribute four increases for the hips, then divide into two 26-stitch cuffs |
| The suspender straps pucker the shirt | Remove one or two anchoring stitches and remake the straps a little longer |
| Your bunny won't sit up | Shift the legs forward slightly and redistribute fiberfill in the lower body |

For more on why a finished toy can differ from a reference image, see [Why your amigurumi looks different](/blog/why-your-amigurumi-looks-different/) and [How yarn weight changes amigurumi](/blog/how-yarn-weight-changes-amigurumi/).

## A Thread & Petal finishing note

A lavender bunny in mustard shorts is a lovely place to play with color. Swap the orange shirt for soft blue, make the straps cream, or embroider sleeping eyes instead. Clover is happiest when she looks like **your** little handmade bunny.
