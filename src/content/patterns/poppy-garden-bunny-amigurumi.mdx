Poppy is the little bunny who arrives at the vegetable patch before the sun, checks on every seedling and insists that even the tiniest radish deserves a celebration. Her oatmeal-colored body, soft rose-lined ears and gentle expression make a wonderfully comforting crochet companion.

This is an **original Thread & Petal project**, inspired by familiar garden-animal themes rather than a reproduction of any published rabbit's stitch sequence. The cover art is **an illustration, not a photograph of a finished test sample**. The written counts have been reviewed, but the finished design has **not yet been physically test-crocheted**, so check the fit of each piece as you assemble it.

## Your project at a glance

| You'll make | What to practice |
| --- | --- |
| A round head and softly tapered body | Even increases, decreases and consistent stuffing |
| Two long, unstuffed ears | Keeping paired pieces the same length |
| Two arms, two broad little feet and a tail | Neat seams and a stable seated pose |
| Optional small coral radish | Shaping a tiny three-dimensional detail |

**Stitch key (US terms):** **MR** = magic ring, **sc** = single crochet, **inc** = 2 sc in one stitch, **dec** = decrease over 2 stitches, **sl st** = slip stitch, **ch** = chain, **FO** = fasten off. The number in **[brackets]** is the total at the end of a round. Work in **continuous spirals** unless told otherwise.

If you are just learning to count stitches, read [how to keep your crochet tension even](/learn/controlling-crochet-tension/). Keep a marker in the **first stitch** of each round and move it upward every time.

## Part 1 — Make Poppy's round head

With **oatmeal beige**, start at the top.

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Inc in each stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *(1 sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *(3 sc, inc)* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *(4 sc, inc)* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *(5 sc, inc)* six times. **[42]**
8. **Rnd 8:** Repeat *(6 sc, inc)* six times. **[48]**
9. **Rnd 9:** Repeat *(7 sc, inc)* six times. **[54]**
10. **Rnd 10–17:** Work one sc in every stitch of each round. **[54 per round]**
11. **Rnd 18:** Repeat *(7 sc, dec)* six times. **[48]**
12. **Rnd 19:** Repeat *(6 sc, dec)* six times. **[42]**

**Face checkpoint:** Before the head closes further, mark the front center. Embroider two dark eyes at approximately Rnds 13–14, **10–12 stitches apart**, and check the placement from directly in front. If using plastic toy eyes for an adult display piece, add the washers before working the smaller rounds.

13. **Rnd 20:** Repeat *(5 sc, dec)* six times. **[36]**
14. **Rnd 21:** Repeat *(4 sc, dec)* six times. **[30]**
15. **Rnd 22:** Repeat *(3 sc, dec)* six times. **[24]**

Stuff with small tufts, especially at the sides of the face. The head should feel gently springy; stuffing that pushes apart the stitches is too firm.

16. **Rnd 23:** Repeat *(2 sc, dec)* six times. **[18]**
17. **Rnd 24:** Repeat *(sc, dec)* six times. **[12]**
18. **Rnd 25:** Make six decreases. **[6]**

FO, thread the tail through the front loops of the six remaining stitches and draw closed. Hide the yarn end.

### Small cream muzzle and pink cheeks

Make **two little cream muzzle ovals**. For each, ch 4. Starting in the second chain, work **2 sc in the first chain, 1 sc in the next, 3 sc in the last; turn to the underside, 1 sc in the middle underside loop and 1 sc in the first underside loop [8]**. Leave sewing tails.

Sew the two ovals side by side low on the face, beneath the eyes. Embroider a tiny blush-pink nose where the ovals meet, plus a short downward line and a gentle curved smile. Add one or two short blush stitches on each cheek.

**A little trick:** Pin the muzzle before sewing. The tiniest movement upward can make Poppy look curious; a tiny movement downward makes her look sleepy. For more detail ideas, visit [embroidering amigurumi faces](/learn/embroider-amigurumi-faces/).

## Part 2 — A soft seated body

Work the body **from the bottom upward**, separately from the head.

1. **Rnd 1:** In oatmeal beige, 6 sc in MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Inc around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** *(sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** *(3 sc, inc)* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** *(4 sc, inc)* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** *(5 sc, inc)* six times. **[42]**
8. **Rnd 8:** *(6 sc, inc)* six times. **[48]**
9. **Rnd 9–13:** Sc all around. **[48 per round]**
10. **Rnd 14:** *(6 sc, dec)* six times. **[42]**
11. **Rnd 15–16:** Sc all around. **[42 per round]**
12. **Rnd 17:** *(5 sc, dec)* six times. **[36]**
13. **Rnd 18–19:** Sc all around. **[36 per round]**
14. **Rnd 20:** *(4 sc, dec)* six times. **[30]**
15. **Rnd 21–22:** Sc all around. **[30 per round]**
16. **Rnd 23:** *(3 sc, dec)* six times. **[24]**
17. **Rnd 24:** Sc all around. **[24]**

FO leaving a **long sewing tail**, and **leave the top opening unclosed**. Stuff the base more firmly than the neck. Use the 24-stitch edge to sew the body under the finished head later.

**Shape checkpoint:** The lower body should be fuller than the upper chest, with a relatively flat bottom that helps Poppy sit. If your bunny wobbles, spread the stuffing sideways rather than piling it at the center. Our [stuffing and shaping advice](/blog/the-little-art-of-stuffing-amigurumi/) can help.

### Optional cream tummy patch

With cream, make a flat circle: **Rnd 1: 6 sc [6]; Rnd 2: inc around [12]; Rnd 3: (sc, inc) × 6 [18]; Rnd 4: (2 sc, inc) × 6 [24]**. FO with a tail. Flatten and sew it at the front of the lower body before attaching the arms.

## Part 3 — Two floppy bunny ears

In oatmeal beige, make **two identical unstuffed tubes**.

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Inc all around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** *(3 sc, inc)* three times. **[15]**
4. **Rnd 4–5:** Sc around. **[15 per round]**
5. **Rnd 6:** *(4 sc, inc)* three times. **[18]**
6. **Rnd 7–11:** Sc around. **[18 per round]**
7. **Rnd 12:** *(4 sc, dec)* three times. **[15]**
8. **Rnd 13–16:** Sc around. **[15 per round]**
9. **Rnd 17:** *(3 sc, dec)* three times. **[12]**
10. **Rnd 18–19:** Sc around. **[12 per round]**

FO with long tails. Press each ear flat **without stuffing** and fold the lower opening slightly to create a natural droop.

To suggest a rose-pink inner ear, embroider rows of long, closely spaced **satin stitches** on the flattened front, leaving a beige border. This is easier to size than adding a separately crocheted panel and keeps the ear pleasantly soft.

**Before sewing:** Lay the ears side by side. Check that they have the same number of rounds and that the pink centers point forward. Pin to the crown, roughly around Rnds 4–7, then check the silhouette from the front and back.

## Part 4 — Little paws and wide sitting feet

### Arms (make two)

1. **Rnd 1:** In beige, 6 sc in MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Inc around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3–5:** Sc around. **[12 per round]**
4. **Rnd 6:** *(2 sc, dec)* three times. **[9]**
5. **Rnd 7–15:** Sc around. **[9 per round]**

Add just a wisp of stuffing at the paw end. Flatten the upper end, FO and leave a tail for sewing.

### Feet (make two)

1. **Rnd 1:** In beige, 6 sc in MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Inc around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** *(sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5–7:** Sc around. **[24 per round]**
6. **Rnd 8:** *(2 sc, dec)* six times. **[18]**
7. **Rnd 9:** Sc around. **[18]**
8. **Rnd 10:** *(4 sc, dec)* three times. **[15]**
9. **Rnd 11–12:** Sc around. **[15 per round]**

Stuff the rounded toes; leave the upper ends soft. Flatten the openings, FO and leave long tails.

### A pom-pom tail

**Rnd 1:** 6 sc in MR **[6]**; **Rnd 2:** inc around **[12]**; **Rnd 3:** *(sc, inc)* six times **[18]**; **Rnd 4–5:** 18 sc **[18]**; **Rnd 6:** *(sc, dec)* six times **[12]**. Stuff lightly, FO with a tail and sew to the back of the body.

## Part 5 — Optional coral garden radish

This miniature is for **display or gentle play by older children**, not for babies or toddlers. If you prefer a child-safe version, omit the separate accessory.

Start at the **narrow point** in coral-pink yarn:

1. **Rnd 1–2:** 6 sc in MR, then another round of 6 sc. **[6 per round]**
2. **Rnd 3:** *(sc, inc)* three times. **[9]**
3. **Rnd 4:** Sc around. **[9]**
4. **Rnd 5:** *(2 sc, inc)* three times. **[12]**
5. **Rnd 6–7:** Sc around. **[12 per round]**
6. **Rnd 8:** *(3 sc, inc)* three times. **[15]**
7. **Rnd 9–10:** Sc around. **[15 per round]**
8. **Rnd 11:** *(3 sc, dec)* three times. **[12]**
9. **Rnd 12:** *(2 sc, dec)* three times. **[9]**

Stuff sparingly, then **Rnd 13:** *(sc, dec)* three times **[6]**. Close the opening.

For a tiny sage leaf, **ch 7**, sc in the second chain, hdc in the next two chains, sc in the next two, sl st into the final chain, and FO. Make two or three leaves and sew them firmly to the wide end. You can also embroider a short green stem instead.

**Tiny-parts tip:** Don't fight a difficult four- or six-stitch circle by pulling harder. Let the yarn move freely and use a small marker or contrasting thread to find the first stitch. Read [crocheting tiny amigurumi pieces](/blog/crocheting-tiny-amigurumi-pieces/) for more help.

## Part 6 — Assembly, the Thread & Petal way

1. **Join the head to the body.** Center the face on the widest part of the tummy, pin the 24-stitch body opening evenly beneath the head, then sew around with small secure stitches. Add a little more stuffing before closing the final gap if needed.
2. **Attach the ears.** Pin both at the crown first; allow them to lean and droop naturally.
3. **Place the feet.** Sit Poppy on a flat table, push the lower feet slightly forward and pin both at a matching height. Sew through the actual body fabric, not just the tummy patch.
4. **Sew on the arms** at either side of the upper chest. The paw ends can rest beside the belly or reach toward the optional radish.
5. **Add the tail** at the back just above the sitting base.
6. **Finish the ribbon.** With sage yarn, crochet a chain long enough to drape loosely around the neck and sew it down as a short decorative band rather than a removable tie for young children.

Use the [lesson on sewing amigurumi pieces](/learn/sew-amigurumi-pieces-together/) if this is your first multi-piece animal.

## Little fixes for little bunny troubles

| What you see | What helps |
| --- | --- |
| One ear appears longer | Count Rnds 7–11 and 13–16 again, then compare before sewing |
| A lumpy face | Reposition the stuffing; keep the muzzle pieces unstuffed |
| The bunny tips forward | Place the feet a little more forward, keep the back tail small and balance the bottom fill |
| A visible head seam | Use the same beige yarn and make small stitches into matching loops |
| The radish seems oversized | Use a finer yarn and smaller hook for the *accessory only* |

Handmade animals have their own expressions. If Poppy looks a little different from the project illustration, that's normal; our Journal article about [why amigurumi looks different](/blog/why-your-amigurumi-looks-different/) explains the most common reasons.
