Some handmade projects simply make a shelf feel happier. This rosy octopus has a friendly face, eight short arms, and an unexpected job: hiding a small sachet of fragrant potpourri inside its hollow body.

<h2 id="before-you-begin">Before your first stitch</h2>

- **Finished size:** approximately 3½ in / 8.9 cm high and 4 in / 10.2 cm wide.
- **Shape:** eight small arms are crocheted together to create the bottom edge; the round body is made **upside down**, narrowing toward the top of the head.
- **Important:** don't pack the body with regular toy stuffing. Leave room for the potpourri sachet, which goes in through the bottom opening.
- **Terminology:** US crochet stitch names; use a stitch marker at the start of each continuous round.

With pink yarn, crochet **eight identical pieces**. These are small, softly rounded tubes that will form the skirt of tentacles.

<table className="stitch-counts"><thead><tr><th scope="col">Round</th><th scope="col">What to do</th><th scope="col">Count</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>1</td><td>Make 6 single crochets in a magic ring.</td><td>6</td></tr><tr><td>2–5</td><td>Work 1 sc in each stitch for four rounds.</td><td>6 each</td></tr></tbody></table>

Add only a **little stuffing** to each arm. Flatten the opening so the six stitches become **three opposing pairs**. Fasten off invisibly, saving the yarn tail for tidying up later.

**Keep the arms together:** Lay all eight in a circle before joining. A quick arrangement check helps prevent a twisted or uneven octopus base.

## Part 2 — Join the arms into the body

This construction is the clever part: instead of sewing on the arms after completing the body, you join them as you crochet your opening round. Each flattened arm contributes **three stitches** to the new body: eight arms × three stitches = **24 stitches**.

### Round 1 — Crochet across the flattened arms (24)

1. Take the first flattened arm and **slip-stitch through the first pair of opposite stitches** to join your working yarn.
2. Make a **single crochet through that same paired spot**, then work a single crochet through each of the arm's **two remaining paired positions**.
3. Continue into the next arm, again crocheting through its three paired positions.
4. Work all the way around all eight arms, joining each arm to the growing body. Your starting edge should measure **24 single crochets**.

Crochet over any short yarn tails as you work if they can be safely tucked in; otherwise secure them on the inside. Maintain an opening beneath the arms for the drawstring closure.

### Rounds 2–13 — Build the upside-down head

Continue in pink yarn, working in continuous rounds.

**Add the face as you work:** Install the eyes **between rounds 6 and 7**, with **seven stitches between them**. Because you're making this octopus upside-down, those same eyes appear between rounds **8 and 9 if counting from the finished top**.

At the end of round 13, leave a long yarn tail. Draw it through the front loops of the remaining **six stitches**, pull the opening closed, and tuck the tail in securely.

**For babies or pets:** Use embroidered eyes rather than hard plastic safety eyes. Potpourri and sachets are not safe toy fillings; this version is meant for decorative adult use.

The head stays hollow, which makes this a good time to embroider from the inside.

1. Thread your tapestry needle with black yarn. From **inside** the octopus, bring the needle out at the spot chosen for one corner of the smile.
2. Travel across the outside and insert the needle **three stitches to the left** to form a gently curved line. Bring the needle back out **one round below**, centered between the two smile corners.
3. Use that lower stitch to catch the curved yarn lightly, pulling just enough to give it a smiling shape. Return the needle through the same lower opening.
4. Tie the yarn ends neatly **inside** the octopus and trim them short.

Aim for a small smile rather than a taut straight line; the tiny dip at its center adds expression.

The bottom remains open between the arms so you can add a little potpourri bag. Here's how to close it without stitching the sachet permanently inside.

1. Cut a comfortable length of **pink yarn** and thread your needle. Starting from the inner side of the base, pass the needle **around one stitch at the center of an arm**, exactly where that arm joins the body. Leave a tail of about **6 in / 15 cm**.
2. Continue to the center of the next arm, moving **counterclockwise** around the octopus. Pass the needle around one joining stitch on each of the eight arms, forming a loose drawstring circle.
3. Place a **securely closed bag of potpourri** into the hollow head. Trim the two ends of the drawstring to equal lengths, draw them together until the base closes, then tie a neat bow.
4. Hide the bow and loose ends inside the body. To refresh the scent, carefully loosen the drawstring and replace the sachet.

## Style and care notes

This little octopus looks especially pretty perched on a dresser or inside a wardrobe where its fragrance can gently circulate. Keep it away from moisture, open flames, children and pets; avoid using loose potpourri that could escape through the stitches.

For a different mood, make your octopus in sea-glass blue, lavender or warm apricot. Changing the color is an easy way to make a gift feel personal without changing the stitch counts.
