The classic granny square is built from groups of three double crochets, called **granny clusters**, arranged around four corners. This small, **two-round square** is a friendly introduction to working in the round; make more rounds if you'd like a larger coaster.

This pattern uses **US crochet terms**: *ch* = chain, *sl st* = slip stitch, and *dc* = double crochet (**UK treble**). New to any of these? Review [double crochet](/learn/double-crochet-stitch/), [magic rings and joined rounds](/learn/magic-ring-crochet-in-the-round/), or [crochet abbreviations](/learn/how-to-read-crochet-patterns/).

## Before you start

A **granny cluster** here means **3 dc worked into the same space**. A **ch-2 space** is the opening created by chaining two stitches; it's where you'll make the corners on the next round.

For this square, the beginning **chain 3 counts as your first dc** in each round. Slip stitches used to move to a corner or join rounds **don't count as double crochets**.

## Round 1 — Make four corners

1. Make a **magic ring**. Alternatively, **chain 4 and slip stitch into the first chain** to form a small ring.
2. Chain **3** (this counts as the first dc), then work **2 dc** into the ring. Chain **2**. That's your first granny cluster and corner gap.
3. Work **3 dc into the ring, ch 2** three more times.
4. If you used a magic ring, carefully pull it closed. Slip stitch into the **top of your starting ch-3** to join the round.

**Check your count:** you should see **4 groups of 3 dc** (12 dc in total, counting the starting ch-3) and **4 ch-2 corner spaces**.

## Round 2 — Turn the circle into a square

1. Slip stitch across the **next two double crochets** and then into the **next ch-2 corner space**. Your hook is now at a corner.
2. Chain **3** (counts as the first dc). Work **2 dc, ch 2, 3 dc** into **that same corner space**. You've made two clusters with a chain-2 corner between them.
3. **Chain 1.** In the next corner space, work **(3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc)**.
4. **Chain 1**, then repeat Step 3 in the third corner. **Chain 1**, then repeat Step 3 in the fourth corner.
5. **Chain 1** one last time and slip stitch into the top of the starting ch-3 to join.

**Check your count:** you now have **8 groups of 3 dc**, **4 ch-2 corner spaces**, and **4 ch-1 spaces between corners**.

## Finish your coaster

Cut the yarn, pull the tail through the final loop, and weave in the ends securely. Lay the square flat and gently shape its corners. If your yarn's care label permits it, light blocking can help even out the edges.

The square is intentionally open and lacy. If you need a less open coaster, consider adding a suitable backing; use yarn and materials appropriate for the surface and temperature you expect it to contact.

## Add a third round (optional)

Slip stitch to a corner again. Work **(3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc)** in each corner space, **3 dc** in each ch-1 side space, and **ch 1** between neighboring clusters. Join to the beginning ch-3 as before. The same principle builds larger granny squares.

**Troubleshooting:** If the edges ripple, check that you didn't add extra clusters. If your corners don't look square, make sure every corner contains **two clusters separated by ch 2**.
