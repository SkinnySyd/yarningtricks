Tucked into the coziest corner of the imaginary Ice Age is **Mallow**, a small woolly mammoth who would much rather collect warm hugs than brave a snowstorm. He has a wonderfully long trunk, two little ivory tusks and a tousled fringe that looks like he has just woken up from a very good nap.

This is a more adventurous amigurumi make, but nothing needs to happen in a rush. We'll shape his head and trunk, make the curved tusks one at a time, build his woolly hair and finish with four chunky, cream-toed paws. Keep your stitch marker close and enjoy the small discoveries as the character comes together.

**About the illustration:** The picture is a generated *crochet styling reference* for Thread & Petal. It shows exactly four limbs, but it does not depict a test-crocheted sample, so treat the written rounds and construction notes—not the artwork—as your guide to the finished toy.

<a id="before-you-begin"></a>
## Before you begin — a few woolly notes

- **Skill:** Advanced. You'll use an unusual opening between the trunk and head, mirrored parts, surface loops, shaped flat sections and frequent small color changes.
- **Approximate size:** 19 cm / 7.5 in tall with the yarn and hook size used for the reference design. Your finished size will change with your tension and yarn.
- **Method:** Most pieces are crocheted in **continuous spiral rounds**. A few tusk sections and the eyelids are worked in rows.
- **Terminology:** **US crochet terms** throughout. Move the stitch marker after every full round. The count in **[square brackets]** is the number of stitches at the end of that round.
- **Colors:** Brown makes the body, trunk, ears, fringe and most of the paws. Ivory is for the tusks and little toe-bobbles. For a shaggy look, you can hold a fine furry brown strand alongside the ordinary brown yarn.

**Safety note:** Plastic safety eyes are small hard components, and even designs labeled "safety" can present a choking hazard if detached. For a mammoth intended for a baby or young child, use securely embroidered facial features, avoid loose embellishments and inspect every seam and attachment. The finished project is not a certified child-safe toy.

<a id="stitch-key"></a>
## Stitch key — the language of Mallow's pattern

| Abbreviation | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **MR** | Magic ring |
| **ch** | Chain |
| **sc** | Single crochet |
| **hdc** | Half double crochet |
| **dc** | Double crochet |
| **sl st** | Slip stitch |
| **inc** | Two single crochets in one stitch |
| **dec** | Single-crochet decrease over two stitches |
| **FLO / BLO** | Work through front loops only / back loops only |
| **5-dc bobble** | Five partial double crochets closed together in one stitch (explained below) |
| **rnd / row** | Round / flat row |

When the directions say **(4 sc, inc) × 6**, work four regular stitches and then one increase, repeating that group six times. Need to revisit the basics? Our [magic-ring lesson](/learn/magic-ring-crochet-in-the-round/), [increase and decrease guide](/learn/crochet-increases-and-decreases/) and [crochet pattern reading guide](/learn/how-to-read-crochet-patterns/) are nearby.

### The five-double-crochet bobble

The contrasting toes are little raised bobbles. In the designated stitch, work a **partly completed dc** five times: yarn over, insert hook, draw up a loop, yarn over and draw through two loops, then pause. Repeat in that **same stitch** until there are **six loops** on the hook. Yarn over and draw through all six loops to close. One bobble counts as **one stitch**.

For a neat ivory toe, work the partial double crochets with ivory, then use **brown for the final pull-through**. Continue the next ordinary stitch in brown. Keep the resting strand on the inside with enough slack that the paw doesn't pucker.

<a id="make-the-pieces"></a>
## Part 1 — Shape the head and leave room for the trunk

Mallow's face begins at the **front of the head**, around the base of his trunk. Rather than sewing an entirely separate snout onto a ball-shaped head, you'll make an opening in the early rounds and return to it later. Use **brown yarn**.

### Open out the front

1. **Rnd 1:** Crochet 6 sc into an adjustable MR. Pull the center closed. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in every stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *(inc, 1 sc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *(inc, 2 sc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *(inc, 3 sc)* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *(inc, 4 sc)* six times. **[36]**

On the following round, mark the **25th stitch**. Keep this marker in place: it identifies where the trunk will begin.

7. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *(inc, 5 sc)* six times. **[42]**
8. **Rnd 8 — the important split:** Work **23 sc**, **leave the next 18 stitches unworked**, then work **1 sc into the final stitch**. You have formed a smaller, **24-stitch** opening around which the remainder of the head is worked. **[24]**

**Pause here:** This step deliberately leaves an opening between the skipped stitches and the head. Don't close it or sew those stitches together—it's where the trunk will grow.

### Round the head and make room for the eyes

9. **Rnd 9:** Work 24 sc, one in each stitch. **[24]**
10. **Rnd 10:** Repeat *(inc, 3 sc)* six times. **[30]**
11. **Rnd 11:** Work 30 sc. **[30]**

In the next round, mark the **14th and 24th stitches as you crochet**. These become the eye-placement markers.

12. **Rnd 12:** Repeat *(inc, 4 sc)* six times. **[36]**
13. **Rnd 13:** Work 13 sc; repeat *(inc, sc)* six times; finish with 11 sc. **[42]**
14. **Rnd 14:** Crochet once in every stitch. **[42]**
15. **Rnd 15:** Work **7 sc, inc in each of the next 3 stitches, 24 sc, inc in each of the next 3 stitches, 5 sc**. **[48]**
16. **Rnd 16:** Crochet 48 sc. **[48]**
17. **Rnd 17:** Work 16 sc; repeat *(inc, 2 sc)* six times; finish with 14 sc. **[54]**
18. **Rnds 18–20:** Work 54 sc in each of these three rounds. **[54 each round]**

If you are using plastic eyes on a decorative mammoth, place them at the two previously marked locations **before the head closes**. For embroidered eyes, mark the positions with spare yarn and embroider the eyes securely during finishing instead.

### Close the back of the head

21. **Rnd 21:** Repeat *(dec, 7 sc)* six times. **[48]**
22. **Rnd 22:** Repeat *(dec, 6 sc)* six times. **[42]**

Begin adding stuffing now. Spread it evenly around the cheeks and the back of the head rather than creating one firm lump.

23. **Rnd 23:** Repeat *(dec, 5 sc)* six times. **[36]**
24. **Rnd 24:** Repeat *(dec, 4 sc)* six times. **[30]**
25. **Rnd 25:** Repeat *(dec, 3 sc)* six times. **[24]**
26. **Rnd 26:** Repeat *(dec, 2 sc)* six times. **[18]**
27. **Rnd 27:** Repeat *(dec, sc)* six times. **[12]**
28. **Rnd 28:** Decrease six times. **[6]**

Cut the yarn and draw the end through the last loop. With a tapestry needle, thread the tail through the **front loops of the final six stitches** and pull the opening closed. Hide the end inside the head.

## Part 2 — Give Mallow his long, curved trunk

Return to the **opening left during head round 8**. Join brown yarn at the marker you placed earlier. You'll crochet around this opening to form a tapering tube.

1. **Trunk rnd 1:** Work **16 sc** around the opening. At the first corner, make a decrease spanning the next stitch and its adjacent edge; at the other corner, make another decrease spanning the edge and the next stitch. These corner decreases close the little gaps and produce an **18-stitch** round. **[18]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Work 3 sc, dec, 8 sc, dec, 3 sc. **[16]**
3. **Rnds 3–6:** Crochet 16 sc in each round. **[16 each round]**
4. **Rnd 7:** Work 3 sc, dec, 7 sc, dec, 2 sc. **[14]**

Add a *little* fiberfill to the tube. Too much stuffing will make it harder to create Mallow's upward curl.

5. **Rnds 8–10:** Crochet 14 sc in each round. **[14 each round]**
6. **Rnd 11:** Work 4 sc, dec, 6 sc, dec. **[12]**
7. **Rnds 12–16:** Crochet 12 sc in each of the five rounds. **[12 each round]**
8. **Rnd 17:** Work six decreases. **[6]**

Fasten off and draw the yarn tail through the front loops of the final six stitches to close the tip. Hide the tail inside. During assembly, the end of the trunk is curved upward and secured with a few small stitches.

**Little tip:** Look for the two junctions at the corners when you start the trunk. The joining decreases are worked across those junctions, not as regular increases into the old head fabric. Count your 18 stitches before moving on.

## Part 3 — Make the two soft eyelids

These little crescent-shaped pieces soften Mallow's expression. Use brown yarn and make each side as written: they mirror one another.

**Left eyelid — chain 7.** Starting in the second chain from the hook, work:

- **Row 1:** 1 sc, 1 hdc, 1 dc, 1 hdc, 1 sc, 1 sl st. **[6]**

**Right eyelid — chain 7.** Starting in the second chain from the hook, work:

- **Row 1:** 1 sl st, 1 sc, 1 hdc, 1 dc, 1 hdc, 1 sc. **[6]**

Leave a sewing tail on each eyelid. Don't attach them until you've checked the placement against the eyes.

## Part 4 — Crochet two little ivory tusks

The tusks are mirror images: the **first 23 rounds are identical**, and only the small, flattened brown bases at the end differ. Start both in **ivory yarn**. Work **two**.

### The shared tusk rounds

1. **Rnd 1:** Work 4 sc in an MR. **[4]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Crochet 4 sc. **[4]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Make 1 inc, then 3 sc. **[5]**
4. **Rnds 4–6:** Work 5 sc in each round. **[5 each round]**
5. **Rnd 7:** Make 1 inc, then 4 sc. **[6]**
6. **Rnds 8–10:** Work 6 sc in each round. **[6 each round]**

Begin stuffing the tusk gently, adding little wisps as it grows.

7. **Rnd 11:** Make 1 inc, then 5 sc. **[7]**
8. **Rnd 12:** Increase in each of the next 2 stitches, decrease over the following 2, work 1 sc, then decrease over the last 2. **[7]**
9. **Rnd 13:** Crochet 7 sc. **[7]**
10. **Rnd 14:** Work 1 sc, inc in each of the next 2 stitches, then dec twice. **[7]**
11. **Rnds 15–21:** Continue with 7 sc per round. **[7 each round]**

Change to **brown**. The next rounds form the subtle rim and widening base at the point where the tusk joins the head.

12. **Rnd 22:** Work a slip stitch in the **front loop** of each of the 7 stitches of round 21. **[7]**
13. **Rnd 23:** Working in the **back loops of round 22**, make 1 inc and 6 sc. **[8]**

Now stop working in a spiral: the last section is shaped in **short rows and around their edges**.

### Right tusk: the base flap

- **Row 24:** Crochet into the next **1 stitch**, chain 1 and turn. **[1]**
- **Row 25:** Work **6 sc** across the accessible base stitches, chain 1 and turn. **[6]**
- **Row 26:** Decrease three times over those six stitches, work 2 sc down the first row edge, 2 sc across the available base stitches and 2 sc up the other row edge. **[9]**

Slip stitch once to secure and leave a long tail for sewing.

### Left tusk: the mirrored flap

- **Row 24:** Crochet into the next **4 stitches**, chain 1 and turn. **[4]**
- **Row 25:** Work **6 sc** across the accessible base stitches, chain 1 and turn. **[6]**
- **Row 26:** Decrease three times over those six stitches, work 2 sc down the first row edge, 2 sc across the available base stitches and 2 sc up the other row edge. **[9]**

Slip stitch once and leave a sewing tail.

**Check the mirror image:** Put the two tusks side by side before attaching them. The curved ivory portions should frame the trunk, with the brown base sections ready to lie against the face. The short-row shaping changes direction and is the trickiest bit of this small piece; use temporary markers if the edges are difficult to identify.

## Part 5 — Two floppy, mismatched-in-the-best-way ears

Each ear has the same rounded foundation, but the **last round is arranged differently** so the fuller edge faces outward on both sides. Crochet both in brown.

### Both ears: rounds 1–4

1. **Rnd 1:** Make 6 sc into an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Repeat *(sc, inc)* three times. **[9]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *(sc, inc, inc)* three times. **[15]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *(2 sc, inc, inc, sc)* three times. **[21]**

### Right ear — round 5

Work **3 sc, 2 hdc in the next stitch, 3 dc in the next stitch, 2 hdc in the next stitch, 3 sc, dec twice, 8 sc**. **[23]**

Slip stitch and leave a tail for sewing.

### Left ear — round 5

Work **9 sc, dec twice, 3 sc, 2 hdc in the next stitch, 3 dc in the next stitch, 2 hdc in the next stitch, 2 sc**. **[23]**

Slip stitch and leave a tail for sewing. Leave the ears **unstuffed** so they'll bend softly against the head.

## Part 6 — Make Mallow's cloud of curls

This two-part hairpiece is what gives Mallow his tousled woolly look. You'll first crochet an **oval foundation**, deliberately leaving the *front loops* free on several rounds. Then you'll return to those loops and add long chain loops that build the fringe.

### Crochet the oval hair base

With brown yarn, **chain 9**, then work around both sides of that chain.

1. **Rnd 1:** Starting in the second chain from the hook, increase in that chain, crochet 6 sc along the foundation, then work **3 dc** into the far end chain. Continue along the underside with **7 sc**. **[18]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Working in **BLO**, repeat *(1 sc, inc, 6 sc, inc)* twice. **[22]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Still in **BLO**, work 1 sc; repeat *(inc, 2 sc)* three times; work inc, sc; repeat *(inc, 2 sc)* three times; finish with inc. **[30]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Work all 30 stitches in **BLO**. **[30]**
5. **Rnds 5–6:** Make 30 sc in each round, using both loops normally. **[30 each round]**

Slip stitch and leave a generous tail to attach the hair base to Mallow's head.

### Add the long woolly loops

Find the **first unworked front loop of hair-base round 1**. Join a fresh strand of brown yarn there. Move through the unworked front loops in their spiral order.

- Along **round 1's 18 loops:** Repeat *(1 sc into the next free front loop, chain 8)* **18 times**.
- Along **round 2's 22 loops:** Repeat *(1 sc into the next free front loop, chain 12)* **22 times**.
- Along **round 3's 30 loops:** Repeat *(1 sc into the next free front loop, chain 16)* **30 times**.

You'll make **70 anchored loop groups altogether**, with their chain lengths growing toward the outer sections. Slip stitch beside the last single crochet and hide the working end.

The loops can look a little chaotic while you're working. Once the hairpiece is attached and gently spread out, the separate strands become the lovely woolly mop.

## Part 7 — Crochet the round tummy

Make one body in **brown yarn**. This is a bottom-to-top shape: it widens into a generous belly and then narrows toward the neck. Leave the upper opening available to sew to the head.

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in each stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *(sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *(3 sc, inc)* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *(4 sc, inc)* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *(5 sc, inc)* six times. **[42]**
8. **Rnd 8:** Repeat *(6 sc, inc)* six times. **[48]**
9. **Rnd 9:** Repeat *(7 sc, inc)* six times. **[54]**
10. **Rnds 10–14:** Crochet 54 sc in each of five rounds. **[54 each round]**
11. **Rnd 15:** Repeat *(7 sc, dec)* six times. **[48]**
12. **Rnd 16:** Work 48 sc. **[48]**
13. **Rnd 17:** Repeat *(6 sc, dec)* six times. **[42]**
14. **Rnd 18:** Work 42 sc. **[42]**
15. **Rnd 19:** Repeat *(5 sc, dec)* six times. **[36]**
16. **Rnd 20:** Work 36 sc. **[36]**
17. **Rnd 21:** Repeat *(4 sc, dec)* six times. **[30]**
18. **Rnd 22:** Work 30 sc. **[30]**
19. **Rnd 23:** Repeat *(3 sc, dec)* six times. **[24]**
20. **Rnd 24:** Work 24 sc. **[24]**
21. **Rnd 25:** Repeat *(2 sc, dec)* six times. **[18]**
22. **Rnd 26:** Work 18 sc. **[18]**

Fasten off, leaving a long sewing tail. Stuff the round belly firmly enough to keep its shape but not so tightly that the yarn gaps open. Keep the **18-stitch neck edge open** for joining.

## Part 8 — Make the two arms with ivory toes

You'll make **two** brown arms. The toes are contrasting **five-dc bobbles** worked during round 5. Keep the unused ivory yarn at the back, switching only for the bobbles.

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc into an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in each stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *(inc, sc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *(inc, 2 sc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *(1 ivory 5-dc bobble, 2 brown sc)* four times, then crochet 12 more brown sc. **[24]**
6. **Rnds 6–11:** Crochet 24 sc in each of six rounds. **[24 each round]**

Add stuffing to the hand, then keep stuffing as the arm lengthens. The upper end doesn't need to be quite as firm.

7. **Rnd 12:** Repeat *(dec, 2 sc)* six times. **[18]**
8. **Rnd 13:** Repeat *(dec, 7 sc)* twice. **[16]**
9. **Rnds 14–23:** Work 16 sc for each of ten rounds. **[16 each round]**
10. **Rnd 24:** Repeat *(dec, 2 sc)* four times. **[12]**
11. **Rnd 25:** Decrease six times. **[6]**

Cut the yarn. Close the remaining six stitches by threading the tail through their front loops and pulling the opening together. Tuck in the end. Repeat for the second arm.

## Part 9 — Make two sturdy seated legs

Each leg begins with a broad, rounded foot. The **four ivory toe-bobbles** appear in round 7; the rest of the leg is brown. Work **two** pieces.

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc into an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in every stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *(inc, sc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *(inc, 2 sc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *(inc, 3 sc)* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *(inc, 4 sc)* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *(1 ivory 5-dc bobble, 3 brown sc)* four times, then work 20 more brown sc. **[36]**
8. **Rnds 8–10:** Crochet 36 sc in each round. **[36 each round]**

Stuff the broad foot and keep adding a little filling as the leg grows.

9. **Rnd 11:** Repeat *(dec, 4 sc)* six times. **[30]**
10. **Rnd 12:** Work 30 sc. **[30]**
11. **Rnd 13:** Repeat *(dec, 3 sc)* six times. **[24]**
12. **Rnd 14:** Work 24 sc. **[24]**
13. **Rnd 15:** Repeat *(dec, 2 sc)* six times. **[18]**
14. **Rnd 16:** Repeat *(dec, 7 sc)* twice. **[16]**
15. **Rnds 17–24:** Crochet 16 sc in each of eight rounds. **[16 each round]**
16. **Rnd 25:** Repeat *(dec, 2 sc)* four times. **[12]**
17. **Rnd 26:** Work six decreases. **[6]**

Close the remaining stitches with a tapestry needle and hide the end. Make sure both legs have about the same firmness so Mallow sits evenly.

## Part 10 — Crochet a tiny tassel tail

The tail is made from its **base toward the fluffy end**. Use brown yarn and leave a long **starting tail** for sewing it to the body later.

1. **Rnd 1:** Crochet 6 sc in an MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnds 2–9:** Work 6 sc in each round. **[6 each round]**
3. **Rnd 10:** Repeat *(1 sc, chain 8)* six times. **[6 sc with 6 chain loops]**

Slip stitch once and fasten off, hiding the final end. Leave the six chain loops at the tip as a little woolly tassel.

## Part 11 — Bring Mallow to life

Lay out all the crocheted pieces first. Move them around gently until the face feels balanced and the paws look ready for a cuddle.

1. **Make the sleepy expression.** Position the two mirrored eyelids so each curves above and around its eye. Secure the ends neatly without covering the eye itself.
2. **Add the mop of curls.** Center the oval hairpiece across the crown, with its **long axis running from ear to ear** and the front edge near **head round 16**. Sew about halfway around its base, tuck a little stuffing beneath it, then finish stitching the base securely. Arrange the dangling loops.
3. **Join the head and body.** Check that the head is centered over the body's open neck. Sew around the opening with small, firm stitches; add a little more filling at the join if needed to keep the head supported.
4. **Curl the trunk.** Lift the tip so it curves upward and secure that curve with a few hidden stitches, taking care not to crush the shape.
5. **Position the ivory tusks.** Pin the brown bases diagonally along either side of the trunk, approximately **1 cm / ½ in below the eyes**. Check the look from the front and sides before sewing both bases firmly in place.
6. **Attach the ears.** Place them around **head round 18**, close to the side edges of the hairpiece. Angle their fuller edges outward and stitch securely.
7. **Fit the seated legs.** Using a strong strand of yarn and a long needle, sew through one leg about **1 cm / ½ in below its top**, pass through the body and through the corresponding part of the other leg. Work back in the opposite direction, repeating until both legs are attached firmly. Keep the tension secure without compressing the body. This produces legs that can still pivot a little.
8. **Attach the arms.** Repeat that through-the-body attachment method, placing the arms at the upper sides of the body, just below the neck.
9. **Finish with the tail.** Use the long starting yarn tail to attach the narrow base to the lower back. Hide every end and remove all temporary pins.

**One final check:** The tusks, eyes, curls, limbs and tail should all be securely attached. If the mammoth is for a young child, avoid detachable facial parts and make sure the fiberfill cannot escape through the fabric.

## A few little tricks from the making table

**The trunk looks uneven:** Check the 18-stitch trunk opening and the two edge-spanning decreases before continuing. They create the transition from the head to the smaller tube.

**The ivory toes have brown flecks:** Make the bobble's partial double crochets in ivory and use brown only for the last draw-through. Our Journal note on [tidy color changes](/blog/neater-amigurumi-color-changes/) helps explain why that timing matters.

**The woolly cap feels crowded:** Separate the chain loops with your fingers instead of pulling them tight. The three loop lengths are meant to make the fringe look full and a little unruly.

**Mallow has trouble sitting:** Before reattaching the legs, check whether their stuffing is equally distributed and whether the through-body yarn is pulling more strongly to one side.

**The stitches feel too open:** A slightly smaller hook or different yarn may help, but you should still be able to stitch comfortably. See [how to choose yarn for amigurumi](/blog/choosing-yarn-for-amigurumi/) for the details.

When the last yarn end is tucked away, give Mallow a place on the shelf. His little woolly head, sturdy round tummy and upward-curled trunk make him a cozy character to keep around, whatever the weather.
