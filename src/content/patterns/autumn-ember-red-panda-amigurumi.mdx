There is something irresistible about a red panda's round face and enormous striped tail. **Autumn Ember Red Panda** is a little crochet woodland friend with a bright orange head, tiny dark-brown paws and cream-colored details that make those inquisitive eyes stand out.

This one is wonderfully compact. Rather than building a complicated toy, you'll make a **round head**, a **small orange body**, four little legs, a flattened white muzzle, two soft ears and one very special tail. The tail does most of the decorating all by itself, thanks to a few carefully placed color changes and a clever curved section.

The pattern uses basic stitches, with a few **half double crochet stitches** to shape the tail. We'll go part by part, keeping the source stitch counts intact so you always know when a piece is growing or narrowing correctly.

## Your red panda at a glance

- **Finished height:** around **9 cm / 3.5 in** with the suggested yarn, hook and tension.
- **Skill level:** easy, with a little extra concentration needed for the changing tail colors.
- **Hook:** **3.75 mm (US F-5)**; the original uses worsted-weight yarn.
- **Colors:** orange for the head and body, white for ears and muzzle, brown for paws and the end of the tail, beige/tan for tail stripes, black for the nose.
- **Construction:** a separate round head, lightly shaped body, four unstuffed feet, two unstuffed ears and a striped, stuffed tail.
- **Language:** **US crochet terms**. Work in spiral rounds unless otherwise noted. Keep a stitch marker at the beginning of each round.

Need help with spirals? Our [magic ring lesson](/learn/magic-ring-crochet-in-the-round/) and [amigurumi shaping guide](/learn/crochet-increases-and-decreases/) are great to keep beside you.

## Stitch abbreviations

| Abbreviation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **MR** | Magic ring |
| **sc** | Single crochet |
| **inc** | Work 2 sc into one stitch |
| **dec** | Work 2 stitches together as 1 sc |
| **hdc** | Half double crochet |
| **rnd** | Round |
| **st / sts** | Stitch / stitches |
| **[number]** | Number of stitches after completing a round |

**Color-changing reminder:** When the instructions call for a new color, start that new shade during the final yarn-over of the previous stitch. That makes a much smoother boundary for the tail stripes. You can cut and weave the old strand or carry it loosely on the inside of the tail.

## Part 1 — Make the round orange head

Pick up your **orange yarn** and **3.75 mm hook**. We start at the top of the head and increase steadily until the shape is almost like a little orange ball.

1. **Rnd 1:** Make a magic ring and work 6 sc inside it. Pull the center closed. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Put an increase in every stitch. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 inc, then 1 sc* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *1 inc, then 2 sc* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *1 inc, then 3 sc* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *1 inc, then 4 sc* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** Repeat *1 inc, then 5 sc* six times. **[42]**
8. **Rnd 8:** Repeat *1 inc, then 6 sc* six times. **[48]**
9. **Rnd 9:** Repeat *1 inc, then 7 sc* six times. **[54]**
10. **Rnd 10:** Crochet once into each of the 54 stitches. **[54]**

The widest part is finished. Over the next rounds we'll decrease in six evenly spaced places so the head keeps its round outline rather than becoming a straight cylinder.

11. **Rnd 11:** Repeat *1 dec, 7 sc* six times. **[48]**
12. **Rnd 12:** Repeat *1 dec, 6 sc* six times. **[42]**
13. **Rnd 13:** Repeat *1 dec, 5 sc* six times. **[36]**
14. **Rnd 14:** Repeat *1 dec, 4 sc* six times. **[30]**

**Pause to place the eyes:** Position the two **6 mm safety eyes between Rounds 10 and 11**, with **11 stitches between them**. Make sure they look level and that you like the spacing before locking the backs. For a toy intended for a child under three, embroider the eyes instead. Add fiberfill to the head and continue to stuff gently as the opening gets smaller.

15. **Rnd 15:** Repeat *1 dec, 3 sc* six times. **[24]**
16. **Rnd 16:** Repeat *1 dec, 2 sc* six times. **[18]**
17. **Rnd 17:** Repeat *1 dec, 1 sc* six times. **[12]**
18. **Rnd 18:** Work six decreases in a row. **[6]**

Fasten off, leaving enough yarn to pull through the front loops of the final six stitches and close the bottom neatly. Bury the remaining end inside the head.

**Shape check:** The head is much bigger than the body on purpose. That big-eyed proportion is one of the cutest parts of the little red panda.

## Part 2 — Crochet the white muzzle and little nose

The white muzzle is a small, flattened disc, not a stuffed ball. Keep the **3.75 mm hook**, switching to **white yarn**.

1. **Rnd 1:** Make 6 sc in a magic ring. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Work one sc in each stitch. **[6]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Increase in all six stitches. **[12]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Repeat *1 inc, 1 sc* six times. **[18]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *1 inc, 2 sc* six times. **[24]**

Fasten off, keeping a long white tail to use as your sewing thread. With **black yarn**, embroider a compact nose across the little center of the muzzle (the first round). The source describes making this with longer horizontal stitches.

Don't add any stuffing. Center the muzzle **beneath and between the eyes**, pin it into place, and sew its edge onto the head. The white oval should sit flat without pulling in the orange stitches.

For an extra-soft expression, leave a little room between the muzzle and the eyes rather than pushing the muzzle up over them.

## Part 3 — Make the two soft white ears

The ears are white, subtly pointed in shape and flattened before attaching. Make **two** with your **white yarn**.

1. **Rnd 1:** Crochet 6 sc into a magic ring. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Work 6 sc around. **[6]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Increase in all six stitches. **[12]**
4. **Rnd 4:** Crochet 12 sc without shaping. **[12]**
5. **Rnd 5:** Repeat *1 inc, 1 sc* six times. **[18]**
6. **Rnd 6:** Repeat *1 inc, 2 sc* six times. **[24]**

Fasten off each ear with a generous tail. **Do not stuff the ears.** Press them flat, gently pinch the bottom ends and sew them to opposite sides of the head. Try both ears with pins first, so their tips sit at similar heights.

**Little-ear tip:** You can angle the ears slightly outward. It makes the red panda look more alert without changing the crochet stitches.

## Part 4 — Shape the tiny orange body

Switch back to **orange yarn**. This is a separate, short body that will tuck beneath the broad head, giving the finished red panda its low, four-footed stance.

1. **Rnd 1:** Work 6 sc in a magic ring. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in all six stitches. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Repeat *1 inc, 1 sc* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnds 4–5:** Work 18 sc in **each** of two rounds. **[18 each round]**
5. **Rnd 6:** Make **3 decreases**, then **6 sc**, then another **3 decreases**. **[12]**
6. **Rnd 7:** Work **2 decreases**, **4 sc**, and **2 more decreases**. **[8]**

Fasten off with a long sewing tail. Add a little fiberfill. Pin the orange body under the head with its rounded part toward the rear, then sew it securely in place. This toy isn't meant to stand upright like a doll; its tiny feet will support a four-legged pose.

## Part 5 — Crochet the two front legs

Use **dark-brown yarn** for the paws. The front legs are little tubes, just four rounds high, and the source specifically leaves them **unstuffed**.

Make **two** of the following:

1. **Rnd 1:** Make 6 sc in a magic ring. **[6]**
2. **Rnds 2–4:** Crochet 6 sc in each of the three rounds. **[6 each round]**

Fasten off with a long tail on each leg. Flatten them lightly. Attach the two front paws beneath the head, toward the **front of the orange body**. Use the same number of sewing passes on both, so they sit as a matched pair.

## Part 6 — Add the two short hind legs

The back feet are slightly shorter than the front pair. Use **dark-brown yarn** again and make **two**.

1. **Rnd 1:** Work 6 sc in a magic ring. **[6]**
2. **Rnds 2–3:** Crochet 6 sc in each round. **[6 each round]**

Leave a long sewing tail and **do not stuff**. Sew the hind legs underneath the orange body, toward its back end, with enough space between them to leave the tail attachment point clear.

**Four-foot check:** Turn the toy right-side up before knotting the sewing tails. The little body should feel supported by all four paws rather than leaning onto one corner.

## Part 7 — Build the long striped woodland tail

Now for the part that gives Autumn Ember her personality! This tail starts as a narrow orange tube, widens into a bold striped shape and then tapers toward a little dark-brown tip.

Use the **3.75 mm hook**. Keep the different yarn colors nearby so you can make the changes at exactly the rounds shown below.

**Important:** Before beginning the orange magic ring, leave a **long starting yarn tail**. That strand will later be used to **attach the narrow orange starting end of the tail to the body**; the finishing end at the narrow tip is a separate yarn tail.

### Start with the orange base and first cream stripe

1. **Rnd 1 (orange):** Make 6 sc in a magic ring. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2 (orange):** Crochet 6 sc around. **[6]**
3. **Rnd 3 (orange):** Increase in each of the six stitches. **[12]**

**Change to tan/beige yarn.**

4. **Rnd 4 (tan):** Work 12 sc without shaping. **[12]**
5. **Rnd 5 (tan):** Repeat *1 inc, 1 sc* six times. **[18]**

**Change back to orange yarn.**

6. **Rnd 6 (orange):** Crochet 18 sc around. **[18]**
7. **Rnd 7 (orange):** Increase in the **first 3 stitches**, crochet **12 sc**, then increase in the **last 3 stitches**. **[24]**

**Change to tan/beige yarn again.**

8. **Rnd 8 (tan):** Crochet 24 sc without shaping. **[24]**
9. **Rnd 9 (tan):** Increase in the **first 3 stitches**, work **18 sc**, then increase in the **last 3 stitches**. **[30]**

**Return to orange yarn.**

10. **Rnd 10 (orange):** Crochet 30 sc evenly. **[30]**

### Shape the curve with half double crochet stitches

The next two rounds are a little different. The half double crochet sections add height along the edges of the curved tail, while the decreases at the middle take in extra fabric.

11. **Rnd 11 (orange):** Work **6 hdc**, then **3 sc**, then **6 decreases**, then **3 sc**, and finish with **6 hdc**. **[24]**
12. **Rnd 12 (orange):** Work **3 hdc**, **18 sc**, then **3 hdc**. **[24]**

**Counting checkpoint:** Round 11 starts from 30 stitches and ends with 24. The six decreases consume twelve old stitches but make six new stitches. The following round stays at 24. Don't replace the hdc with sc if you want to preserve the source's gently curved shaping.

### Finish the brown, tapered end

Begin adding fiberfill now, stuffing as you work toward the narrow tip. **Change to dark brown yarn**.

13. **Rnds 13–14 (brown):** Work **24 sc in each** of these two rounds. **[24 each round]**
14. **Rnd 15 (brown):** Repeat *1 dec, 1 sc* eight times. **[16]**
15. **Rnd 16 (brown):** Crochet 16 sc evenly. **[16]**
16. **Rnd 17 (brown):** Work **8 decreases**. **[8]**
17. **Rnd 18 (brown):** Make **2 more decreases** in the next four stitches, leaving the other four stitches unworked. The original ends the round here. **[6]**

Fasten off with a tail long enough to close the opening. Use a yarn needle to sew together the remaining **six stitches**, then draw the end inside the tail and hide it. With the **starting orange tail** left at Round 1, sew the narrow orange starting end to the red panda's back.

**Why Round 18 looks unusual:** The source specifically calls for **only two final decreases**, rather than a complete round of four. That is intentional in this adaptation: it goes from 8 stitches to 6 before sewing the tip closed. Don't silently add extra decreases or another round.

## Part 8 — Add the final face markings and assemble

With the paws and tail ready, the last job is bringing all the little details together. Pin everything before sewing, especially the long striped tail, which can change the toy's balance.

1. **Finish the face:** Check that the white muzzle is centered under the eyes. Use short white embroidered stitches to add the characteristic **eyebrow marks and curved cheek patches** on either side of the face.
2. **Attach the ears:** Flatten the two white ears and sew them to the sides of the orange head, with their rounded tips angled gently outward.
3. **Join the body to the head:** Place the stuffed little orange body below the head and secure the seam with several firm passes.
4. **Attach the front feet:** Add the two longer brown tubes at the front and leave them unstuffed.
5. **Attach the hind feet:** Sew the two shorter brown paws under the back of the body.
6. **Position the tail:** Pin the narrow orange starting end to the rear of the body and arrange the striped tube behind the toy. Sew the base firmly, using the long strand left when you started Round 1.
7. **Check the profile:** Set the red panda on a flat surface to make sure all paws touch down and the tail doesn't twist the body to one side.
8. **Tuck away loose ends:** Weave ends to the inside, check the eye attachments, and give the tail one final gentle curve.

## A few helpful fixes

**My tail stripes look jagged.** Work the last yarn-over of the previous stitch with the new color. Avoid pulling the inside yarn strands too tightly; they should follow the tail's curve without puckering.

**The tail doesn't curve.** The important shaping occurs in **Rounds 11 and 12**, especially the half double crochets at both ends of the round. Verify the stitch order and count before undoing any work.

**The red panda looks top-heavy.** That's part of the design's charm, but the short legs still need to support the body. Pin all four paws first and make adjustments before sewing the joins tight.

**The muzzle looks too round.** The white muzzle is **not stuffed** in the original design. Press it flat and sew its edge gently onto the orange head.

**I can't find the place for the cheek patches.** Add them after the eyes and muzzle are attached. Keeping the embroidery small and balanced will help the expression look natural.
