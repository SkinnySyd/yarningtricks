There is something cheerful about a penguin with a very serious little beak and very unserious round feet. Meet **Harbor**, a cozy blue bird with a cream face and red-and-ivory stripes that feel like a day by the sea.

The project looks intricate, but the big secret is simple: almost everything uses **single crochet**. You'll work the **head and body in one piece**, change colors to shape the face and sweater, then sew on two navy flippers, yellow feet, beak and pink cheeks.

**About the reference:** The penguin section of *Animal Friends of Pica Pau 3* (pages 82–86) informed the general materials and techniques. This is a **separately drafted Thread & Petal pattern**, with different shaping and round counts for the supplied images, not a reproduction of that book's instructions. **The images show an intended look, not a physically tested sample produced from these rounds.** Counts have been checked arithmetically, but the finished pattern has not been test-crocheted.

## Before you begin

- **Skill level:** Intermediate, mostly because the cream facial field changes color in the middle of a round.
- **Terms:** US crochet vocabulary. **MR** means magic ring, **sc** single crochet, **inc** 2 sc in one stitch, **dec** a decrease worked over two stitches, **BLO** back loops only, and **FO** fasten off.
- **Counts:** The number in **[brackets]** gives the completed stitch total. A round range like 15–18 includes all four rounds.
- **Method:** Work continuous spiral rounds and move your stitch marker after each lap, unless otherwise indicated.
- **Photo note:** The bottom-view image is a useful **finishing reference**, not an exact photograph of a specific numbered round.

Before you start the face, review [changing yarn colors](/learn/changing-yarn-colors-crochet/). Each new shade begins on the **final yarn-over of the preceding stitch**. Carry floats loosely inside the head or use small separate bobbins; tight floats can make the face buckle.

## Part 1 — Crochet the navy crown

With **navy** yarn, crochet from the center of the head outward.

1. **Rnd 1:** Place 6 sc in a magic ring. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Increase in all six stitches. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** *(sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5:** *(3 sc, inc)* six times. **[30]**
6. **Rnd 6:** *(4 sc, inc)* six times. **[36]**
7. **Rnd 7:** *(5 sc, inc)* six times. **[42]**
8. **Rnd 8:** *(6 sc, inc)* six times. **[48]**
9. **Rnd 9:** *(7 sc, inc)* six times. **[54]**

**Count checkpoint:** You should have 54 stitches. The marker sits at the back, leaving the carefully arranged cream sections at the front.

## Part 2 — Make the creamy little face

The first few cream sections leave a deep-blue wedge between the two eyes. Later they meet into a broad cream lower face. Each sequence below adds up to **54 stitches**. Work each sequence in its listed order.

10. **Rnd 10:** Navy **16 sc**, cream **6 sc**, navy **10 sc**, cream **6 sc**, navy **16 sc**. **[54]**
11. **Rnd 11:** Navy **15 sc**, cream **8 sc**, navy **8 sc**, cream **8 sc**, navy **15 sc**. **[54]**
12. **Rnd 12:** Navy **14 sc**, cream **10 sc**, navy **6 sc**, cream **10 sc**, navy **14 sc**. **[54]**
13. **Rnd 13:** Navy **14 sc**, cream **12 sc**, navy **2 sc**, cream **12 sc**, navy **14 sc**. **[54]**
14. **Rnd 14:** Navy **14 sc**, cream **26 sc**, navy **14 sc**. **[54]**
15. **Rnd 15–18:** Repeat the Rnd 14 color distribution for each round. **[54 per round]**

**Expression checkpoint:** The blue point should sit near the top center of the face, and the cream portion should span the area where the cheeks and beak will go. If your color pattern drifts, mark the center of the face with a temporary yarn strand and adjust the next color transition gently rather than adding stitches.

For embroidered eyes, add two short vertical black stitches over the cream, roughly between rounds 13–16. If you're using installed eyes instead, choose positions before the head opening narrows.

## Part 3 — Neck and striped torso

Continue into the little sweater section without breaking the work apart.

16. **Rnd 19:** In navy, repeat *(7 sc, dec)* six times. **[48]**
17. **Rnd 20:** Repeat *(6 sc, dec)* six times. **[42]**

Give the head some stuffing now, a little at a time. Keep the cream face rounded rather than stretched.

18. **Rnd 21:** Change to cream. Repeat *(6 sc, inc)* six times. **[48]**
19. **Rnd 22:** In cherry red, repeat *(7 sc, inc)* six times. **[54]**
20. **Rnd 23:** In cream, repeat *(8 sc, inc)* six times. **[60]**
21. **Rnd 24–33:** Sc 60 stitches each round, alternating **two cream rounds and one red round**. Use cream for Rnds 24–25, red for Rnd 26, cream for Rnds 27–28, red for Rnd 29, cream for Rnds 30–31, red for Rnd 32, and cream for Rnd 33. **[60 per round]**

The white and red stripes will show a tiny offset at the back because we're working in a spiral. That's normal. Keep the yarn joins at the back of the bird, not over its cream face.

## Part 4 — Sky-blue belly and softly rounded base

Change to **pale blue** after Rnd 33. This section gently widens before closing, making a sweet round belly that sits between the little feet.

22. **Rnd 34:** *(9 sc, inc)* six times. **[66]**
23. **Rnd 35–44:** Sc 66 all around each round. **[66 per round]**
24. **Rnd 45:** *(9 sc, dec)* six times. **[60]**
25. **Rnd 46:** *(8 sc, dec)* six times. **[54]**
26. **Rnd 47:** *(7 sc, dec)* six times. **[48]**
27. **Rnd 48:** *(6 sc, dec)* six times. **[42]**
28. **Rnd 49:** *(5 sc, dec)* six times. **[36]**

![Open pale-blue bottom of a striped crochet penguin being finished beside a hook.](/images/tutorials/harbor-stripes-penguin-amigurumi/base-closure.avif)

*Check that the lower body sits round rather than flat and empty; the final decrease rounds below close the base.*

Add a little more fiberfill around the base now. Use smaller tufts, pushing them out toward the side walls rather than putting one hard ball in the center.

29. **Rnd 50:** *(4 sc, dec)* six times. **[30]**
30. **Rnd 51:** *(3 sc, dec)* six times. **[24]**
31. **Rnd 52:** *(2 sc, dec)* six times. **[18]**
32. **Rnd 53:** *(sc, dec)* six times. **[12]**
33. **Rnd 54:** Dec six times. **[6]**

Cut a long tail and weave through the remaining front loops, drawing the opening closed. Hide the yarn inside.

**Helpful reminder:** If your fabric grows holes as you stuff, the answer is usually a slightly smaller hook or gentler stuffing, not endlessly pulling the yarn tighter. See our [tension guide](/learn/controlling-crochet-tension/).

## Part 5 — Two long, drooping navy flippers

Make **two**, starting at the rounded tip in navy.

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2–3:** Sc in each stitch. **[6 per round]**
3. **Rnd 4:** *(2 sc, inc)* twice. **[8]**
4. **Rnd 5–6:** Sc around. **[8 per round]**
5. **Rnd 7:** *(3 sc, inc)* twice. **[10]**
6. **Rnd 8–10:** Sc 10 around. **[10 per round]**
7. **Rnd 11:** *(4 sc, inc)* twice. **[12]**
8. **Rnd 12–15:** Sc 12 all around. **[12 per round]**
9. **Rnd 16:** *(5 sc, inc)* twice. **[14]**
10. **Rnd 17–20:** Sc 14 all around. **[14 per round]**

Do **not** stuff the flippers. Flatten the open edges, fasten off with long tails, and pin them around the upper red-and-cream torso. Point the tips slightly inward or downward for a relaxed stance.

## Part 6 — Sunny yellow feet and the beak

### Two rounded feet

Make **two** in golden yellow.

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** Inc around. **[12]**
3. **Rnd 3:** *(sc, inc)* six times. **[18]**
4. **Rnd 4:** *(2 sc, inc)* six times. **[24]**
5. **Rnd 5–8:** Sc 24 around. **[24 per round]**
6. **Rnd 9:** *(2 sc, dec)* six times. **[18]**
7. **Rnd 10:** Sc 18 around. **[18]**
8. **Rnd 11:** *(sc, dec)* six times. **[12]**

Stuff the toes lightly and flatten the open end. Leave tails for sewing. Pin the feet low at the front, with the broad yellow toes pointing slightly outward.

### A little yellow beak

With yellow:

1. **Rnd 1:** 6 sc in MR. **[6]**
2. **Rnd 2:** *(sc, inc)* three times. **[9]**
3. **Rnd 3:** Sc 9 all around. **[9]**

Fasten off with a sewing tail and flatten gently. Attach the beak at the center of the cream face, roughly between rounds 14–17. You can sew it as a raised little oval without stuffing.

### Two blush cheeks

For each cheek, work **6 sc into a pink magic ring [6]** and slip stitch to finish. Leave a tail to sew a small pink circle on each side of the face.

## Part 7 — Sew everything together

1. Confirm the **face centerline** before sewing the beak and cheeks. The point in the navy crown helps you find it.
2. Attach the two **flippers** at matching heights. Pin both first so neither one hangs lower.
3. Stitch the **feet** to the lower front edge of the blue belly, just far enough apart for a stable seated pose.
4. Lift and set the penguin on a table. If it tips, adjust the feet or redistribute a little stuffing before tying off the last tails.
5. Bury all sewing ends inside the body. Recheck that each attachment is secure.

Our [amigurumi sewing guide](/learn/sew-amigurumi-pieces-together/) is useful here, especially if this is your first one-piece body with separate flippers.

## Tiny troubles, friendly fixes

| If… | Try… |
| --- | --- |
| The cream face is uneven | Count the navy-and-cream segments separately and keep the stitch marker to the back |
| The stripe joins look like steps | Keep the step at the back; don't introduce an unplanned stitch to hide it |
| The base feels lumpy | Remove a little stuffing and spread smaller pieces toward the sides |
| One flipper is longer | Count the even-round stretches and compare the two before sewing |
| The penguin leans | Pin the feet more forward and check the lower-body stuffing |

If you've ever wondered why changing the hook gives you a differently shaped toy, try [How yarn weight changes amigurumi](/blog/how-yarn-weight-changes-amigurumi/) and [Why amigurumi looks different from the photo](/blog/why-your-amigurumi-looks-different/).

## Make Harbor your own

Swap cherry red for coral, make the sweater navy-and-white, or stitch tiny sleepy eyes for a gentler face. The happiest penguin is the one whose stitches make you smile.
