Crochet shaping comes down to two ideas: **increases** add stitches, and **decreases** remove stitches. Once you understand the stitch counts, round-by-round amigurumi patterns become much less intimidating.

This lesson uses **US single crochet (sc)**. The same motions apply to many other stitch types, but their details differ.

## Single crochet increase: 2 sc in one stitch

An increase marked **inc** in an amigurumi pattern usually means **work two single crochets into the same stitch**.

1. Insert your hook into the next stitch and complete one sc.
2. Insert the hook **into that same stitch again** and make a second sc.
3. You used one stitch from the previous round and created **two new stitches**—your stitch count grows by one.

**Practice count:** start with 6 sc in a circle. Work **2 sc in each stitch**. Your next round contains **12 sc**.

## Increase evenly around a circle

A common sequence is:

- Round 1: **6 sc** in a magic ring. *(6)*
- Round 2: **inc in each stitch**. *(12)*
- Round 3: **(1 sc, inc) repeat six times**. *(18)*
- Round 4: **(2 sc, inc) repeat six times**. *(24)*

The numbers in parentheses are the total stitches at the end of the round. Mark the beginning of each round and count before moving on.

Even increases can make a flat circle; actual shape also depends on yarn, tension, stitch type, and where the increases are placed.

## Standard single crochet decrease: sc2tog

A standard **sc2tog** combines two stitches into one:

1. Insert hook into the next stitch, yarn over, pull up a loop. **Two loops** on hook.
2. Insert hook into the following stitch, yarn over, pull up a loop. **Three loops** on hook.
3. Yarn over and pull through **all three loops**. You have made one decrease, and your stitch count is reduced by one.

This is a beginner-friendly decrease, but it may leave a slightly visible bump on tight amigurumi fabric.

## Invisible single crochet decrease

An **invisible decrease** is popular with amigurumi worked with the right side facing you:

1. Insert the hook through the **front loop only** of the next stitch, without completing a stitch.
2. Insert it through the **front loop only** of the following stitch as well. Two front loops sit on the hook, plus your working loop.
3. Yarn over, pull through the **two front loops**.
4. Yarn over again and pull through the **two loops** remaining on your hook.

You have worked one stitch across two old stitches, usually with a flatter appearance than an ordinary sc2tog. Check the pattern to see which decrease it expects.

## Check the mathematics

If you begin a round with **18 stitches** and work **(1 sc, dec)** six times, each repeat consumes **3 old stitches** and makes **2 new stitches**. The next round will have **12 stitches**.

That's a handy way to spot a missed decrease before your shape changes unexpectedly.

## Common shaping mistakes

- **Too many increases:** you may be adding two stitches to a spot where the pattern calls for one.
- **A lopsided round:** the stitch marker might have moved, or a repeat was missed.
- **A hole at a decrease:** try the invisible method if the pattern allows it and keep tension even.
