**Half double crochet** sits between single crochet and double crochet in height. It makes a soft, flexible fabric without the taller gaps of double crochet.

This guide uses **US terms**: **hdc**. In UK terms, the same stitch is called **half treble (htr)**.

![Schematic showing half double crochet: three loops on the hook before one pull-through.](/images/learn/half-double-crochet-loops.svg)

*Loop-count reference only; follow the written instructions for hook placement.*

## The three-loop moment

The easiest way to remember hdc is that you pull the yarn through **all three loops at once**.

1. Yarn over before inserting your hook.
2. Insert the hook into the indicated stitch or chain.
3. Yarn over and pull up a loop. You now have **three loops** on your hook.
4. Yarn over once more.
5. Pull through **all three loops**. One loop remains. That is one hdc.

**Remember:** yo → insert → yo, draw up a loop (**3 loops**) → yo, through all 3.

## Your first two rows

For a practice swatch:

1. Make **chain 12**.
2. Work one hdc in the **third chain from the hook** and in each chain across. You will have **10 hdc**.
3. Chain 2, turn. In this practice exercise, the chain-2 turning chain **does not count** as a stitch.
4. Make one hdc in the first stitch and every stitch across: **10 hdc** again.
5. Repeat until you can work an even rectangle.

**Pattern note:** Different designers use different turning-chain heights and counting conventions. Always follow the instructions of the pattern you're making; the "ch 2 doesn't count" rule above applies to **this exercise**, not every hdc pattern.

## Find the third loop

As you gain confidence, notice that hdc creates an extra horizontal strand near the back of each stitch, sometimes called the **third loop**. Some textures are made by crocheting into it. For now, work under the normal **top two loops** unless the instructions say otherwise.

## Hdc vs. sc: what changes?

- A **single crochet** starts without a yarn over and is completed through **two loops**.
- A **half double crochet** begins with a yarn over and is completed through **three loops** at once.
- A **double crochet** also begins with a yarn over but is finished in **two pull-through stages**.

## If your edges look uneven

Count the 10 top Vs on every row. If your swatch gets wider, check that you haven't worked into the turning chain. If it gets narrower, look for the last stitch hiding at the edge.
