The **single crochet** is a compact, sturdy stitch used in everything from simple bookmarks to amigurumi animals. In US patterns it is abbreviated **sc**; in UK crochet terminology the same stitch is called **double crochet (dc)**.

If you can make a chain, you're ready. If not, first try our [slip knot and chain guide](/patterns/first-crochet-chain/).

## Recognize the top of a stitch

Look down at the upper edge of a crochet row. Each completed stitch makes a tiny **V** with two loops: the front loop and the back loop. Unless a pattern says otherwise, insert your hook under **both loops** of that V.

The loop already sitting on your hook does **not** count as a completed stitch.

## Practice single crochet into a chain

Make **chain 11**. You will work into the **second chain from your hook**. The loop on the hook is not counted as a chain.

1. Insert your hook into the second chain from the hook.
2. Bring your working yarn over the hook (**yarn over**, or *yo*).
3. Pull the yarn back through the chain. You now have **two loops** on your hook.
4. Yarn over again.
5. Draw the yarn through **both loops** on your hook. One loop remains: you have made **one single crochet**.
6. Work one sc into each remaining chain. At the end, you should have **10 single crochets**.

**Loop check:** insert → yarn over and draw up a loop (**2 loops**) → yarn over and pull through both (**1 loop**).

## Turn and make the second row

1. Chain 1 to give the row a little height.
2. Turn your fabric as you turn a page.
3. Insert the hook under both loops of the **first stitch** of the previous row, not into the turning chain.
4. Work one sc in each of the 10 stitches across.
5. Count **10 stitches** again. For this practice exercise, the chain-1 turn **does not count** as a stitch.

Try five rows. Your rectangle will become more even as you practice.

## How to count single crochet stitches

Count the V shapes along the top edge from one side to the other. Be sure you include the last stitch at the row edge—that small stitch is easy to miss.

If the number falls from 10 to 9, inspect the first and last stitches. If it rises to 11, you may have accidentally crocheted into the turning chain.

## Fix common single crochet problems

### The hook won't fit into the foundation chain

Your chain may be very tight. Try a larger hook for the foundation, or loosen the tension as you chain.

### My rectangle keeps getting narrower

Place a removable marker in the first and last stitches of each row until you can recognize them easily. Count before turning.

### My stitches are different heights

Keep your drawn-up loops approximately the same size. Hold the working yarn comfortably rather than tugging after every stitch.
