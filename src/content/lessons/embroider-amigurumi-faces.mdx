A mouth only a stitch wide can change an entire little character. Embroidery is a lovely way to add expression, and it also lets you take out a mark and try again before securing it.

This lesson is a **practice guide**, not a replacement for the facial placement in a specific pattern. We use US crochet terminology where stitch names appear.

## Gather your tiny face-making kit

Choose a tapestry or embroidery needle that suits your yarn, a smooth strand of embroidery floss or yarn in your chosen color, two removable markers, and scissors. A little stuffing is helpful for testing facial placement on a three-dimensional head.

Before choosing a thread, check whether it disappears into the stitches or makes a bulky ridge. Different yarn weights need different thicknesses of embroidery.

## Find the center before the smile

1. Stuff the head to approximately the shape specified in your pattern, leaving access to its interior when possible.
2. Locate the front and identify the relevant rounds.
3. Lay a **removable contrasting strand** vertically along the center front.
4. Mark matching eye positions by counting the stitches to each side, rather than measuring by eye alone.
5. Check the face from the front and both sides. Adjust before stitching.

If the pattern uses safety eyes, their attachment must normally happen **before the head is closed**, and the manufacturer's assembly directions take priority. For babies and young children, avoid detachable hard eyes and other small loose parts; securely embroidered features may be a more appropriate option, but the finished toy still requires suitable safety assessment.

## Sew a simple curved smile

A short sequence of small stitches is easier to control than one long strand that can snag.

1. Mark the left and right corners of the mouth with a removable yarn scrap.
2. Bring the needle from the inside or another concealed starting point to one corner.
3. Make a short embroidery stitch toward the center of the planned smile, catching the crochet fabric without dragging it inward.
4. Continue in one or two similarly sized stitches until you reach the other corner.
5. Gently adjust the curve, then secure the thread on the inside. Do not leave long, accessible floats on the toy.

A smile that looks uneven may need **one stitch moved**, not a tighter pull.

## Make a little stitched nose

For a tiny oval or triangle, place a few short horizontal stitches close together, then one small vertical stitch below if the design calls for it. Work gradually and keep the yarn tension soft enough that the face doesn't pucker. A narrower nose often looks more delicate than a thick bundle of thread.

## Try a French-knot dot

A French knot can make a small freckle or decorative dot. First practice on a spare swatch:

1. Bring the needle up to the front.
2. Wind the working thread around the needle **twice** while holding the wraps gently in place.
3. Reinsert the needle **close beside**, rather than into, the exact emergence point.
4. Ease the needle through while keeping the wraps controlled.
5. Secure the thread on the reverse.

If the knot slips through the crochet fabric, use thicker thread, position it over a denser stitch area, or choose an ordinary embroidered dot instead. A knot can be a poor choice where a child might pull at protruding details.

## Help both eyes look like they belong together

Work one eye, then compare the second against the centerline at every step. Count the same number of stitches across, use the same thread thickness, and check the height between crochet rounds. Mirroring the *placement* matters more than making two identical knots at any cost.

## Five common face troubles

| If you see… | Look at… |
| --- | --- |
| One eye looks higher | Different rounds or tension around a stitch |
| The smile disappears into the head | Thread color or stitches that are too short |
| The fabric dimples | Embroidery pulled too tightly |
| A knot pulls through | A loose base stitch or undersized thread |
| The face changes after stuffing | Placement tested before the head had its final shape |

## A five-minute face audition

Make a small crocheted circle or use a practice head. Mark the center. Stitch **one short smile, a tiny nose, and two balanced dots**. Undo one detail and replace it without damaging the stitches. That is the entire practice.
