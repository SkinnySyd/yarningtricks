The **US double crochet (dc)** is a taller stitch often used in granny squares, blankets, and lacy fabrics. Its UK name is **treble crochet (tr)**. This difference matters: a UK pattern that says "double crochet" usually means the shorter US single crochet.

## Work one double crochet

1. **Yarn over** before you insert the hook.
2. Insert your hook into the next stitch.
3. Yarn over and pull up a loop: **three loops** sit on your hook.
4. Yarn over and pull through the **first two loops**: **two loops** remain.
5. Yarn over and pull through the **last two loops**: **one loop** remains.

**Loop check:** 3 → 2 → 1. If you pull through all three loops at once, you've made a half double crochet instead.

## A ten-stitch double crochet swatch

1. Chain **13**.
2. Make one dc in the **fourth chain from your hook**.
3. Make one dc in each remaining chain. You now have **10 worked dc stitches**, plus the starting chain-3 that stands in for one stitch: **11 stitches counted in the first row**.
4. Chain 3 and turn. **For this exercise**, the turning chain counts as the first dc of the next row.
5. Skip the first stitch directly below the turning chain, then dc in each remaining stitch across, including the top of the previous row's turning chain. Your stitch count remains **11**.

The first row intentionally counts the beginning chain-3 as one stitch. If you prefer a ten-stitch swatch, chain 12 instead and follow the same counting convention: **9 worked dc plus the turning chain = 10**.

**Important:** Some patterns count a turning chain as a stitch and some do not. Double crochet is especially sensitive to this choice, so check the pattern notes before starting.

## Learn to recognize a granny cluster

A **granny cluster** is commonly three double crochets worked into the same space. Cluster groups and chain spaces form the familiar corners of a granny square.

If you know how to chain and double crochet, you're almost ready to try our [Classic Granny Square Coaster](/patterns/granny-square-coaster/).

## Troubleshooting a gap at the edge

A counted chain-3 at the start of a row can create a visible gap beside the next dc. That doesn't necessarily mean something is wrong. If a pattern uses an alternate turning-chain method, follow its instructions rather than mixing conventions.
