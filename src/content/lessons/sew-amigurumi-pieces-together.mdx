The crocheting is finished, the little ears and paws are waiting in a bowl, and suddenly the most important tool is your yarn needle. Sewing is where separate shapes become a character. You don't have to rush this part.

This lesson uses **US crochet terms**. Follow your pattern's own placement and stuffing directions first: a seam that looks lovely on one animal may be wrong for another.

## Make a small assembly basket

Have the crocheted pieces, a blunt tapestry needle, matching yarn or a long fastening-off tail, removable stitch markers, and enough stuffing for any pieces that require it. A contrasting scrap of yarn makes a temporary centerline.

**Before sewing:** identify the front of the body, its round marker or color-change line, and the right side of each piece. Count any placement rounds specified in your pattern. Lay the matching pieces side by side to check their size.

## First, pin on a personality

1. Gently stuff each piece **only as the pattern directs**. Some ears, wings and narrow limbs should remain unstuffed.
2. Mark the center front of the head or body using a removable strand.
3. Position the first ear, arm or leg. Pin it with a removable marker or hold it with a temporary stitch.
4. Count the stitches from the centerline to position the matching piece on the other side.
5. Check **front, side and top views** before taking a permanent stitch. Check the figure can sit or stand as intended.

**Little test:** photograph the arrangement on your phone. An unexpected tilt is often easier to spot in a picture than while holding the toy.

## Joining an open round piece

Use this for a tube-shaped arm, muzzle or neck **when the pattern says to attach the opening without flattening it**.

1. Leave an appropriate sewing tail, or thread a matching strand through your tapestry needle.
2. Hold the open end against the body where you marked the outline.
3. Take small stitches through a loop or edge strand of the open piece and a nearby stitch of the body.
4. Work your way around the opening, moving the needle evenly so the join follows the intended outline.
5. Pause every few stitches. If you see a ridge, loosen the previous stitches; if a gap appears, add a small securing stitch.
6. Finish by securing the tail inside the stuffed piece, following the pattern's finishing directions.

Do not pull so tightly that the entire opening contracts: the goal is a **secure join that preserves its shape**.

## Joining a flattened ear or limb

Some pieces are folded so their opening becomes a straight edge. You may have already worked a final row through both layers, or the pattern may simply tell you to flatten the opening.

1. Line up the front and back edges of the flattened piece.
2. Check whether its end should point forward, backward, up, or down.
3. Sew along the **flat attachment edge**, catching the crocheted edge and a stable part of the body beneath.
4. Test the angle after the first few stitches; lift and re-pin before finishing if necessary.
5. Secure the whole seam, including both corners.

A flattened ear usually needs a neat **line of attachment**, while an open muzzle usually needs a seam **around its perimeter**. Treating them as identical can distort both.

## Joining a head to a body

A head needs an especially secure, even connection. If your chosen pattern joins an open neck to the head, position and sew around that opening evenly. If the design uses another construction method, follow it instead.

**Check before closing the seam:** Is the face centered over the body's front? Is the neck opening adequately stuffed or supported? Does the head lean when you let go? You can correct these while the seam is accessible.

For a child's toy, securely attach every part and avoid detachable decorations. Handmade construction alone does not establish that an item meets applicable toy-safety standards.

## Why does my seam look strange?

| What you notice | Try this |
| --- | --- |
| A puckered ring | Undo a few stitches and use less sewing tension. |
| An ear tilts differently | Recheck its attachment round and distance from the centerline. |
| A limb feels loose | Add small, well-anchored stitches along the full attachment edge. |
| Stuffing shows at the seam | Recheck the edge alignment and stuffing amount before resewing. |
| The face seems off-center | Check the body's true front, not only the spiral's visible join. |

## A tiny practice before the real thing

Make two small single-crochet circles, or use scraps from an unfinished project. Practice attaching one as an open rounded piece and another as a flattened edge. Aim for a seam that stays put without shrinking the fabric beneath it.
