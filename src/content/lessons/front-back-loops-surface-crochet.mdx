Some crochet stitches are little crossroads. Working under both loops makes one kind of fabric; choosing only the front or back loop can leave a clean ridge or a place to add another detail later.

You'll meet **FLO** and **BLO** in flower petals, shoe soles, ears, and animal textures. This lesson uses US single crochet (sc) and US slip stitch (sl st).

## Find the two loops

Look at the top of a completed crochet stitch. You'll usually see a V.

- **Front loop:** the strand of the V nearest to you as you hold the work.
- **Back loop:** the strand of the V farther away.
- **Both loops:** the hook goes beneath the two strands together, as it normally would.

Turn the fabric and the viewpoint changes, so follow the pattern's right-side orientation when identifying loops. Always check whether its special instruction applies for **one round**, **one row**, or just a few stitches.

## Make a small ridge with BLO

Try a simple practice swatch:

1. Chain 11, then single crochet into the second chain from your hook and across. You should have **10 sc**.
2. Chain 1, turn. Unless otherwise specified, that turning chain does **not** count as an sc.
3. Work **10 sc in the back loops only** of the stitches across.
4. Turn the swatch and examine the unworked front loops. They form a little ridge on one side.
5. Make another row of ordinary sc under both loops and compare the texture.

**What changes?** The new row sits slightly differently because one strand of each original V was left unused. Repeating BLO across rows can also make a more flexible, ribbed-looking fabric.

## FLO is a different choice

Make another row using **front loops only**. The ridge lies on the opposite face from a BLO row, relative to the direction of your work.

A pattern may use FLO or BLO to create a sharp turn from the base of a foot into its sides, or to preserve unworked loops for a decorative edging. The result depends on the piece's orientation.

## Surface slip stitch: draw with yarn

Surface crochet works **on top of fabric you've already made**. You aren't adding a normal new row at the edge; you're embellishing the existing surface.

1. Take a crocheted swatch, a second yarn color, and your hook.
2. Place the working yarn behind the fabric and bring a loop up at the point where your decorative line should begin.
3. Insert the hook into the fabric at the next point along the planned line.
4. Catch the yarn from behind and pull it through the fabric **and the loop already on your hook**.
5. Repeat, keeping each visible stitch loose enough that the fabric remains flat.
6. Secure both ends on the reverse.

Draw a small curved line or an initial on a practice square. For a smoother result, try placing the hook under consistent stitch bars rather than improvising widely spaced punctures.

## Surface single crochet: a slightly raised line

With a working loop on the hook and yarn behind the fabric:

1. Insert the hook under a suitable strand or bar of the finished fabric.
2. Yarn over from behind and draw up a loop. **Two loops** are now on your hook.
3. Yarn over again and pull through both loops to complete one surface single crochet.
4. Move to the next anchor point and repeat, without tightening the fabric into a crease.

Unlike surface slip stitch, this creates a fuller little line. Surface stitches can help with an animal's textured patch or a decorative motif, but work a test first: a densely stuffed toy may be more difficult to crochet through.

## Which technique when?

| Instruction | Where you work | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary sc | Under both top loops | Builds normal fabric |
| FLO sc | Under the front top loop | Leaves the back loop unused |
| BLO sc | Under the back top loop | Leaves the front loop unused |
| Surface sl st | Through the finished fabric | Makes a relatively flat decorative line |
| Surface sc | Through a suitable surface anchor | Makes a fuller raised line |

## Three friendly troubleshooting checks

**The ridge appears on the wrong side:** inspect which way you're holding the piece and whether the pattern expects the right side to face out.

**The fabric puckers:** loosen your surface crochet stitches, especially on a curve.

**You can't see the stitches:** practice with contrasting smooth yarn before trying fluffy or chenille yarn.
