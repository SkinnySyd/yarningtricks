You need surprisingly little to begin crocheting: **one hook and one ball of yarn** are enough to practice. A few inexpensive extras make finishing easier, but don't feel you need a big kit before your first stitch.

## Start with a simple beginner kit

- **One smooth, light-colored medium-weight yarn.** Look for a label describing it as *medium* or *weight 4* (often called *worsted* in North America or sometimes *aran* in the UK). Labels vary by brand, so the printed hook recommendation matters more than the name alone.
- **A 5 mm crochet hook (US H/8).** This is a comfortable starting point for many medium-weight yarns. If your yarn label suggests a noticeably different size, use its recommendation instead.
- **Small scissors** for trimming yarn tails.
- **A blunt tapestry or yarn needle** with an eye wide enough for your yarn, for weaving in ends later.
- **A stitch marker** (optional). A removable marker or small contrasting thread makes it easier to find the first stitch when you begin crocheting in the round.

Avoid fuzzy, very dark, or heavily textured yarn for your first practice piece. You want to see the little V-shaped stitches clearly.

## Read the yarn label

A yarn label usually tells you the **fiber**, **weight**, **recommended hook size**, **care instructions**, and **amount of yarn**. The number on a weight label describes the thickness category, not the number of stitches to make.

For early practice, comfortable and visible stitches matter more than exact gauge. When you eventually make a fitted garment, matching the pattern's gauge becomes much more important.

**Global maker note:** Hook sizes are written in **millimeters** throughout Thread & Petal, with US letters/numbers where useful. Hook-letter conventions vary between brands, so the millimeter measurement is the most reliable comparison.

## Hold the hook in a way that feels natural

Two common grips are:

1. **Pencil grip:** hold the hook a little like a pencil.
2. **Knife grip:** hold the hook a little like a dinner knife.

Neither is the universal "correct" way. Choose the grip that lets your wrist stay relaxed, and try to avoid squeezing the hook too tightly. Your other hand feeds yarn and gently controls its tension.

If your hands feel uncomfortable, pause and adjust your position. Crochet should not require gripping through pain.

## Set up your first practice space

Sit where you have good light. Pull a little yarn from the ball, keep the ball somewhere it can unwind smoothly, and place the hook in your dominant hand. Leave the yarn loose enough to move over the hook.

For your first attempt, don't worry about making something beautiful. The goal is to learn how the yarn travels around the hook.

## Your first five-minute challenge

1. Find the hook-size recommendation on your yarn label.
2. Hold the hook using either comfortable grip.
3. Pull out an arm's length of yarn and notice how it slides between your fingers.
4. Open our [first crochet chain tutorial](/patterns/first-crochet-chain/), make a slip knot, and try ten chain stitches.
5. Undo the chain and try again. Being able to unravel and restart is a useful crochet skill.

## Common beginner questions

### Do I need an expensive hook?

No. An inexpensive, smooth hook in the correct size is fine. Ergonomic grips are a preference, not a requirement.

### Is cotton or acrylic better for learning?

Both can work. The best beginner yarn is smooth, comfortable to hold, and easy to see. Some cotton yarns show stitch definition particularly clearly; some acrylics are softer and easier to find.

### What if my chain is too tight?

Relax your yarn-holding hand, avoid pulling the working yarn after each stitch, and consider a slightly larger hook just for practice. The loop should slide along the hook shaft without force.
