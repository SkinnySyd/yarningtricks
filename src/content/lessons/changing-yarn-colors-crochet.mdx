A little color can turn a plain shape into a flower, a striped scarf, or a character with a wonderfully expressive face. The secret is to **begin the next color before the previous stitch is completely finished**.

These instructions use US crochet terminology and focus on **single crochet (sc)**. Follow any special color-change method given by your chosen pattern.

## The last-draw-through method

You need two contrasting colors of similarly weighted yarn. Call them **A** and **B**.

1. Work with A until you reach the stitch *before* the new color should appear.
2. Insert the hook into that stitch, yarn over with A, and pull up a loop. You have **two loops** on the hook.
3. Drop A without cutting it yet. Yarn over with B and pull B through **both loops** to finish the stitch.
4. Your completed stitch is mostly A; the new working loop on your hook is B. Work the **next** stitch with B.
5. Gently adjust the tails so the join is neat, but do not pull hard enough to shrink the fabric.

**Why not change after the stitch?** If you complete the whole stitch with A and only then pull up B, the old-colored top loops may show at the boundary.

## Practice with a striped rectangle

1. With A, chain 11 and work 10 sc into the chain, beginning in the second chain from the hook. **[10]**
2. Chain 1, turn, then work 10 sc across. Repeat until you have four rows of A.
3. In the last stitch of the fourth row, use B for the final draw-through as described above.
4. Chain 1 and turn with B; crochet 10 sc across. Work three more rows with B.
5. Count 10 sc after each row. Keep the unused tail out of the way until you can secure it neatly.

The turning chain does not count as a stitch in this exercise. With the rectangle in front of you, inspect both faces: a color boundary is rarely perfectly invisible, but it should look intentional.

## Why do spiral stripes have a step?

Amigurumi is often worked in **continuous spirals**, not separately joined rings. The beginning of one round sits a little higher than its end, so a strong horizontal color band can show a visible *jog* even when you change yarn cleanly.

The last-draw-through method reduces stray color loops; it **does not by itself make spiral stripes perfectly level**. Some designs use joined rounds, shifted color changes, or a special stripe technique. Follow the instructions for the particular project rather than adding an extra stitch to force the colors to line up.

For the difference between methods, read [our Journal note on neater amigurumi color changes](/blog/neater-amigurumi-color-changes/).

## Carry the yarn or cut it?

- **Short gap, small color section:** You may be able to carry unused yarn along the inside, if the pattern and fabric allow it. Avoid long floating strands that can snag, distort the work, or be visible through pale stitches.
- **Long gap or one-off detail:** Cutting the yarn and leaving a sufficient tail to secure is often neater.
- **Stuffed toys:** Keep loose ends contained and securely woven or stitched in. Don't rely on a knot alone for a child's toy.

If switching between different brands or textures, first check that the resulting stitches are approximately the same size. Even matching label categories can vary.

## Troubleshooting

**Little flecks of color A remain:** check which color was used for the previous stitch's final draw-through.

**A hole appears at the join:** hold both tails steady for the first few stitches of B and secure them afterward; don't tighten until the fabric puckers.

**Your new color looks too tight:** inspect your yarn hold and use the tips in [controlling crochet tension](/learn/controlling-crochet-tension/).

**The stripe seems to climb:** that can be the natural result of a continuous spiral, not a stitch-count mistake.
