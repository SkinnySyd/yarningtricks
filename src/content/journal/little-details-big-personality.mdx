Some of the sweetest crochet decisions happen *after* the last round.

An ear tilts a little to the left. A smile sits a touch higher. A soft curve of stuffing helps a nose stand proud. These small choices turn a pile of crocheted pieces into a character with a story.

Here are the finishing habits worth keeping in your making basket.

## Give every piece a moment to settle

Before sewing, lay out the head, body, arms, ears, and other little parts. Check your pattern's orientation notes, especially if there's a particular front, back, or color-change line.

If a piece is meant to be stuffed, do that first as directed. Work in small amounts, spreading the filling with your fingers. Overstuffing can show through the stitches; understuffing can leave a face or body looking flat.

Place matching pieces side by side. Are the ears equally full? Do the feet feel the same size? Tiny differences can be charming, but it's easier to choose them deliberately.

## Try a little character audition

Position the pieces before you secure them. Use removable stitch markers or temporary sewing stitches and check the silhouette from a few angles.

Try moving the ears one stitch closer together. Raise a pair of arms. Tilt a tail slightly. You might be surprised how much personality these shifts add without changing the crochet pattern itself.

**Look from three directions:** straight on, from the side, and from above. It's easy to make a face look centered from one angle while a nose or ear sits too far forward from another.

## Plan the face before you stitch it

Decide where the eyes will sit *before* closing a head, especially if your pattern needs access to the inside. Count stitches across the face and use a contrasting strand of yarn as a temporary centerline if that helps.

An embroidered face is often the gentlest finishing option, especially when the toy may be given to a young child. Choose firmly anchored stitches, keep the embroidery neat, and make sure loose ends are secured inside the piece.

Plastic eyes and buttons can become choking hazards if detached. Avoid them on toys for babies and young children, and always check the finished object for loose or breakable parts.

A face does not need many features. Two small eyes and a carefully placed mouth can say quite a lot.

## Make the joins feel intentional

Use the yarn tail left by the pattern when possible, and choose a needle that passes comfortably through the stitches. Hold the pieces firmly enough to sew, but not so tightly that you flatten their shape.

A few practical habits help:

- Catch the edge stitches of both pieces consistently so the seam doesn't wander.
- Pause after several stitches to check the position again.
- Tighten the seam gradually rather than tugging all at once.
- Secure and hide yarn tails according to your project's finishing instructions.

If a limb needs to move or an ear is meant to fold, don't stitch down more than the design requires. A join should support the character's shape.

## Fix the little things without starting over

A slightly uneven smile? Remove the embroidery and try again before hiding the thread. An ear that looks too low? Reposition it while the seam is still accessible.

If a stuffed piece looks lumpy, gently redistribute the filling through the fabric with your fingers. If the problem remains, reopening a small section and restuffing can be easier than living with a shape you don't like.

None of this means the project went wrong. Finishing is part of making.

## A tiny end-of-project ritual

Before you call a character finished, take one slow look for exposed ends, loose attachments, visible stuffing, and forgotten pins. If the toy is for a child, check every part especially carefully and consider the recipient's age.

Then take a picture. The little details are worth remembering.
